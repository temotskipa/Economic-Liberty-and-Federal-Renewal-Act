# Economic Liberty and Federal Renewal Act of 2026

## A BILL

To reduce Federal barriers to entry and regulatory privileges that disproportionately benefit incumbent firms, to move toward a balanced Federal budget through structural spending reform and the transition of Social Security and Medicare to private arrangements, to replace existing Federal taxes with a transparent tiered value-added tax, to wind down the Federal Reserve System while preserving the continuity of currency and payment systems, to establish a free banking regime based on sound-money principles, and for other purposes.

**Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,**

### SEC. 2. FINDINGS AND PURPOSES

**(a) Findings.**—Congress finds the following:

(1) The accumulation of Federal regulations has created barriers to entry and compliance costs that disproportionately advantage large corporations over small businesses and innovators, stifling competition and economic growth.

(2) Intellectual property laws, in practice, function as government-granted monopolies that enrich incumbents at the expense of consumers and new entrants, rather than as genuine property rights.

(3) The Congressional Budget Office projected in February 2026 that Federal outlays for fiscal year 2026 would total approximately $7,400,000,000,000, Federal revenues would total approximately $5,600,000,000,000, and the resulting deficit would total approximately $1,900,000,000,000.

(4) Social Security, Medicare, and other transfer programs create substantial long-term obligations and can crowd out private saving, insurance, mutual aid, and charity; any transition from those programs must state clearly which obligations the United States will honor and how those obligations will be financed.

(5) The current tax system is complex, distortionary, and punitive, discouraging work, investment, and savings.

(6) The Federal Reserve System has enabled unchecked monetary expansion, inflation, and financial instability, undermining the dollar's value and economic liberty.

(7) Central-bank credit expansion can suppress or obscure the market interest-rate signals produced by voluntary saving, distort intertemporal production decisions, encourage malinvestment and excessive leverage, socialize losses through emergency support, and concentrate monetary decisions in officials who cannot possess the dispersed knowledge held by market participants.

(8) Competition among payment media, including fully disclosed commodity-backed and other asset-backed instruments, can increase monetary choice and innovation, but the transition from a central-bank system must preserve the enforceability of existing obligations and the continuity of currency, clearing, settlement, and Treasury operations. A free-banking regime requires an affirmative and nonexclusive right of entry, note and account issuance, contractual reserve selection, and voluntary clearing access; merely abolishing a Federal charter while leaving entry contingent on discretionary State permission would not establish free banking. Such a regime may include both fully reserved bailment claims and contractually disclosed fractional-reserve debt claims; this Act does not permit a debt claim to be falsely described as a bailment or mandate a single reserve ratio.

(9) Transition safeguards in this Act address the execution of institutional change and do not constitute a finding that the institutions, programs, or regulatory powers being terminated are constitutionally authorized, economically sound, or free from systemic risk.

(10) The reforms in this Act are intended to unleash entrepreneurial energy, reduce Federal overreach, and move toward a balanced budget, consistent with the principles of federalism embodied in the Constitution.

(11) It is the policy of Congress, to the maximum extent permitted by binding judicial precedent, to construe Federal power in accordance with the original public meaning of the enumerated powers in article I, section 8 of the Constitution and not as a general Federal police power.

(12) Federal spending conditions should be stated unambiguously, should relate to the Federal interest in the program for which funds are provided, and should not coerce States or require a State to administer a Federal regulatory program.

(13) Matters not entrusted to the Federal Government by the Constitution should remain with the States and the people, and no provision of this Act should be construed to require a State to accept a transferred Federal function.

(14) A destination-based value-added tax may be structured as a geographically uniform excise throughout the States, territories, and possessions under article I, section 8, clause 1 and article IV, section 3, clause 2 of the Constitution, but rate preferences and exclusions must be defined by statute and administered under reviewable standards.

(15) Repealing or abolishing a program does not by itself resolve outstanding contracts, benefit claims, enforcement proceedings, records, Federal employee obligations, treaty commitments, or payment-system dependencies; express transition and savings rules are therefore necessary.

(16) Because Congress exercises authority over territories and possessions under article IV, section 3, clause 2 of the Constitution, a Federal tax, regulatory regime, customs barrier, or other Federal rule terminated by this Act should not remain in force merely because conduct or a person is located in a territory or possession. Uniform Federal application is consistent with preserving the separate authority of a territorial legislature over law that it independently enacts.

(17) Puerto Rico has had an Article III district court since 1966, while Guam, the United States Virgin Islands, and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands have Federal territorial courts whose judges serve fixed terms, and American Samoa has no Federal district court. Equal access to the Federal judicial power, bankruptcy relief, and locally available adjudication of Federal rights should not depend on residence in a permanently inhabited territory. Article III district courts can provide that access without absorbing the separate local judiciaries or displacing territorial law and customs.

**(b) Purposes.**—The purposes of this Act include—

(1) dismantling regulatory capture and cronyism;\
(2) achieving measurable progress toward a balanced Federal budget without concealing transition liabilities;\
(3) replacing the existing Federal tax system with a consumption-based system that has clear rates, a defined base, and auditable administration;\
(4) restoring monetary choice and banking freedom while maintaining an orderly transition for existing obligations;\
(5) preserving accrued claims, due process, judicial review, and the continuity of functions expressly retained by this Act;\
(6) enacting complementary measures to maximize economic liberty; and\
(7) providing Article III judicial access in every permanently inhabited territory while preserving separate territorial courts and local law.

## TITLE I—REPEALS AND ECONOMIC DEREGULATION

### SEC. 101. ABOLITION OF FEDERAL AGENCIES AND DEPARTMENTS

**(a) Specified agencies and components.**—Effective on the date that is 1 year after the date of enactment of this Act, each of the following agencies or components is abolished, and each function of the agency or component that is not expressly preserved or transferred under subsection (e) terminates:

(1) The Environmental Protection Agency.\
(2) The Food and Drug Administration.\
(3) The Federal Communications Commission.\
(4) The Securities and Exchange Commission.\
(5) The Federal Trade Commission.\
(6) The Occupational Safety and Health Administration.\
(7) The Consumer Product Safety Commission.\
(8) The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.\
(9) The Mine Safety and Health Administration.\
(10) The National Labor Relations Board.\
(11) The Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection (commonly known as the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau).\
(12) The Commodity Futures Trading Commission.

**(b) Executive departments and cultural entities.**—Effective on the date that is 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, each of the following departments and entities, and each subordinate component thereof, is abolished, subject to subsection (e):

(1) The Department of Education, except that the abolition shall not take effect until section 203(a)(7) has taken effect and preserved records and obligations have been assigned under section 202.\
(2) The Department of Housing and Urban Development, except that the abolition shall not take effect until section 203(a)(12) has taken effect and preserved records and obligations have been assigned under section 202.\
(3) The Department of Energy, except that the nuclear-security, nuclear-weapons, naval-reactor, and nuclear-material stewardship functions carried out under the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (42 U.S.C. 2011 et seq.) and the National Nuclear Security Administration Act (50 U.S.C. 2401 et seq.) shall be transferred under subsection (e).\
(4) The Department of Transportation.\
(5) The Department of Agriculture, except that the abolition shall not take effect until sections 203(a)(2), 203(a)(9), and 203(a)(10) have taken effect and preserved records and obligations have been assigned under section 202, and ordinary custody, wildfire protection, records, and property-management functions for National Forest System land and other Federal land administered by that Department shall be transferred to the Department of the Interior under subsection (e) pending disposition or further legislation.\
(6) The Department of Health and Human Services, except that the abolition shall not take effect until sections 203(a)(1), 203(a)(3), 203(a)(8), and 203(a)(11) have taken effect, the records and preserved obligations of those repealed programs have been assigned under section 202, and the transfer required by section 205(e)(3) is operational.\
(7) The National Endowment for the Arts.\
(8) The National Endowment for the Humanities.

**(c) Review of additional agencies.**—Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment of this Act, the President shall submit to Congress a list of each executive agency, as defined in section 105 of title 5, United States Code, that received less than $10,000,000,000 in discretionary budget authority for the preceding fiscal year, together with proposed legislation to abolish or consolidate each agency that the President determines is not necessary to carry out a function vested in the Federal Government by the Constitution. No agency is abolished solely by reason of its inclusion in, or omission from, the list.

**(d) Social Security Administration.**—Not later than 3 years after the date of enactment, the Secretary of the Treasury shall complete an independently tested transfer under section 201 and report to Congress whether it is operational and will avoid interruption of payment or adjudication. The Social Security Administration is abolished on the earlier of the Secretary's affirmative certification or the 3-year deadline. At abolition, all remaining personnel, records, systems, appropriations, and contracts necessary to administer title II of the Social Security Act before the tax conversion date and protected legacy benefits thereafter transfer to the Department of the Treasury. If a material deficiency remains at the deadline, the Office of Legacy Social Insurance is the temporary custodian under subsection (e)(4), shall use the transferred systems and personnel solely to maintain service and correct the deficiency, and may not delay abolition or terminate a payment lawfully due.

**(e) Continuity and transfer of retained functions.**—

(1) **Functions that shall continue.**—Each function described in this paragraph is preserved by this Act to the extent stated, and the President shall transfer the function to an appropriate department or agency that is not abolished by this Act. A function is described in this paragraph only to the extent necessary to—

(A) carry out intelligence, national defense, foreign affairs, border, customs, sanctions, or counterterrorism authorities not repealed by this Act;

(B) secure nuclear weapons, special nuclear material, radioactive waste, dams, air-navigation systems, or other Federal property the uncontrolled disposition of which would present an imminent threat to life or national security;

(C) administer a valid Federal contract, debt, benefit, judgment, trust, or other obligation expressly preserved by this Act;

(D) maintain, transfer, or provide lawful access to Federal records;

(E) carry out a function that another provision of this Act expressly preserves; or

(F) maintain ordinary custody of Federal land or natural resources pending lawful disposition, including actions narrowly necessary to prevent wildfire, waste, trespass, or physical destruction, without creating a new regulatory program or expanding public ownership.

(2) **Transition plan.**—Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment of this Act, the President shall transmit to Congress and publish in the Federal Register a transition plan that identifies, for each function transferred under paragraph (1), the statutory authority for the function, the receiving entity, the personnel and property transferred, the funding source, and the date on which the transfer will be operational. The plan may not continue a function for which the underlying statutory authority is repealed by this Act.

(3) **No commandeering of States.**—No function, liability, personnel, or property may be transferred to a State or an officer of a State without the affirmative agreement of that State under State law. A State's refusal to accept a transfer does not authorize the President to continue a function that this Act terminates.

(4) **Failure to complete assignment.**—The failure of an officer to complete a transfer by the applicable abolition date does not terminate a function preserved by paragraph (1), revive a terminated function, or authorize the abolished entity to continue. The President shall designate a lawful temporary custodian from among entities not abolished by this Act until the transfer is completed, and the custodian may exercise only authority necessary to prevent an interruption of the preserved function.

**(f) Disposition of property and funds.**—The Administrator of General Services, in consultation with the Director of the Office of Management and Budget and the Secretary of the Treasury, shall dispose of surplus real and personal property of an entity abolished under this section through a transparent competitive process. The Administrator may not sell classified material, records subject to a retention requirement, property needed for a transferred function, or hazardous or nuclear material except under law specifically governing that material. A sale is subject to valid liens, contracts, and property interests. Net proceeds shall be deposited in the general fund of the Treasury, and unobligated balances shall be rescinded except to the extent needed to satisfy a preserved obligation or complete an authorized wind-down.

**(g) References.**—After a transfer under subsection (e), a reference in Federal law to an abolished entity with respect to the transferred function shall be deemed to refer to the receiving entity. No such reference shall revive a function or authority terminated by this Act.

**(h) Effect on unrepealed organic provisions.**—On the effective date of an abolition, a provision enacted before this Act that assigns a function to the abolished entity or its head has no effect to the extent the function is not transferred under subsection (e). The absence of a technical conforming amendment does not continue the entity or function, and no officer may assume the function by implication.

**(i) No orphaned prospective regulatory duties.**—On and after the effective date of an abolition, a prospective Federal permit, license, registration, approval, reporting, inspection, testing, labeling, design, rate, entry, labor-practice, or similar compliance requirement may not be enforced with respect to later conduct if the abolished entity or its head possessed rulemaking, permitting, approval, supervisory, inspection, or primary civil-enforcement authority for that requirement, unless this Act expressly preserves the requirement and transfers its administration. Shared enforcement by another officer, a State, or a private person does not preserve the prospective requirement. This subsection does not extinguish a pre-effective-date liability, impair an independently valid contract or private property right, displace State law, or limit a generally applicable criminal law preserved by section 102(b)(1).

**(j) Long-stop for conditional abolitions.**—For an entity listed in subsection (b), an assignment of records, obligations, personnel, property, or a preserved function, or an operational certification required after the last substantive repeal or transition condition applicable to that entity has occurred, may not delay abolition for more than 180 days after that condition. At the end of that period the entity is abolished by operation of law, subsection (e)(4) applies to an incomplete assignment, and the receiving or temporary custodial entity succeeds to the minimum authority necessary to complete it. A tax-conversion-dependent program condition is not deemed to have occurred before the tax conversion date.

### SEC. 103. ABOLITION OF INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY MONOPOLIES

**(a) Repeal of patent, trademark, and plant-variety laws.**—The following are repealed in their entirety:

(1) Title 35, United States Code.\
(2) The Act of July 5, 1946 (chapter 540; 60 Stat. 427), commonly known as the "Trademark Act of 1946" or the "Lanham Act" (15 U.S.C. 1051 et seq.).\
(3) The Plant Variety Protection Act (7 U.S.C. 2321 et seq.).

**(b) Termination of other Federal exclusive rights.**—Each provision of Federal law enacted before this Act that grants a private person an exclusive right in a name, word, phrase, emblem, symbol, designation, design, work, data set, invention, plant variety, biological reference product, or comparable intangible subject matter, including sections 220106 and 220506 of title 36, United States Code, has no effect with respect to conduct occurring on or after the date of enactment. This subsection does not repeal a generally applicable prohibition on fraud, passing off, impersonation of a public officer, misuse of an official seal of the United States, theft, or unauthorized disclosure of classified information.

**(c) Repeal of copyright laws.**—Title 17, United States Code, is repealed in its entirety.

**(d) Immediate termination of Federal intellectual property protections.**—

(1) **Prospective conduct.**—On and after the date of enactment of this Act, no patent, trademark, copyright, plant-variety protection certificate, or other exclusive right repealed or terminated by this section shall be enforceable under Federal law with respect to conduct occurring on or after that date, and no new such right shall be issued or registered. A claim based solely on conduct occurring before that date remains subject to the law in effect when the conduct occurred and to sections 603 and 604.

(2) **Contracts and other law.**—Nothing in this section impairs the obligation of a valid contract between the parties to that contract or preempts a generally applicable State law concerning trade secrets, passing off, fraud, false designation of source, or unfair competition that does not grant an exclusive right equivalent to a right repealed or terminated by this section against a person who is not a party to the contract. The Trademark Act of 1946 creates no cause of action for conduct occurring on or after the date of enactment. With respect to conduct occurring after that date, no person is entitled under State law to an exclusive right in a functional invention, plant variety, expressive work, mark, trade dress, or designation that is equivalent to a right repealed or terminated by this section, and a claim asserting such a right is preempted; this sentence does not preempt a remedy for passing off, fraud, or likely deception as to source.

(3) **Compensation.**—Any compensation constitutionally required as a result of this section shall be available only in accordance with section 604.

**(e) International obligations.**—Not later than 30 days after the date of enactment of this Act, the United States Trade Representative and the Secretary of State shall notify Congress of each treaty or international agreement affected by this section and shall promptly initiate any withdrawal, renegotiation, or other procedure available under that instrument. Nothing in this subsection delays the domestic effective date of subsection (d) or authorizes the Executive to revive a Federal intellectual property right repealed or terminated by this section.

**(f) Offices, records, and pending applications.**—

(1) On the date of enactment, the United States Patent and Trademark Office, the Copyright Office, and the Plant Variety Protection Office shall cease issuing, registering, or renewing rights repealed or terminated by this section. A pending application does not create a right, and the responsible office shall refund the unearned portion of a prepaid examination, issuance, registration, or renewal fee. Amounts in an existing fee account of the responsible office remain available without fiscal year limitation for those refunds and associated wind-down costs, and there are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury such additional sums as are necessary to make a refund required by this paragraph.

(2) Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment, the Secretary of Commerce, the Librarian of Congress, and the Secretary of Agriculture shall transfer records having continuing evidentiary, historical, or contractual value to the National Archives and Records Administration or another lawful custodian that provides public access consistent with privacy and records law. Records needed to adjudicate pre-enactment conduct shall remain reasonably accessible for the limitations period applicable to that conduct.

(3) Each statutory office and function that exists solely under a provision repealed or terminated by this section terminates 180 days after the date of enactment. Personnel and property shall be treated under sections 107 and 202, and no wind-down authority may issue or revive an exclusive right.

### SEC. 104. DEREGULATION OF LABOR MARKETS

**(a) Abolition of Federal wage mandates.**—Section 6 of the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 (29 U.S.C. 206), including subsection (d) of that section (commonly known as the Equal Pay Act), is repealed effective 180 days after the date of enactment of this Act.

**(b) Repeal of Federal overtime mandates.**—Section 7 of the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 (29 U.S.C. 207) is repealed, effective 180 days after the date of enactment of this Act.

**(c) No effect on State law or contract.**—Nothing in this section preempts a State or local wage-and-hour law or impairs a wage, hour, or overtime obligation established by contract. No provision of Federal law that merely cross-references section 6 or 7 of the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 shall be construed to recreate the requirement repealed by this section.

**(d) Federal contract and assistance wage mandates.**—Effective on the date specified in subsection (a), subchapter IV of chapter 31 and sections 3702 and 3703 of title 40, United States Code, chapters 65 and 67 of title 41, United States Code, and each provision of Federal law incorporating or applying those provisions are repealed or have no prospective effect to the extent they establish, administer, or enforce a minimum-wage, prevailing-wage, fringe-benefit, overtime, work-hour, wage-determination, certified-payroll, withholding, liquidated-damages, or debarment requirement as a condition of a Federal contract, grant, loan, guarantee, or other financial assistance. The termination includes a materially equivalent requirement under section 113 of title 23, section 5310 of title 42, section 5333(a) of title 49, or another preexisting law. A reference to a repealed or inoperative provision does not recreate the requirement. This subsection does not impair wages or benefits already earned, a valid pre-effective-date contract, a voluntary private agreement, State or local law, or a law against slavery, involuntary servitude, forced labor, trafficking, violence, fraud, or theft.

### SEC. 105. REPEAL OF FEDERAL GUN-CONTROL LAWS AND ABOLITION OF THE ATF

**(a) Definitions.**—In this section:

(1) The term "firearm" means—

(A) a weapon, including a starter gun, antique arm, machinegun, short-barreled arm, large-bore arm, or other projectile arm, that is designed to, may readily be converted to, or does expel a projectile by the action of an explosive;

(B) the frame or receiver of such a weapon, a firearm muffler or silencer, a machinegun-conversion device, and a combination of parts designed or intended to assemble or convert such a weapon; and

(C) a projectile weapon having a barrel, regardless of bore diameter, action, ignition, date of manufacture, or intended sporting, defensive, collector, or other lawful use.

The term does not include a bomb, hand grenade, explosive rocket or missile, mine, or chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear device that is not a projectile weapon having a barrel.

(2) The term "ammunition" means a cartridge case, primer, bullet or other projectile, propellant, shell, or complete round designed or ordinarily used in a firearm. The term does not include bulk explosive material or an explosive or incendiary device not packaged or designed as a firearm component or round.

(3) The term "firearm accessory" means a magazine, feeding device, sight, optic, stock, brace, grip, trigger, suppressor, mount, holster, safety device, replacement or conversion part, tool, software, technical file, instruction, or other article principally useful with, for, or in the manufacture or repair of a firearm or ammunition.

(4) The term "firearm activity" means manufacturing, making, designing, assembling, modifying, repairing, printing, publishing information about, acquiring, importing, exporting, possessing, carrying, transporting, mailing, shipping, receiving, selling, buying, lending, gifting, bequeathing, insuring, financing, or otherwise transferring or using a firearm, ammunition, firearm accessory, imitation firearm, or related information.

(5) The term "Federal gun-control requirement" means a Federal prohibition, criminal or civil liability, licensing or registration requirement, tax, fee, marking or serialization mandate, background check, waiting period, age or status restriction, quantity or feature limit, recordkeeping or reporting duty, zoning or business restriction, import or export control, postal or carrier restriction, grant condition, eligibility disability, enhanced penalty, forfeiture, or other legal burden that applies because of firearm activity.

(6) The definitions in this subsection apply only to this section. They do not replace a definition in an unrepealed statute or convert a firearm into an explosive, destructive device, contraband article, or dangerous weapon under another law. An offense whose elements and punishment do not depend on firearm status remains enforceable even if a firearm is evidence or an instrument of the offense.

**(b) Express repeals and amendments.**—Effective 180 days after the date of enactment—

(1) the National Firearms Act and chapter 53 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 (26 U.S.C. 5801 et seq.) are repealed;

(5) section 1715 of title 18, United States Code, section 5001 of title 15, United States Code, and sections 4181 and 4182 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 are repealed;

>
> (b) **Criminal penalty involving disregard for human life.**—An individual who willfully and without regard for the safety of human life, or with reckless disregard for the safety of human life, violates subsection (a) shall be fined under title 18, imprisoned for not more than 20 years, or both, and, if death results to any person, shall be imprisoned for any term of years or for life.
>

The item relating to section 46505 in the table of sections for chapter 465 of title 49, United States Code, is amended to read: "46505. Carrying an explosive or incendiary device on an aircraft.";

(7) sections 5104 and 5109(a) of title 40, United States Code, are amended as follows:

**(c) Comprehensive termination of remaining Federal gun control.**—On the effective date in subsection (b), a provision of Federal law in effect immediately before that date, and any regulation, rule, order, guidance, license, permit, registration, classification, determination, contract condition, grant condition, or enforcement policy issued under it, has no prospective effect to the extent it imposes or authorizes a Federal gun-control requirement. This subsection includes a requirement imposed under section 2778 of title 22, United States Code, the Export Control Reform Act of 2018 (50 U.S.C. 4801 et seq.), a customs law, a Federal-property law, an appropriations rider, a sentencing or supervision provision, or an authority of an executive, legislative, or judicial branch entity. A firearm, ammunition, firearm accessory, imitation firearm, technical file, or firearm activity may not be treated as contraband, a regulated defense article, a controlled export, nonmailable matter, a prohibited dangerous weapon, or an aggravating status under Federal law solely by reason of that character. Section 2778 of title 22, the Export Control Reform Act of 2018, and their implementing regulations do not authorize a firearm-specific license, registration, classification, end-use condition, technical-data restriction, or penalty. A generally applicable embargo or sanctions rule directed to all transactions with a named foreign country, government, organization, or person, and a control independently directed to a missile or chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear device, may apply without a firearm-specific license, classification, or condition.

The firearm-specific portions of 14 C.F.R. 135.119; 22 C.F.R. parts 121, 123, and 127; 27 C.F.R. part 447; 36 C.F.R. 2.4, 261.8, 261.10, 327.13, 504.14, 520.15, 702.7, and 1280.14; 39 C.F.R. 232.1(l); 41 C.F.R. 102-74.440; 43 C.F.R. 423.30; 49 C.F.R. 1540.111; 50 C.F.R. 27.42; 6 C.F.R. 139.75; 7 C.F.R. 500.12; 15 C.F.R. 265.39 and 742.17; 31 C.F.R. 91.13 and 407.13; 32 C.F.R. 234.10; and 38 C.F.R. 1.218 are revoked. Each listed regulation remains in force only for a nonfirearm dangerous weapon, explosive or incendiary device, hazardous material, actual or threatened violence, or another subject independently regulated without regard to firearm activity. Omission of a regulation from this list does not preserve it from the general rule in this subsection.

**(d) No administrative re-creation; records and systems.**—

(1) No Federal officer may enforce, reissue, transfer, rename, or substantially reproduce a requirement terminated by this section under another statute, a general administrative authority, management of Federal property, a procurement or grant condition, or a reference to public safety, dangerous weapons, contraband, or national security. A later Act shall not be construed to impose a Federal gun-control requirement unless it expressly identifies this section and states the particular requirement, or unless the later Act is irreconcilable with this section to that extent. This sentence is a rule of construction and does not purport to bind a later Congress.

(2) On the effective date in subsection (b), each Federal firearm license, registration, tax stamp, permit, approval, disability, denial, and classification ceases to create a Federal duty, privilege, or restriction. Its expiration or cancellation does not impair a private contract or property interest.

(3) The Attorney General shall terminate the National Instant Criminal Background Check System as a firearm-eligibility system and each Federal registry, trace repository, out-of-business record system, or comparable collection maintained solely to administer Federal gun control. Records needed to afford relief under subsection (e), return property, or resolve a preserved non-firearm offense may be placed in a segregated temporary archive with access logs and may not be used to regulate later firearm activity. Not later than 1 year after the last such matter is resolved, the Attorney General shall return a record to its lawful non-Federal originator or securely destroy the Federal copy and shall publish an independently audited destruction and retention report that discloses no personal information.

(4) No Federal amount may be obligated or expended on or after the effective date to create, administer, enforce, research for purposes of creating, or subsidize another government's administration or enforcement of a Federal gun-control requirement or another gun law. An unobligated balance available solely for such a purpose is rescinded. This paragraph does not prevent the Armed Forces or a Federal law-enforcement or custodial entity from acquiring, maintaining, or training with government-owned arms for official use, preserve a restriction on private firearm activity, or create authority otherwise repealed by this Act.

**(e) Pending matters, convictions, and property.**—Notwithstanding section 109 of title 1, United States Code, or section 603 of this Act—

(1) no Federal investigation, prosecution, civil action, administrative proceeding, sentence, supervision condition, fine, assessment, forfeiture, or disability may begin or continue on or after the effective date in subsection (b) to the extent it depends on conduct that this section makes no longer a Federal offense or violation;

(2) the Attorney General shall move to dismiss each pending count described in paragraph (1). Not later than 30 days after enactment, the Bureau of Prisons and each probation office shall identify every person held or supervised under such a count. Not later than 90 days after enactment, the Attorney General, each United States attorney, the clerk of each district court, and the Administrative Office of the United States Courts shall identify every reasonably discoverable pending or final Federal count, civil judgment, forfeiture, disability, and supervision condition that may qualify for relief, notify the affected person at the person's last known address and place of custody, and provide a public case list that omits personal information not already in a public judicial record;

(3) the Attorney General shall file, not later than 180 days after enactment, a motion to vacate every final conviction and sentence for a count described in paragraph (1). The affected person need not file a motion or pay a fee and may file if the United States does not. The sentencing court shall decide the motion promptly on the existing record unless a hearing is necessary to separate conduct under a surviving offense. A person imprisoned or supervised solely under a vacated count shall be released from Federal custody or supervision immediately. If another count remains, the court shall resentence the person on that count without increasing the aggregate punishment lawfully imposed for the remaining conduct;

(4) an unpaid fine, assessment, civil penalty, fee, tax, or forfeiture based solely on a vacated or dismissed count or on post-effective-date firearm activity is canceled. A paid fine, assessment, civil penalty, forfeiture judgment, post-effective-date firearm tax or fee, and the unearned portion of a license, registration, permit, or tax-stamp fee shall be refunded with interest from the date of payment at the rate applicable to a civil judgment. This paragraph does not refund victim restitution, a tax lawfully imposed on pre-effective-date conduct, or an amount independently due for a surviving offense, and there are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury such sums as are necessary for the refunds;

(5) not later than 30 days after enactment, the Attorney General shall publish a single no-fee claim procedure for property seized, administratively forfeited, civilly forfeited, or transferred to another Federal, State, territorial, local, or Tribal custodian solely under a requirement terminated by this section. Each Federal custodian shall inventory the property and notify the apparent owner. A claimant may establish ownership from reasonably available records and is entitled to a written decision and an evidentiary hearing before a neutral officer if a material fact is disputed. Failure to file an administrative claim does not forfeit the judicial remedy in paragraph (6);

(6) the United States shall retrieve from another governmental custodian and return, not later than 60 days after the effective date or allowance of a later claim, a firearm, ammunition, firearm accessory, money, or other property seized or forfeited solely under a requirement terminated by this section to its owner or the owner's designated lawful transferee. If the United States destroyed, disposed of, or cannot retrieve the property, the owner may recover fair market value at the time of disposition plus interest in the United States Court of Federal Claims, and the United States waives sovereign immunity for that relief. This paragraph does not require return to a thief, impair the superior right of a nonconsenting third party, return property still needed as evidence in a prosecution for an independent offense, or prevent a forfeiture proved under a law that does not depend on the property's status as a firearm or firearm-related article; and

(7) an agency may retain a record or property beyond a deadline in this subsection only on a particularized court order finding that the item is necessary to adjudicate relief under this subsection or prove an independent surviving offense and setting the shortest reasonable retention period. A general assertion of administrative burden, public safety, or possible future use is insufficient.

The clerk of each court granting relief under this subsection shall mark the affected count vacated and transmit the order to each Federal database known to contain the conviction, count, or resulting disability. A Federal officer shall remove the vacated count and any disability based solely on it not later than 30 days after receipt.

**(f) Abolition of the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives.**—On the effective date in subsection (b), the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives (commonly known as "ATF") is abolished, section 599A of title 28, United States Code, is repealed, and section 599B of that title has no effect to the extent it applies to ATF. A firearm-related function, statutory office, task-force seat, membership, liaison, grant-administration role, and duty assigned to ATF terminates and does not transfer. A function confined to enforcing chapter 40 of title 18 against a bomb, grenade, explosive rocket or missile, mine, bulk explosive material, or incendiary device not within the definitions in subsection (a) transfers to the Federal Bureau of Investigation, together with the minimum personnel, property, and records necessary to perform that function. A reference to ATF or its Director concerning that transferred explosives function is deemed to refer to the Attorney General or the Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation and does not revive firearm authority. Authority under section 599B relating to the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau is not affected before the tax conversion date.

**(g) Matters preserved.**—This section does not—

(1) impair a generally applicable law against homicide, assault, threat, kidnapping, robbery, burglary, theft, fraud, property damage, trespass, arson, sabotage, hijacking, terrorism, reckless endangerment, obstruction of justice, or use of force merely because a firearm is evidence or an instrument of the offense, but no separate element, liability, disability, forfeiture, or punishment may arise solely from firearm activity;

(2) limit a law governing a bomb, grenade, explosive rocket or missile, mine, bulk explosive material, or incendiary, chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear device, but a firearm, ammunition, or firearm accessory may not be brought within such a law solely because it contains a primer, propellant, or ordinary cartridge component;

(3) prevent the Armed Forces from controlling issuance and official use of arms owned by the United States, a custodian from preventing a person in lawful detention from possessing a weapon within a secure custodial facility, or an evidence custodian from preserving an item for an independent criminal proceeding;

(4) impair the right of a private owner, employer, school, carrier, insurer, association, or other private person to set conditions concerning firearms on private property or in a voluntary contract;

(5) repeal section 104906 of title 54, United States Code, or another provision that protects a person from a governmental restriction on lawful firearm activity. This paragraph does not preserve a Federal immunity from otherwise applicable State tort, contract, warranty, or property law; or

(6) prevent either House of Congress from exercising its authority under article I, section 5 of the Constitution to adopt and enforce a rule confined to its proceedings, chamber, and rooms subject to its exclusive institutional control, or prevent a court from issuing and enforcing a case-specific order reasonably necessary to maintain order, decorum, and the immediate security of a particular proceeding. Neither exception authorizes a generally applicable Federal firearm offense, license, registration, disability, property restriction, or rule extending beyond the institution and function from which the constitutional authority arises.

**(h) Interstate transportation.**—A person who may lawfully possess a firearm, ammunition, or firearm accessory under the law of the place where an interstate journey begins and the place where it ends is entitled to transport that property through an intervening State or political subdivision notwithstanding its law, if during transportation the firearm is not readily accessible from the passenger compartment or, in a vehicle without a separate compartment, is secured in a locked container other than the glove compartment or console. A reasonable stop for fuel, food, lodging, medical care, vehicle service, an emergency, or another incident of the journey does not end the protection. This subsection is enacted under Congress's power to regulate actual interstate commerce and does not authorize a Federal license, registry, or preclearance.

**(i) State, territorial, and Tribal law; no commandeering.**—Except for the interstate-transport protection in subsection (h), this section neither enacts nor preempts a State, territorial, local, or Tribal firearm law and does not require a non-Federal government or officer to enforce Federal law, receive Federal records or property, perform a background check, or maintain a firearm restriction. Federal repeal applies on the same terms in every territory and possession; this subsection does not preserve a Federal gun-control requirement there.

**(j) Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau.**—On the tax conversion date, the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau is abolished. A prospective Federal permit, registration, formula approval, label approval, production restriction, reporting requirement, or trade-practice requirement administered by that Bureau under the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, the Federal Alcohol Administration Act (27 U.S.C. 201 et seq.), or another preexisting law has no effect with respect to conduct occurring on or after that date. A function and record necessary to resolve a pre-conversion tax liability or refund shall transfer to the Value-Added Tax Administration; other records, valid contracts, and preserved obligations shall be handled under sections 202 and 603. This subsection does not limit a generally applicable law concerning fraud, adulteration causing serious physical injury, customs, sanctions, or criminal contraband.

### SEC. 106. TRANSPORTATION SECURITY DEREGULATION AND ABOLITION OF THE TSA

**(a) Transportation Security Administration.**—Effective on the date specified in subsection (c), the Transportation Security Administration (referred to in this section as "TSA") is abolished.

**(b) No Federal passenger-screening mandate.**—On and after the effective date specified in subsection (c), no Federal law shall require passenger screening or baggage screening by a Federal agency as a condition of travel on a common carrier by air. Nothing in this subsection shall be construed to limit the authority of an airport operator, aircraft operator, or other private carrier to impose screening or other conditions of carriage pursuant to contract.

**(c) Effective date.**—The repeal of law made by section 102(a)(15) and section 102(a)(16), and the abolition under subsection (a), shall take effect on the date that is 180 days after the date of enactment of this Act.

**(d) Savings for border, criminal, and defense authorities.**—Nothing in this section shall be construed to repeal or limit authority to—

(1) conduct inspections or searches at the international border or its functional equivalent under otherwise applicable law;\
(2) investigate or prosecute criminal offenses, including offenses relating to aircraft piracy, sabotage, or terrorism; or\
(3) provide for the common defense and protection of Federal property under otherwise applicable law.

**(e) Duties arising under other statutes.**—Subsections (e) through (i) of section 101, and sections 107 and 202, apply to TSA and to a TSA function arising under a provision other than section 114 or chapter 449 of title 49, United States Code. A function necessary to exercise an authority preserved by subsection (d) shall be transferred to an appropriate component of the Department of Homeland Security or the Department of Justice. Any other prospective screening, credentialing, vetting, approval, fee, or regulatory duty assigned to TSA terminates on the abolition date, and a statutory reference to TSA does not continue the duty or the agency.

### SEC. 107. TRANSITIONAL RULES

For purposes of this section, the term "affected entity" means an entity abolished under this title and, within an entity that is not abolished, only the office, component, or function to the extent it administers a provision, requirement, program, or function repealed or terminated by this title. A limitation in this section applies only to the affected office, component, or function and does not restrict an unrelated authority of the surviving entity. Before the applicable effective date, ordinary administration and enforcement of law that remains in force may continue to the extent consistent with subsection (a)(1) and the timely processing rule in subsection (a)(2).

**(a) Wind-down authority.**—During the period between the date of enactment of this Act and the effective date of an abolition or repeal under this title, the head of an affected entity may take only such actions as are reasonably necessary to preserve records and property, satisfy obligations preserved by this Act, transfer a retained function, avoid an entry freeze under paragraph (2), and conduct an orderly reduction in force.

(1) Except as necessary for those purposes, the entity may not issue a new regulation, grant, loan, loan guarantee, or contract extending beyond the effective date. A rule that removes or reduces an existing restriction may take effect during the wind-down.

(2) While a prospective Federal approval requirement remains in force, the entity shall continue timely processing and may issue a permit, license, registration, or approval necessary for otherwise lawful entry or competition. Such an approval may impose only conditions required by then-existing law and expires when the underlying requirement is repealed, unless another provision of this Act preserves it. An agency's failure to act during the wind-down may not be used to bar conduct after the requirement terminates.

(3) Notwithstanding paragraph (1), the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau may continue ordinary assessment, collection, refund, customs-coordination, and taxpayer-service functions for a tax or duty that remains in force before the tax conversion date. It may not use that authority to enlarge a tax or prospective regulatory requirement or extend an obligation beyond the date on which the underlying law terminates.

(4) Notwithstanding paragraph (2), on the effective date in section 105(b), a pending Federal firearm, ammunition, firearm accessory, National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS), National Firearms Act, import, export, mailing, transfer, license, registration, tax-stamp, classification, or disability-relief application terminates without approval, denial, adverse inference, or prospective condition because Federal permission is no longer required. The responsible agency shall refund an unearned fee with interest under section 105(e)(4), return submitted property and original documents, and expunge a denial or delay code that has no independent lawful basis.

**(b) Notice.**—Not later than 90 days after the date of enactment of this Act, each affected entity shall publish a wind-down notice that identifies relevant deadlines, the treatment of pending applications and proceedings, the custodian of records, and a contact for claims.

**(c) State participation voluntary.**—An affected entity may offer a State records, nonhazardous property, and technical assistance relating to a terminated function. Acceptance by a State is voluntary and may not be conditioned on the enactment or administration of a State regulatory program.

**(d) Federal personnel.**—A reduction in force or transfer of personnel under this title shall be conducted under otherwise applicable Federal employment law. Nothing in this subsection guarantees continued employment or preserves a position that this Act abolishes.

**(e) Operative disposition inventory.**—Not later than 90 days after the date of enactment, each affected entity shall publish a machine-readable inventory identifying every statutory provision, regulation, order, permit class, exclusive franchise, subsidy, and material information collection that it administers. For each item, the inventory shall identify the date and provision of this Act under which the item is repealed, terminated, preserved, transferred, or limited to pre-effective-date conduct. An omission does not preserve an authority or duty. A published classification is binding on the issuing agency as its litigating and enforcement position but does not bind a court or alter the legal effect of this Act. A person directly regulated by an item may, within 180 days after publication or a threatened contrary enforcement action, bring a civil action for declaratory or injunctive relief in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia or the district in which the person resides or has its principal place of business. The waiver of sovereign immunity and fee rule in section 606(c) apply to that action.

**(f) No continuation of incumbency privilege.**—A Federal permit, license, allocation, approval, charter, or other regulatory instrument does not survive a repeal as an exclusive franchise or right to exclude a new entrant merely because it was issued before the repeal. This subsection does not take tangible property, cancel a private contract, eliminate a specifically defined spectrum-use right established under subsection (g), deny compensation required by the Fifth Amendment, limit an otherwise available remedy under the Tucker Act, or bar payment from the appropriation under section 1304 of title 31, United States Code.

**(g) Spectrum and communications transition.**—For purposes of this subsection, the "communications repeal date" is the effective date of the repeal under section 102(a)(10).

(1) Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment, the Federal Communications Commission and the National Telecommunications and Information Administration shall publish a machine-readable inventory of non-Federal and Federal spectrum assignments, technical parameters, license expiration dates, numbering resources, emergency-communications dependencies, and unresolved interference claims.

(2) On the communications repeal date—

(A) an existing non-Federal license becomes a transferable spectrum-use right until the expiration date stated in the license, with the same frequency, geography, power, emission, and interference parameters, but no right to renewal or to exclude a technically compatible use that does not cause harmful interference;

(B) spectrum that is unassigned or for which a temporary right under subparagraph (A) has expired may be used without Federal permission, subject to contract and to remedies for harmful interference; no unlicensed user, including the former licensee, acquires an exclusive or perpetual priority merely through earlier use, and use before expiration of a temporary right creates no post-expiration priority;

(C) a Federal assignment strictly necessary for national defense, air navigation, border operations, or emergency communications remains in force and shall be administered by the National Telecommunications and Information Administration solely for that governmental use; and

(D) no officer may create or renew a post-repeal exclusive non-Federal right except under an Act of Congress.

The National Telecommunications and Information Administration shall maintain a ministerial public registry of each temporary right and transfer under subparagraph (A). A transfer is effective between the parties when executed and against a later purchaser or lien creditor when recorded; as between inconsistent transfers, the first transferee to record without notice of the other transfer has priority. Registry authority expires when the last temporary right expires and confers no allocation, renewal, or rulemaking power.

(3) A holder or lawful user injured by knowing, reckless, or continuing harmful interference may bring a civil action for actual damages and narrowly tailored injunctive relief in a court of competent jurisdiction. "Harmful interference" means radio-frequency energy that materially degrades, obstructs, or repeatedly interrupts a spectrum use within its lawful technical parameters and does not include mere economic competition. The court shall apply objective engineering evidence, protect compatible uses, require reasonable coordination where feasible, and, between incompatible unlicensed uses, select the narrowest practicable time, place, power, or technical limitation without creating a perpetual exclusive right. This paragraph creates no right to suppress competition or speech based on content.

(4) Before the communications repeal date, the Secretary of Commerce shall facilitate through open competition the formation of a user-owned, nonprofit utility for telephone numbering and comparable shared addressing resources under transparent nondiscrimination, portability, cost-based fee, audit, cybersecurity, conflict-of-interest, and dispute-resolution rules. A provider participates only through a voluntary reciprocal contract. Federal facilitation or recognition confers no franchise, sovereign power, or exclusive legal authority; an allocation or dispute decision binds only contracting participants, carries no governmental penalty, and may not prohibit a nonparticipant from using a lawful technically interoperable alternative. The utility's governing body shall fairly represent large and small providers, business and individual users, and technical experts, and no provider or affiliated group may control it. A decision shall apply published contractual rules, state reasons, and be reviewable through prompt arbitration or in a court of competent jurisdiction under the participation contract. The utility has no Federal credit, power to regulate entry or rates, or authority beyond contractual resource coordination. The Secretary may provide interim administration for Federal systems and willing participants for not more than 2 years if no qualified utility is operational on the communications repeal date.

### SEC. 108. RESTORATION OF PRIVATE FREEDOM OF ASSOCIATION

**(a) General rule.**—Effective on the date that is 1 year after the date of enactment, a provision of Federal law enacted before this Act has no prospective effect to the extent it compels a private person to enter, continue, or refrain from terminating an employment, sale, lease, service, credit, membership, housing, or other consensual relationship, or to select or retain a counterparty, because of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, familial status, or genetic information. For purposes of this section, a "private person" does not include the United States, a State, territory, or possession or a political subdivision thereof, a federally recognized Indian Tribe, or a person to the extent the person is acting under color of Federal, State, territorial, or local law.

**(b) Specific private-sector provisions.**—On the effective date specified in subsection (a), and consistent with that subsection—

(1) section 201 of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. 2000a) is repealed, and sections 203 through 207 of that Act (42 U.S.C. 2000a–2 through 2000a–6) remain in force only to the extent necessary to enforce section 202 of that Act (42 U.S.C. 2000a–1) against discrimination or segregation required under color of governmental law;

(2) title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. 2000e et seq.) and an amendment or remedy dependent on that title have no effect with respect to the conduct of a private employer, employment agency, labor organization, joint labor-management committee, or apprenticeship program, but remain in force with respect to a governmental employer to the extent otherwise constitutional;

(3) the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 (29 U.S.C. 621 et seq.) has no effect with respect to a private employer, employment agency, or labor organization, but section 15 of that Act (29 U.S.C. 633a) and provisions necessary to enforce it remain in force for Federal employment;

(4) titles I and III of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (42 U.S.C. 12111 et seq. and 12181 et seq.), and each remedy, retaliation, coercion, or interference provision dependent on a right under either title, have no effect with respect to a private person, but title II of that Act and provisions governing a governmental entity remain in force to the extent otherwise constitutional;

(5) title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968, commonly known as the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. 3601 et seq.), has no effect with respect to the voluntary disposition, financing, rental, or occupancy of privately owned property by a private person, but remains in force against conduct under color of governmental law;

(6) titles I and II of the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act of 2008 and the amendments made by those titles have no effect with respect to a private employer, employment agency, labor organization, joint labor-management committee, health insurer, insurance issuer, group health plan, or plan sponsor, but remain in force for a governmental employer or governmental health plan to the extent otherwise constitutional;

(7) the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (15 U.S.C. 1691 et seq.) has no effect with respect to a private creditor's voluntary selection of a counterparty or terms, but remains in force against a governmental creditor to the extent otherwise constitutional;

(8) section 1981(c) of title 42, United States Code, is amended by striking "impairment by nongovernmental discrimination and impairment under color of State law" and inserting "impairment under color of Federal, State, territorial, or local law"; and

(9) section 1982 of title 42, United States Code, is amended by inserting before the period at the end the following: ", but only against impairment under color of Federal, State, territorial, or local law".

**(c) Equal Employment Opportunity Commission.**—

(1) The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission is abolished on the effective date in subsection (a), and a function relating solely to prospective enforcement of a private-sector mandate terminated by this section terminates.

(2) The Office of Personnel Management succeeds to all preserved Federal-employment functions of the Commission other than the adjudicatory functions assigned by this paragraph, including intake, investigation, conciliation, notice, rulemaking, plan review and approval, program evaluation, progress reporting, consultation, coordination, orders and instructions, records, personnel, and systems functions. The Merit Systems Protection Board succeeds to all preserved Federal-employment adjudication, final-order, and administrative-appellate functions, with de novo judicial review preserved to the extent provided by the law governing the claim. A final administrative determination shall be made by the Board or an adjudicating officer appointed in accordance with section 601(d).

(3) The Civil Rights Division of the Department of Justice succeeds to all preserved State, territorial, possession, or local governmental-employment functions of the Commission other than the adjudicatory functions assigned by this paragraph, including intake, investigation, conciliation, notice, referral, right-to-sue, rulemaking, program review, consultation, coordination, orders and instructions, records, and litigation functions, and may complete an administrative prerequisite otherwise assigned to the Commission. The Merit Systems Protection Board succeeds to each preserved adjudication, final-order, and administrative-appellate function under section 304 of the Government Employee Rights Act of 1991 (42 U.S.C. 2000e–16c) or another governmental-employment provision. A final order of the Board under that section is reviewable under chapter 158 of title 28, United States Code, and the Board is deemed the agency for purposes of that review.

(4) On the effective date in subsection (a), subsections (b), (c), and (d) of section 7702 of title 5, United States Code, are repealed, and the special panel described in the former subsection (d) terminates. A decision of the Merit Systems Protection Board under subsection (a) of that section is final agency action and a judicially reviewable action. Section 7702(e) and section 7703 of that title remain in force, a reference in either section to action under a repealed subsection is disregarded, and the existing right to file a civil action and obtain a trial de novo on a preserved discrimination claim is not narrowed. If the Board has not issued final action within 120 days after an appeal is filed under section 7702(a), the employee or applicant may file the civil action authorized by section 7702(e). A petition, reconsideration, or special-panel matter pending under a repealed subsection becomes a judicially reviewable action on the effective date; the Board shall promptly notify each party, and a 30-day period for filing a civil action begins when the party receives that notice.

(5) The Attorney General shall establish a temporary Civil Rights Claims Wind-Down Unit to receive every charge, Commission suit, decree file, and record involving private conduct occurring before the effective date. The Unit may investigate past conduct, make a nonbinding reasonable-cause determination, issue a notice necessary to permit judicial review or a private action, substitute the United States in pending Commission litigation, collect a final judicial award or voluntary settlement, and seek modification of prospective relief under subsection (f), but may not apply a terminated mandate to later conduct or begin a charge based on later conduct. Final private civil liability and contested legal relief may be determined only by a court of competent jurisdiction, subject to a party's right to jury trial, or resolved by voluntary settlement. The Unit terminates after the last preserved matter is resolved and its records are transferred to the National Archives and Records Administration.

(6) A reference in Federal law to the Commission or its Chair, member, officer, or employee is deemed to refer to the appropriate successor or the successor's head or designee only with respect to the preserved function assigned by this subsection. A transfer does not revive a private-sector mandate, create a duplicate seat on a board or interagency body, or authorize one successor to review its own final action.

(7) The Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall allocate the Commission's personnel, contracts, property, systems, and unobligated budget authority among the Office of Personnel Management, the Merit Systems Protection Board, and the Department of Justice in proportion to the transferred workload. Transferred balances remain available without fiscal year limitation solely for a preserved governmental-employment function or the temporary wind-down in paragraph (5), are subject to separate audit, and shall be rescinded when the assigned work is complete.

**(d) Matters preserved.**—This section does not—

(1) authorize discrimination, segregation, or unequal treatment by the United States, a State, territory, possession, or political subdivision, or a person acting under color of Federal, State, territorial, or local law, or narrow a remedy against governmental action under the Constitution or section 1983 of title 42, United States Code;

(2) limit a prohibition on slavery, involuntary servitude, forced labor, trafficking, violence, threat, trespass, fraud, theft, or destruction of property;

(3) impair a voluntary contract, deed restriction, association rule, insurer standard, exchange rule, or other private undertaking requiring nondiscrimination, or prevent a private organization from conditioning participation on such an undertaking;

(4) prevent the United States from governing its own employment, property, procurement, or armed forces, or from stating an unambiguous prospective condition directly related to a voluntarily accepted Federal payment; or

(5) create a Federal privilege or immunity from generally applicable State, territorial, or local contract, tort, property, civil-rights, or public-accommodation law; or

(6) notwithstanding paragraph (4), authorize a condition of a Federal contract, grant, loan, guarantee, or other assistance that compels a private person to enter, continue, or refrain from terminating an employment, sale, lease, service, credit, membership, housing, or other consensual relationship because of a ground specified in subsection (a). This paragraph does not impair a voluntary private undertaking or a condition directed to governmental conduct, slavery, involuntary servitude, forced labor, trafficking, violence, fraud, theft, or performance of the specific goods or services purchased by the United States.

**(e) State and territorial law; no commandeering.**—Nothing in this section preempts a State, territorial, or local law regulating discrimination or private association; requires a State, territory, or political subdivision to enact or repeal such a law; or requires a State or territorial officer to administer this section.

**(f) Savings.**—Section 603 applies to conduct occurring before the effective date in subsection (a), but no coercive statutory term of an injunction or consent decree based solely on a private-sector mandate terminated by this section may compel prospective conduct after that date. A court may modify such prospective relief while preserving a final award for past conduct and any independently enforceable promise that the parties voluntarily undertook in a settlement.

### SEC. 109. ELIMINATION OF FEDERAL DISPARATE-IMPACT LIABILITY

**(a) Intentional-discrimination standard.**—Effective 180 days after the date of enactment, no provision of Federal law, regulation, guidance, grant condition, condition imposed by the United States in a Federal contract, enforcement policy, or judicially inferred cause of action may impose liability, a penalty, loss of funds, a burden of justification, compelled preference, or other adverse legal consequence because a facially neutral act, practice, qualification, standard, allocation, or policy produces a statistical disparity, disparate impact, disproportionate effect, demographic imbalance, or unequal group outcome. A Federal discrimination claim requires proof that the defendant adopted, maintained, applied, or enforced the challenged act because of a purpose to discriminate on a ground prohibited by the otherwise applicable law.

**(b) Specific repeals and construction.**—On the effective date specified in subsection (a), and consistent with that subsection—

(1) subsection (k) of section 703 of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. 2000e–2(k)) is repealed;

(2) section 2 of the Voting Rights Act of 1965 (52 U.S.C. 10301) is amended—

(A) in subsection (a), by striking "in a manner which results in a denial or abridgement of" and inserting "with a purpose to deny or abridge";

(B) in subsection (a), by striking ", as provided in subsection (b)"; and

(C) by striking subsection (b); and

(3) a provision or rule implementing title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. 2000d et seq.), title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972 (20 U.S.C. 1681 et seq.), section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (29 U.S.C. 794), the Age Discrimination Act of 1975 (42 U.S.C. 6101 et seq.), the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 (29 U.S.C. 621 et seq.), the Fair Housing Act (42 U.S.C. 3601 et seq.), the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (42 U.S.C. 12101 et seq.), the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (15 U.S.C. 1691 et seq.), or another Federal law has no effect to the extent it authorizes disparate-impact, effects-based, statistical-imbalance, or results-only liability without proof of discriminatory purpose.

**(c) Evidence and burdens.**—Reliable statistical evidence may be admitted with other evidence as circumstantial proof of discriminatory purpose, but a numerical disparity without contextual evidence supporting a reasonable inference of discriminatory purpose does not shift a burden of proof or production, require a business-necessity or less-restrictive-alternative defense, establish a presumption, or authorize race-, sex-, or status-conscious relief. Nothing in this section forecloses proof of discriminatory purpose from an exceptionally stark and otherwise unexplained pattern under governing constitutional precedent. The claimant bears the burden of proving discriminatory purpose and each other element of the claim by the standard otherwise applicable to that claim.

**(d) Matters preserved.**—This section does not—

(1) legalize an express classification or intentional discrimination prohibited by the Constitution or an otherwise applicable Federal law;

(2) prevent the Federal Government or another person from collecting or publishing accurate aggregate statistics for a lawful purpose, subject to privacy law;

(3) prohibit voluntary outreach, accommodation, or private standards that do not impose a legal penalty on another person; or

(4) preempt a State, territorial, or local cause of action or regulatory standard, require a State or territory to adopt an intent requirement, or require a State or territorial officer to enforce this section.

This section does not treat an individualized accommodation of a known person's disability under a governmental duty preserved by section 108 as disparate-impact liability merely because the accommodation changes an otherwise general rule; the claimant must still prove each element of that individualized duty without relying solely on a group disparity.

**(e) Pending and prospective relief.**—Section 603 preserves a final award and liability for pre-effective-date conduct. On and after the effective date in subsection (a), no Federal officer or court may begin or continue prospective enforcement or relief based solely on a theory terminated by this section, and a court shall modify an injunction or consent decree to the minimum extent necessary to conform it to this section while preserving a voluntary contractual obligation and relief independently supported by proven intentional discrimination.

## TITLE II—FEDERAL BUDGET REFORMS

### SEC. 201. PRIVATIZATION OF SOCIAL SECURITY

**(a) Prospective repeal and conversion date.**—

(1) **Prospective repeal.**—Title II of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 401 et seq.) is repealed effective on the later of the tax conversion date and the date that is 5 years after the date of enactment of this Act, except that provisions of that title shall remain in force solely to the extent incorporated by this section for calculating, paying, reviewing, and enforcing a protected legacy benefit. If no tax conversion date has occurred, the 5-year date does not independently trigger repeal. Provisions establishing and operating the Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund and the Federal Disability Insurance Trust Fund remain in force solely as accounting and payment authorities until all obligations preserved by this section are discharged.

(2) **Conversion date.**—No wages, self-employment income, quarters of coverage, or other service after the tax conversion date shall create coverage or increase a benefit under title II of the Social Security Act.

**(b) Protected legacy benefits.**—

(1) **Current beneficiaries.**—An individual receiving an old-age, survivor, or disability insurance benefit under title II of the Social Security Act on the date of enactment of this Act has a protected legacy benefit equal to the benefit payable under the law in effect on the day before that date, as subsequently adjusted under the cost-of-living adjustment provisions of that title.

(2) **Individuals age 55 or older.**—An individual who is at least 55 years of age on the date of enactment of this Act and is not then receiving a benefit has a protected legacy benefit determined under title II of the Social Security Act as in effect on the day before the date of enactment, based on wages, self-employment income, quarters of coverage, and other service credited through the tax conversion date. The benefit becomes payable when the individual first satisfies the applicable eligibility conditions under that law.

(3) **Other covered individuals.**—Each individual not described in paragraph (1) or (2) who has wages, self-employment income, quarters of coverage, or other service credited under title II of the Social Security Act on or before the tax conversion date, including an individual born after the date of enactment, has a protected legacy benefit equal to the actuarial present value, as of the tax conversion date, of benefits attributable to that credited service through that date, determined under paragraph (4).

(4) **Actuarial determination.**—Not later than 9 months after the date of enactment, the Chief Actuary of the Social Security Administration shall recommend and publish proposed mortality, disability, interest-rate, wage-indexing, family-status, and other assumptions used to determine actuarial present value. Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, after considering those recommendations and an advisory review by the Comptroller General, the Secretary of the Treasury shall prescribe and publish the final methodology. If the Comptroller General does not submit a review within 60 days after receiving a complete proposed methodology, the Secretary may proceed and shall explain any material departure from the Chief Actuary's recommendation. Assumptions shall be applied uniformly by birth cohort and beneficiary class, identify uncertainty ranges, and include the expected value, as of the tax conversion date, of disability and survivor contingencies that may occur after that date. The methodology shall allocate auxiliary and survivor value among affected accounts and annuities without counting the same expected payment more than once.

**(c) Transition to private retirement accounts.**—

(1) **Statement and election.**—Not later than 2 years before the earliest candidate tax conversion date, and after the final methodology under subsection (b)(4) has been published, the Secretary of the Treasury shall provide each individual described in subsection (b)(3) a statement showing the individual's earnings record, estimated protected legacy benefit, material actuarial assumptions, available account options, fees, and the consequences of electing or declining a transfer. The ordinary election period begins on the date of the statement and ends 180 days before that candidate date. If a candidate date fails section 302(m), the Secretary shall issue a supplemental statement not later than 1 year before the next candidate date reflecting additional covered service and using the next candidate tax conversion date as the valuation date. An election may be revoked or changed during the 180 days after the supplemental statement is issued but in all events closes 180 days before the candidate date to which it relates. An individual who first acquires credited service after the initial statement deadline shall receive a statement within 90 days after that service is posted and shall have at least 180 days after receipt to elect. To the extent that period extends beyond 180 days before the applicable candidate tax conversion date or beyond the tax conversion date, the election is administered as a special election and does not delay conversion. If the Secretary fails to provide an individual a timely statement and full 180-day election period, the individual is treated as non-electing at conversion solely to preserve payment continuity but may make a special election during the 180 days after receiving a belated statement; on that election the Secretary shall issue the transfer instruments valued as of the conversion date and adjusted under paragraph (2). Administrative failure under this paragraph does not alter the objective eligibility test or tax conversion date. An election is irrevocable after the applicable ordinary or special election period closes.

(2) **Transfer instrument.**—For an electing individual described in subsection (b)(2) or (b)(3), or a beneficiary electing an account under subsection (e)(2)(A), the Secretary shall issue to a qualified custodian, for the sole benefit of that individual, a series of nonmarketable Treasury transition bonds with an aggregate principal amount equal to the actuarial present value, as of the tax conversion date, of the applicable protected legacy benefit remaining payable on that date, determined under subsection (b)(4) and reduced by the actuarial value, as of the tax conversion date, of each payment or transfer made after that date for the same obligation and included in that valuation. Each bond shall state the valuation and maturity dates, shall be adjusted after valuation by the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, and shall bear the real rate of interest used in the published actuarial assumptions. The maturity schedule shall begin no earlier than the individual's earliest retirement age and shall approximate the expected annual stream of benefits used in the valuation, except that the Secretary may accelerate a proportionate amount for disability or terminal illness. The bonds are obligations of the United States and shall be recorded as Federal debt when issued. At maturity the custodian may redeem a bond solely to acquire investments permitted under paragraph (3); redemption proceeds may not be distributed to the individual before a distribution is permitted under paragraph (3)(B). Issuance and irrevocable credit of the bonds to the qualified custodian satisfy and discharge the corresponding protected legacy benefit to the extent of the value transferred, including for a special or belated election, and the same actuarial value may not also support an annuity or direct benefit.

(3) **Investment and distribution.**—An account under this subsection shall—

(A) offer a low-cost default life-cycle investment and a limited range of diversified investment options comparable to those available under the Thrift Savings Plan;

(B) prohibit a distribution before the earliest age at which an old-age benefit would have been payable under title II of the Social Security Act as in effect on the day before the date of enactment, except for disability, terminal illness, or a qualified domestic relations order;

(C) provide for distributions over the expected lifetime of the individual or designated beneficiaries;

(D) be held in trust, segregated from the custodian's assets, and protected from assignment, alienation, and the claims of the custodian's creditors; and

(E) be subject to fiduciary, bonding, fee-disclosure, cybersecurity, and spousal-consent standards prescribed by the Secretary and enforceable by the account holder through a civil action for appropriate equitable or monetary relief.

(4) **No investment guarantee.**—The United States guarantees the Treasury transition bond but does not guarantee the return or principal of any private investment acquired after redemption of the bond, except as otherwise expressly provided by law.

(5) **Non-electing individuals.**—An individual described in subsection (b)(3) who does not elect a transfer shall receive an inflation-indexed annuity. The Secretary shall accumulate the actuarial present value fixed on the tax conversion date to the annuity commencement date using the published inflation and real-interest assumptions in subsection (b)(4), reduce that accumulated value by the consistently valued amount of any payment already made for the same obligation, and convert the resulting value on the commencement date into an actuarially equivalent payment stream. The payment form shall include each auxiliary and survivor amount allocated to that annuity under subsection (b)(4), including an amount payable after the individual's death, without duplicating a contingency paid under paragraph (7) or another provision. The annuity begins at the earliest retirement age for which the individual would have qualified under title II of the Social Security Act as in effect on the day before the date of enactment.

(6) **Payments during election or review.**—A pending election, record-correction request, or administrative or judicial review may not interrupt a protected legacy payment otherwise due. The Secretary may make a provisional payment subject to later prospective adjustment, but may recover an overpayment only under published hardship, notice, and review standards. A provisional payment attributable to value later transferred under paragraph (2) shall reduce the transferred principal by its value at the transfer date under the published methodology; the Secretary may not both recover the payment and reduce the principal. If a transfer is rescinded for administrative error or fraud, the corresponding direct benefit is restored only after the bonds and duplicative account assets are canceled or returned.

(7) **Contingent value for non-electing individuals.**—If an individual described in subsection (b)(3) who did not elect a transfer dies or becomes disabled after the tax conversion date and before the annuity under paragraph (5) begins, the contingent survivor or disability value allocated to that individual under subsection (b)(4) shall be paid as an actuarially equivalent annuity or structured settlement to the individual or the persons designated by the methodology. The payment is limited to the value fixed at conversion, accumulated to the disability or death date under the published inflation and real-interest assumptions and reduced by the consistently valued amount of any payment for the same contingency, and does not constitute accrual of a new benefit under title II of the Social Security Act.

**(d) Treatment of individuals age 55 and older.**—

(1) **Lifetime protection.**—An individual described in subsection (b)(1) or (b)(2) shall receive the protected legacy benefit for each period for which the individual remains eligible under the rules incorporated by subsection (b). The repeal under subsection (a) does not terminate or reduce that benefit merely because the individual reaches a specified age.

(2) **Optional transfer.**—The Secretary of the Treasury shall permit an individual described in subsection (b)(2) who has not begun receiving benefits to elect a transfer to an account under subsection (c). The Secretary shall provide an individualized statement and an election period of not less than 180 days before the transfer. The election shall use the actuarial assumptions required by subsection (b)(4), is subject to the disclosures, fiduciary protections, and spousal-consent rules in subsection (c), and, when completed, discharges the corresponding protected legacy benefit only to the extent of the value transferred so that no benefit is paid twice.

**(e) Disability and survivor benefits.**—

(1) **Current beneficiaries and pre-conversion events.**—An individual receiving a disability or survivor benefit on the date of enactment, and an individual whose qualifying disability or whose insured family member's death occurs on or before the tax conversion date, shall remain eligible for a protected legacy benefit under the eligibility, family-status, amount, indexing, payment-timing, and termination rules in effect on the day before the date of enactment, using wages, self-employment income, quarters of coverage, family status, and the qualifying disability or death through the tax conversion date as applicable. A post-enactment event is valued and paid under those incorporated rules without extending Federal coverage to a disability or death occurring after the tax conversion date.

(2) **Election to transfer.**—A beneficiary described in paragraph (1) may elect, within 5 years after receiving the notice required by the Secretary, to convert the actuarial present value of remaining benefits to—

(A) a private retirement account described in subsection (c), in the case of a disabled worker or other beneficiary who will be entitled to old-age benefits; or

(B) a structured settlement payable to the beneficiary or, in the case of a minor or legally incapacitated survivor, to a fiduciary subject to court or administrative supervision.

Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, or 90 days after a later qualifying disability or death is established, the Secretary shall give each beneficiary described in paragraph (1) an individualized notice stating the estimated value, election options, fees, effect on remaining benefits, review rights, and election deadline. If the tax conversion date is delayed in a manner that materially changes the estimate, the Secretary shall give a supplemental notice and allow at least 180 days to revise an unexpired election.

(3) **Post-conversion events.**—No new disability or survivor benefit accrues under title II of the Social Security Act based on a disability or death occurring after the tax conversion date. The expected value of such a contingency included under subsection (b)(4) is discharged only through a bond, annuity, or structured settlement under this section and does not create a later direct benefit under that title. Not later than 1 year before the earliest candidate tax conversion date, and again before each later candidate date after a failed candidate, the Secretary shall provide each individual with wages, self-employment income, quarters of coverage, or other service credited under that title a prominent notice of the possible termination of Federal coverage and the objective conversion condition. Nothing in this paragraph limits a private contract or the authority of a State to provide disability or survivor benefits.

**(f) Payroll and self-employment taxes.**—

(1) **Termination.**—Sections 3101, 3111, and 1401 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 are repealed on the tax conversion date, subject to section 301.

(2) **Pre-conversion liabilities.**—Tax imposed with respect to wages paid or self-employment income earned before the tax conversion date remains payable and is not refundable merely because of the repeal under paragraph (1). No tax shall be imposed under a provision repealed by paragraph (1) with respect to wages paid or self-employment income earned on or after the tax conversion date.

(3) **Coordination.**—The Secretary of the Treasury shall prescribe rules for split taxable periods, corrected returns, and the final allocation of receipts to the Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund, the Federal Disability Insurance Trust Fund, and the Federal Hospital Insurance Trust Fund.

**(g) Regulations and implementation.**—

(1) **In general.**—Not later than 18 months after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary of the Treasury, in consultation with the Commissioner of Social Security and the Chief Actuary of the Social Security Administration, shall issue regulations to implement this section, including regulations specifying—

(A) the actuarial assumptions and methods to be used in valuing protected legacy benefits;

(B) procedures for elections and transfers to private retirement accounts;

(C) rules for designation of beneficiaries and spousal consent;

(D) safeguards to prevent fraud and abuse;

(E) fiduciary, fee, bonding, disclosure, and cybersecurity standards for qualified custodians; and

(F) procedures for correcting an earnings record before an election becomes irrevocable.

(2) **Rulemaking process.**—Regulations under paragraph (1) shall be issued after notice and an opportunity for public comment under section 553 of title 5, United States Code. Interim guidance may address an urgent administrative matter but expires not later than 180 days after issuance unless replaced by a final rule.

(3) **Individual statements.**—Before an election period opens, the Secretary shall provide a reasonably accessible administrative process through which an individual may inspect and correct the earnings, family-status, and benefit records used to calculate the individual's protected legacy benefit.

**(h) Funding and fiscal disclosure.**—

(1) **Sources.**—Protected legacy benefits shall be paid first from the applicable Social Security trust fund and then from the general fund of the Treasury. Amounts in the applicable trust fund and, to the extent those amounts are insufficient, amounts from the general fund of the Treasury necessary to pay protected legacy benefits and redeem Treasury transition bonds are permanently appropriated for those purposes.

(2) **No off-budget treatment of transition debt.**—A Treasury transition bond and any other obligation issued under this section shall be included in measures of gross Federal debt and debt subject to limit under chapter 31 of title 31, United States Code, to the same extent as a comparable Treasury obligation.

(3) **Annual report.**—The Secretary of the Treasury and the Chief Actuary shall report annually to Congress the number and value of elections, projected cash payments, bonds issued and redeemed, administrative costs, uncertainty ranges, and the 10-year and 75-year effects of this section on Federal deficits and debt. The report shall separately state the actuarial value and annual cash-payment schedule of additional credited service and protected claims that accrue because one or more candidate tax conversion dates fail section 302(m). Issuance of a noncash transition bond shall be reported as debt rather than a current cash outlay; each redemption, acceleration, interest payment, annuity, and direct benefit shall be reported as an actual or projected cash outlay under sections 204, 302(c), and 302(m). A reduction from current-law benefits may not be described as budgetary savings without displaying those replacement obligations, administration, and uncertainty.

(4) **Trust-fund succession and closing.**—On abolition of the Social Security Administration, the Secretary of the Treasury shall serve as managing trustee and successor fiduciary for the Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund and the Federal Disability Insurance Trust Fund solely for obligations preserved by this section. The Secretary shall maintain separate accounts, obtain an annual independent audit, and, after the last preserved obligation is discharged, publish a final accounting, transfer any unobligated residual balance to the general fund of the Treasury, and transfer records to the National Archives and Records Administration or another lawful custodian under applicable privacy, access, and records-retention law.

(5) **Debt-limit coordination.**—A Treasury transition bond is included in gross Federal debt and debt subject to limit under chapter 31 of title 31, United States Code. For purposes of section 3101(b) of that title, the dollar amount of the limitation otherwise applicable is increased by the outstanding face amount of each bond under this section, including an indexed or capitalized amount that has become part of the legally payable face amount, and is reduced when that amount is paid, redeemed, canceled, or otherwise extinguished, but not below the amount that would otherwise apply. An uncapitalized future interest payment does not increase the limit before it becomes debt otherwise subject to chapter 31. The adjustment is self-executing, may be used only for a bond authorized by this section, and does not authorize borrowing for another purpose. The Secretary shall publish a monthly reconciliation of bonds issued, indexed, capitalized, paid, redeemed, canceled, extinguished, and outstanding and of the corresponding adjustment to the limitation.

**(i) Dispute resolution.**—

(1) **Administrative review.**—An individual aggrieved by an earnings-record determination, a valuation of a protected legacy benefit, the denial of an election, or a custodian disqualification may request administrative review not later than 180 days after receiving notice. The reviewing officer shall be different from the officer who made the initial determination and shall issue a reasoned decision on the record.

(2) **Judicial review.**—An appropriate United States district court has exclusive jurisdiction to review a final benefit, earnings-record, valuation, election, or custodian-qualification determination under paragraph (1), without regard to the amount of any payment incident to the determination, and may award declaratory, injunctive, and legally due monetary relief. The United States Court of Federal Claims has jurisdiction only over a standalone claim on a Treasury transition bond or an account contract that does not require review of such a determination. The United States waives sovereign immunity solely for relief expressly authorized by this paragraph.

**(j) Transfer of administration.**—Not later than 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary of the Treasury shall establish an Office of Legacy Social Insurance within the Department of the Treasury and transfer to that Office the personnel, records, systems, appropriations, and actuarial functions necessary to administer this section. After the transfer, a reference in this section to the Chief Actuary of the Social Security Administration means the chief actuarial officer for legacy social insurance in that Office. The Social Security Administration shall be abolished in accordance with section 101(d). The Office may administer title II of the Social Security Act before the tax conversion date and the obligations and accounts under this section thereafter, but may not create a benefit not authorized by the law then in force and may not disclose or use an individual record for VAT enforcement, banking supervision, or another unrelated function. The Office terminates after the final preserved obligation is discharged and its final accounting and records are transferred under subsection (h)(4).

### SEC. 202. IMPLEMENTATION OF AGENCY ABOLITIONS

**(a) Scope.**—This section applies to an agency, department, bureau, office, commission, board, administration, or other entity abolished under section 101 or another provision of this Act and governs personnel, records, proceedings, contracts, and implementation matters not otherwise addressed.

**(b) Personnel.**—Personnel of an entity described in subsection (a) may be reassigned to a remaining department or agency at the discretion of the President, subject to otherwise applicable law.

**(c) Records.**—To the extent not otherwise provided in this Act, records of an entity described in subsection (a) shall be transferred to a remaining department or agency, the National Archives and Records Administration, or another lawful custodian designated by the President.

**(d) Pending proceedings.**—Subject to sections 107 and 603, an administrative or judicial proceeding commenced by or against an entity described in subsection (a) before the effective date of abolition shall not abate by reason of the abolition, but shall continue with the United States or a successor department or agency designated by the President substituted as the party as appropriate, solely to resolve a preserved liability, contract, property right, or transferred function. A proceeding seeking only a prospective permit, approval, subsidy, or regulatory command under authority terminated by this Act shall be dismissed as moot on the termination date, without prejudice to a claim for legally required compensation or review of pre-effective-date conduct.

**(e) Contracts and obligations.**—Valid contracts, grants, loans, and other binding obligations of an entity described in subsection (a) that are in effect on the effective date of abolition shall remain in force according to their terms, subject to any express repeal or limitation in this Act and to appropriations Acts, and shall be administered by the successor department or agency designated by the President or, if none, by the Department of the Treasury.

**(f) Implementation guidance.**—Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment of this Act, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget, in consultation with the Director of the Office of Personnel Management and the heads of affected departments and agencies, shall issue guidance to implement sections 101 and 202, including guidance on workforce transition, records management, and the orderly wind-down of programs, consistent with the repeals and abolitions in this Act.

**(g) Act-wide no-orphan rule.**—Unless another provision of this Act expressly supplies a different disposition, subsections (e) through (i) of section 101 apply to every entity abolished by this Act as though that entity were listed in section 101. A preexisting Federal preemption provision tied solely to a prospective Federal requirement or approval that terminates under this Act has no effect with respect to conduct occurring after the termination date, except to the extent necessary to preserve a valid contract or property right, a pre-effective-date liability, or a generally applicable criminal prohibition expressly preserved by this Act.

(9) Section 17 of the Child Nutrition Act of 1966 (42 U.S.C. 1786) is repealed.\
(10) The Richard B. Russell National School Lunch Act (42 U.S.C. 1751 et seq.) and section 4 of the Child Nutrition Act of 1966 (42 U.S.C. 1773) are repealed.\
(11) Section 36B of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 is repealed for taxable years beginning on or after the later of the welfare transition date or the tax conversion date, and section 1402 of the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (42 U.S.C. 18071) is repealed on that later date.\
(12) The United States Housing Act of 1937 (42 U.S.C. 1437 et seq.) is repealed, except that a lease, housing-assistance-payment contract, debt, or other commitment legally obligated before the welfare transition date remains governed by sections 202 and 603.

**(b) Temporary transition allotments.**—For purposes of this section, the first transition year is the 12-month period beginning on the welfare transition date, and each of the next 4 transition years is the succeeding 12-month period. At the election of a State, the Secretary of the Treasury shall make a declining allotment based on Federal outlays attributable to residents of that State under the programs affected by subsection (a) during the last full Federal fiscal year before the welfare transition date. The allotment equals 50 percent of that annual base for the first transition year, 40 percent for the second, 30 percent for the third, 20 percent for the fourth, and 10 percent for the fifth. A State may use an allotment for temporary cash, food, medical, housing, education, disability, or administrative transition assistance furnished by the State, a political subdivision, or a private charity selected under State law.

**(c) Notice, election, payment, and records.**—

(1) Not later than 1 year before the welfare transition date, the Secretary shall give each State a preliminary machine-readable calculation of its annual base, underlying program and residency data, and methodology. A State may seek correction within 90 days, and a reviewing officer appointed under section 601(d) shall issue a reasoned final calculation not later than 240 days before the welfare transition date.

(2) A State may elect the allotment for a transition year by notice given not later than 120 days before that year begins. The Secretary shall pay an elected allotment in equal quarterly installments in advance, subject to later reconciliation for a corrected base, fraud, or use outside subsection (b). A delayed Federal installment accrues interest after its due date at the Federal short-term rate determined under section 1274(d) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as then applicable or preserved, plus 3 percentage points.

(3) At an electing State's request, the Federal department, agency, or transition office then administering the affected program under sections 101 and 202 shall transfer the minimum beneficiary and payment records reasonably necessary for continuity and prevention of duplicate payment. The transfer requires a written data-security, purpose-limitation, retention, breach-notification, and destruction agreement; advance notice to affected individuals; and compliance with otherwise applicable privacy law. A State may decline the records without forfeiting the allotment.

**(d) Conditions and audit.**—A State accepting an allotment shall use it only for the purposes in subsection (b), keep records sufficient for an audit, and return an amount used for fraud or for a purpose outside that subsection. No other Federal policy condition applies. The Comptroller General may audit an allotment, but no Federal officer may direct the eligibility rules or benefit design chosen by a State.

**(e) Appropriation and termination.**—There are appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury such sums as are necessary for allotments calculated under subsection (b). No amount may be obligated for a period after the fifth transition year, and this section creates no entitlement to assistance for any individual.

### SEC. 204. CASH-FUNDING REQUIREMENT AND DEFICIT CONTROL

**(a) Budget requirements and complete accounting.**—

(1) A budget submitted by the President under section 1105 of title 31, United States Code, shall propose, for each fiscal year beginning on or after the tax conversion date, recurring total receipts at least equal to total cash outlays. For an earlier fiscal year, the budget shall propose adjusted outlays that do not exceed recurring total receipts.

(2) "Total cash outlays" means every budgetary cash outlay expected during the fiscal year and each cash payment required by this Act that otherwise would be classified as a financing transaction. It includes net interest; protected legacy benefits and vouchers under sections 201 and 205; redemption, acceleration, and interest on Treasury transition bonds; transition allotments and grants; defense and veterans' outlays; refunds and refund interest; judgments and compensation; insurance and receivership backstops; agency wind-down and liquidation costs; court operations and territorial-court conversion; and tax administration. Issuance of a noncash transition bond is not itself a cash outlay, but every scheduled cash payment on the bond is. Issuance or redemption of an ordinary Treasury security solely to refinance outstanding public debt within the limit under chapter 31 of title 31, United States Code, is a financing transaction excluded from total cash outlays; interest and a principal payment that this Act or another Act expressly requires to be made without refinancing remain included. An exclusion from sequestration does not remove an amount otherwise within this paragraph.

(3) "Recurring total receipts" means gross VAT collections before payment of VAT refunds and other gross receipts expected to recur under law during the fiscal year. A refund and refund interest remain cash outlays under paragraph (2) and are not also deducted from receipts. Recurring total receipts exclude borrowing, issuance of an obligation, an intragovernmental transfer, an asset sale, liquidation proceeds, a temporary recovery, and another one-time receipt. An excluded receipt may reduce debt or satisfy a transition cost but may not be used to certify recurring cash balance.

(4) "Adjusted outlays" means total outlays reduced by net interest, protected legacy benefits under sections 201 and 205, and an emergency requirement or expenditure for a war declared by Congress that an Act of Congress expressly designates for purposes of this section. Adjusted outlays are used only for pre-conversion budget enforcement and sequestration allocation; they are not the cash-funding target under paragraph (1) or section 302(c).

(5) For the fiscal year containing the tax conversion date, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall maintain a monthly bridge that allocates receipts, refunds, and outlays to the pre-conversion portion ending the day before that date and the post-conversion portion beginning on that date. Paragraph (1)'s pre-conversion rule and subsection (c)(1) apply only to the first portion, and paragraph (1)'s post-conversion cash rule and subsection (c)(2) apply only to the second. No receipt, refund, outlay, reserve, or reduction may be omitted or counted in both portions.

**(b) Estimates and scorekeeping.**—For purposes of this section, the "budget year" is the fiscal year addressed by the applicable budget, estimate, report, or order. The duties in this subsection apply separately to each fiscal year, and submission of a budget for a later fiscal year does not end scorekeeping or supplemental enforcement for the fiscal year then in progress.

(1) Not later than 30 days after submission of the President's budget, the Director of the Congressional Budget Office shall publish an advisory estimate of recurring total receipts, total cash outlays, the cash surplus or shortfall, adjusted outlays, and the adjusted deficit or surplus for the budget year. Not later than 15 days thereafter, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall publish an initial report containing the same categories, displaying the Congressional Budget Office estimate, explaining each material difference, identifying amounts exempt under subsection (d), and reconciling the estimate with the cash target and rate calculation under section 302(c).

(2) Not later than 10 days after enactment of an appropriation, revenue, or direct-spending law that changes projected receipts or outlays for a budget year by at least $1,000,000,000, or causes the aggregate unreported change since the last update to reach that amount, each Director shall publish an updated estimate. The Office of Management and Budget shall maintain a public scorecard of the cumulative effect of all such laws, including changes below the reporting threshold.

(3) The Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall issue an operative report for the budget year not later than 15 days after the earlier of the end of the session of Congress or enactment of an Act that expressly states that it completes regular appropriations for that budget year. An appropriation is not deemed the last regular appropriation by implication. The Director shall issue a supplemental operative report after a later law described in paragraph (2). Each operative report shall calculate any reserve or uniform sequestration percentage under subsection (c).

(4) To preserve the ability to execute subsection (c), the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall apportion each nonexempt account so that up to 15 percent of its budgetary resources for the budget year remains unobligated until the first operative report and any resulting order. If the report shows no applicable deficit or cash shortfall, the reserve shall be released immediately. A comparable reserve for a later appropriation may be held for not more than 30 days pending a supplemental report and order.

**(c) Automatic deficit control.**—

(1) For a fiscal year or portion before the tax conversion date, if an operative report projects adjusted outlays greater than recurring total receipts, the President shall, not later than 15 days after the report, order a uniform sequestration of each nonexempt discretionary account sufficient to reduce the adjusted deficit, except that the cumulative reduction may not exceed 10 percent for that fiscal year. The President shall submit specific rescission or direct-spending recommendations for a remainder, but the submission is not a condition of any transition under this Act.

(2) For a fiscal year or portion beginning on or after the tax conversion date, the rate calculation in section 302(c) is the first cash-funding control. If an operative report nevertheless projects recurring total receipts below total cash outlays, or if the ceiling shortfall calculated under section 302(c)(5) is positive, the President shall order a uniform sequestration percentage sufficient to eliminate the projected residual shortfall from every nonexempt discretionary and direct-spending account. Use of a fallback rate without a positive ceiling shortfall does not by itself trigger this paragraph. The post-conversion percentage is not limited to 10 percent but may not exceed 100 percent of the budgetary resources otherwise available in an account.

(3) A reduction under this subsection shall be calculated and implemented using sections 251, 254, and 256 of the Balanced Budget and Emergency Deficit Control Act of 1985 (2 U.S.C. 901, 904, and 906), to the extent consistent with this section. The calculation shall account for an earlier order for the same fiscal year and may not restore an earlier reduction unless an Act of Congress expressly provides the restoration and its cash funding.

(4) If sequestration of all nonexempt resources is insufficient to eliminate a post-conversion residual shortfall, the Secretary shall enter the unpaid amount in a public Residual Cash Shortfall Ledger. The entry shall identify the protected obligations producing it, the rate that the uncapped formula in section 302(c) would have produced, and each later payment or reserve application. The ledger balance is reduced when the shortfall is paid from then-available receipts, the fiscal-continuity reserve, or borrowing within the debt limit. Only the unpaid ledger balance that is not already included in projected total cash outlays may be added to a later cash-funding target. This paragraph creates no authority to delay a payment lawfully due, reduce a protected obligation, or borrow above that limit.

**(d) Exempt accounts.**—A sequestration under this section may not reduce budgetary resources for interest or principal on an obligation of the United States; a protected legacy benefit, voucher, or Treasury transition bond under section 201 or 205; a VAT or pre-conversion tax refund and interest lawfully payable on that refund; a final judgment or constitutionally required compensation; the minimum constitutional operations of the legislative and judicial branches; or an amount expressly exempted by a subsequently enacted law that identifies both the amount and its cash funding. Each exempt amount that is within subsection (a)(2) remains in total cash outlays and in the rate calculation under section 302(c). Ordinary refinancing principal excluded by subsection (a)(2) does not become an outlay solely because it is exempt from sequestration.

**(e) No effect on later law.**—Nothing in this section limits the constitutional authority of Congress to enact a later appropriation or other law. An emergency or declared-war designation affects adjusted outlays only if an Act of Congress expressly identifies the amount and states that the designation is made for purposes of this section. The designated amount remains part of total cash outlays and operative cash outlays; the designation itself neither changes a statutory date nor creates an exclusion from section 302(m), although the actual cash disbursement may affect whether a candidate date satisfies that objective test.

**(f) Applicability and duration.**—Subsections (a) through (e) first apply to the first fiscal year for which the President submits a budget after the date of enactment and apply to each later budget year unless amended or repealed by an Act of Congress.

### SEC. 205. PRIVATIZATION OF MEDICARE

**(a) Prospective repeal.**—Title XVIII of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 1395 et seq.) is repealed effective on the later of the tax conversion date and the date that is 7 years after the date of enactment of this Act, except that provisions of that title shall remain in force solely to the extent incorporated by this section for the coverage, payment, review, and enforcement of a protected legacy Medicare benefit. If no tax conversion date has occurred, the 7-year date does not independently trigger repeal. Provisions establishing and operating the Federal Hospital Insurance Trust Fund and the Federal Supplementary Medical Insurance Trust Fund remain in force solely as accounting and payment authorities until all obligations preserved by this section are discharged.

**(b) Protected legacy Medicare benefits.**—

(1) **Protected enrollee.**—The term "protected enrollee" means an individual who—

(A) is entitled to or enrolled in benefits under title XVIII of the Social Security Act on the date of enactment of this Act; or

(B) first becomes entitled to benefits under that title before the later of the tax conversion date or the date that is 3 years after the date of enactment.

(2) **Continued coverage.**—A protected enrollee who does not make an election under subsection (c) shall remain eligible, for each period in which the individual satisfies the eligibility rules incorporated from title XVIII, for the items and services that would have been covered under parts A, B, and D of that title as in effect on the day before the date of enactment. A Medicare Advantage contract in force for a protected enrollee remains governed by its terms and the transition rules prescribed under this section.

(3) **No new entitlement after cutoff.**—An individual who is not a protected enrollee may not first become entitled to a benefit under title XVIII on or after the date specified in paragraph (1)(B).

(4) **Annual actuarial value.**—The "annual actuarial value" of a protected enrollee's benefit is the expected Federal cost for the next coverage year of furnishing the coverage described in paragraph (2), adjusted for age, disability, end-stage renal disease, geographic health-care costs, and clinically validated risk factors. It does not include an amount for services already furnished or a duplicative amount payable under other coverage.

(5) **Coverage year.**—The term "coverage year" means a calendar year, except that the Secretary may use the contract year otherwise applicable to a preserved Medicare Advantage arrangement if the standardized disclosure clearly states the different period and prevents duplicate payment.

**(c) Transition to private health accounts and vouchers.**—

(1) **Annual voucher election and notice.**—After the methodology under paragraph (2) and final regulations under subsection (e) have been in effect for at least 90 days, a protected enrollee may elect to receive, for each coverage year, a risk-adjusted voucher equal to the annual actuarial value determined under subsection (b)(4). The voucher shall be paid monthly to a portable health account, qualified health insurer, employer-sponsored plan, or other qualified health arrangement selected by the enrollee. An election is voluntary and may be changed during an annual open-enrollment period, subject to the reentry and balance rules in paragraph (9). Not later than 1 year before the earliest candidate tax conversion date and, after each failed candidate under section 302(m), not later than 1 year before the next candidate date, the Secretary shall notify each person who is or may become a protected enrollee of the applicable cutoff, coverage, voucher election, enrollment period, and review rights. A person first protected because of a failed candidate shall have at least 180 days after notice to make an initial election.

(2) **Calculation and review.**—Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment, the Chief Actuary of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services shall publish a proposed methodology for calculating annual actuarial value. After considering that proposal and an advisory review by the Comptroller General, the Secretary of Health and Human Services shall establish and publish the final methodology. If the Comptroller General does not submit a review within 60 days after receiving a complete proposal, the Secretary may proceed and shall explain any material departure from the Chief Actuary's proposal. The methodology and each material revision shall identify uncertainty ranges and prevent an insurer from increasing a score through unsupported diagnosis coding.

(3) **Permitted uses.**—Voucher amounts may be used only for premiums for private health coverage, qualified long-term-care coverage, or qualified health-care expenses defined by the Secretary of the Treasury. Amounts may pay costs under employer-sponsored or State-regulated coverage and may be carried forward for later qualified expenses.

(4) **Account protections.**—A portable health account shall be held in trust, segregated from the custodian's assets, portable between qualified custodians, protected from the custodian's creditors, and subject to fiduciary, fee-disclosure, bonding, and cybersecurity standards. On the enrollee's death, a balance attributable to private contributions and earnings on those contributions passes under the account agreement or applicable State law. A balance attributable to Federal vouchers is governed by paragraph (9).

(5) **No lump-sum commutation.**—The Secretary may not commute an enrollee's projected lifetime health costs into a single lump-sum payment. Nothing in this paragraph prohibits the carryforward of an unused annual voucher under paragraph (3).

(6) **Choice and disclosure.**—Before each election, the enrollee shall receive a standardized comparison of expected out-of-pocket costs, coverage limits, provider access, insolvency protections, fees, and the consequences of the election. A qualified health insurer or custodian may not use a waiver that disclaims fraud, fiduciary duty, or the coverage expressly promised in its contract.

(7) **Disabled and end-stage renal disease enrollees.**—A protected enrollee entitled by reason of disability or end-stage renal disease is eligible on the same terms as any other protected enrollee, with the risk adjustment required by subsection (b)(4).

(8) **Qualified coverage arrangements.**—A private insurer or other health arrangement may receive a voucher only if it is lawfully authorized under applicable State law or is an employer-sponsored plan governed by applicable Federal law and contractually agrees—

(A) to accept a protected enrollee who timely elects the arrangement, subject to a disclosed premium and service area;

(B) not to rescind promised coverage after a claim except for intentional material fraud established through reviewable procedures;

(C) to maintain reserves, reinsurance, bonding, or other insolvency protection reasonably sufficient for the promised coverage; and

(D) to provide a binding internal claims process and access to any external or judicial remedy available under the governing contract and applicable law.

These conditions govern eligibility to receive Federal voucher payments and do not preempt a more protective State-law requirement.

For purposes of this section, a "qualified health insurer" or "qualified health arrangement" is an insurer, employer-sponsored plan, or other arrangement that satisfies this paragraph and the fiduciary and disclosure requirements applicable under this section.

(9) **Reentry and public balances.**—An enrollee who returns from a voucher arrangement to coverage under subsection (b)(2) shall first apply any account balance attributable to Federal vouchers to the cost of that coverage. A Federal voucher balance remaining on the death of the enrollee and any surviving spouse reverts to the Treasury; only private contributions and earnings attributable to them pass without the Federal use restriction. The Secretary shall prescribe tracing rules and may not permit annual switching to produce duplicative Federal payment or one-way selection against traditional coverage.

**(d) Federal tax and misuse treatment of transition accounts.**—

(1) For a taxable period ending before the tax conversion date, a voucher or other contribution made under this section, earnings in the account, and a distribution for a qualified health-care expense are not includible in gross income under the Internal Revenue Code of 1986. A distribution for another purpose is includible in gross income to the extent provided under that Code.

(2) A knowing use of a voucher amount for a purpose not permitted by subsection (c)(3) is recoverable as a debt owed to the United States, with interest, after notice and an opportunity for administrative review. No regulation may use a tax repealed by section 301 as a penalty for post-conversion conduct.

**(e) Regulations and implementation.**—

(1) **In general.**—Not later than 18 months after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary of Health and Human Services, in consultation with the Secretary of the Treasury and the Chief Actuary of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, shall issue regulations specifying—

(A) the actuarial assumptions and methods used to calculate annual actuarial value;

(B) procedures for elections and issuance of vouchers;

(C) rules for the establishment and administration of arrangements described in subsection (c)(1); and

(D) safeguards against fraud, unsupported risk coding, insolvency, excessive fees, and misuse of account assets.

(2) **Rulemaking process.**—Regulations under paragraph (1) shall be issued after notice and an opportunity for public comment under section 553 of title 5, United States Code. Interim guidance expires not later than 180 days after issuance unless replaced by a final rule.

(3) **Transfer of functions.**—Not later than 18 months after the date of enactment, the Secretary of the Treasury shall establish an Office of Legacy Health Benefits within the Department of the Treasury. Before the abolition of the Department of Health and Human Services under section 101(b)(6), the President shall transfer to that Office the personnel, records, claims systems, contracts, appropriations, and actuarial functions necessary to administer this section. After the transfer, a reference in this section to the Secretary of Health and Human Services or the Chief Actuary of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services means, respectively, the Secretary of the Treasury or the chief actuarial officer for legacy health benefits in that Office. The Office may contract with private claims administrators but remains responsible for lawful payment and review. It may not disclose or use an individual health record for VAT enforcement, banking supervision, or another unrelated function and terminates after the last preserved obligation and Federal voucher balance are discharged and its final accounting and records are transferred under subsection (f)(3).

**(f) Funding and fiscal disclosure.**—

(1) **Sources.**—Protected legacy Medicare benefits and annual vouchers shall be paid first from the applicable Medicare trust fund and then from the general fund of the Treasury. Amounts in the applicable trust fund and, to the extent those amounts are insufficient, amounts from the general fund of the Treasury necessary to make those payments are permanently appropriated for those purposes.

(2) **Annual report.**—The Secretary of the Treasury and the Chief Actuary shall report annually to Congress on enrollment by option, voucher amounts, traditional-coverage costs, insurer and custodian failures, administrative costs, and the 10-year and 75-year effects of this section on Federal deficits and debt. The report shall separately state the actuarial value and annual cash-payment schedule of each additional protected enrollee, coverage period, or voucher obligation attributable to a failed candidate tax conversion date under section 302(m). Every protected benefit, voucher, traditional-coverage payment, and administrative payment is an actual or projected cash outlay under sections 204, 302(c), and 302(m). Savings may not be reported without separately reporting transition costs, additional delayed-conversion obligations, and uncertainty ranges.

(3) **Trust-fund succession and closing.**—On abolition of the Department of Health and Human Services, the Secretary of the Treasury shall serve as managing trustee and successor fiduciary for the Federal Hospital Insurance Trust Fund and the Federal Supplementary Medical Insurance Trust Fund solely for obligations preserved by this section. The Secretary shall maintain separate accounts, obtain an annual independent audit, and, after the last preserved obligation is discharged, publish a final accounting, transfer any unobligated residual balance to the general fund of the Treasury, and transfer records to the National Archives and Records Administration or another lawful custodian under applicable health-privacy, access, and records-retention law.

**(g) Dispute resolution.**—

(1) **Administrative review.**—A protected enrollee aggrieved by a coverage, payment, annual-valuation, account, or election determination may request administrative review not later than 180 days after notice. The reviewing officer shall be different from the officer who made the initial determination and shall issue a reasoned decision on the record.

(2) **Judicial review.**—An appropriate United States district court has exclusive jurisdiction to review a final coverage, payment, annual-valuation, account, or election determination, without regard to the amount of any payment incident to the determination, and may award declaratory, injunctive, and legally due monetary relief. The United States Court of Federal Claims has jurisdiction only over a standalone claim on an account contract that does not require review of such a determination. The United States waives sovereign immunity solely for relief expressly authorized by this paragraph.

**(h) Coordination with tax reform.**—The fiscal report required by subsection (f)(2) shall be included in the cash-funding reports and automatic rate calculation under section 302(c). No officer may characterize a projected reduction in Medicare outlays as available to finance a tax reduction without also accounting for the annual vouchers and protected legacy benefits required by this section.

### SEC. 206. DIRECT REDUCTION OF DEFENSE SPENDING AND AUDITS

**(a) Direct reduction of new defense spending.**—

(1) For the first fiscal year beginning after the date of enactment, aggregate covered defense budget authority may not exceed $650,000,000,000.

(2) For the second fiscal year beginning after the date of enactment and each fiscal year thereafter, aggregate covered defense budget authority may not exceed $500,000,000,000, multiplied by the ratio of the average Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers for the 12 months ending with June immediately before that fiscal year to the average of that index for the 12 months ending with June 2026, and rounded downward to the nearest $100,000,000. A decline in the index reduces the amount, and no officer may substitute a defense-cost index, GDP measure, requested force structure, or other escalator.

(3) "Covered defense budget authority" means all discretionary and mandatory new budget authority, advance budget authority, obligation limitation, transfer authority, and other authority to incur a new obligation classified in national defense budget function 050, without regard to the department or account receiving it. "New budget authority" has the meaning given in section 3 of the Congressional Budget Act of 1974 (2 U.S.C. 622), and an obligation limitation or comparable authority is counted at the maximum amount that may be obligated. Classification follows the functional scorekeeping conventions used for the President's budget; changing an account label, receiving department, funding vehicle, or stated secondary purpose does not remove authority whose primary purpose is national defense. An amount transferred from another budget function is covered if used for a function 050 purpose. The term excludes only authority necessary to pay compensation already earned; a vested military or civilian retirement, survivor, or retiree-health obligation; a final judgment; or a contract or other obligation legally incurred before the date of enactment. An excluded payment remains a Federal outlay under sections 204 and 302 and may not support a new or enlarged commitment.

**(b) Immediate rescission and transition.**—

(1) On the date of enactment, each unobligated balance of budget authority for function 050 is rescinded to the extent it is not necessary to liquidate an obligation legally incurred before that date, pay an amount excluded by subsection (a)(3), protect classified material or nuclear weapons and material during safe disposition, or complete a termination or settlement that reduces future Federal cost. The rescission occurs by operation of law. Not later than 30 days after enactment, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall publish a machine-readable account-by-account statement of the amount rescinded and the legal basis for each retained balance. An omission from the statement does not preserve an amount rescinded by this paragraph.

(2) Not later than 120 days after enactment, the President shall publish and transmit to Congress an execution plan that reduces force structure, civilian and contractor staffing, overseas presence, procurement, research, construction, and operations as necessary to remain within subsection (a). The plan shall identify each program termination, contract cost, personnel action, asset disposition, base or facility action permitted by existing law, and 10-year outlay effect. It shall protect safe custody and disposition of nuclear, chemical, biological, radiological, explosive, classified, and hazardous property and may not count an unpaid valid obligation or an asset-sale receipt as spending reduction.

(3) A plan, report, procurement action, personnel action, or failure to complete an administrative step is not a condition of the reduction, rescission, or cancellation required by this section.

**(c) Congressional war adjustment.**—The amount in subsection (a) may be exceeded only by an Act of Congress enacted after the date of enactment of this Act that declares war or specifically responds to an armed attack on the United States, expressly identifies the additional dollar amount, fiscal year, military purpose, and source of funding, and states that the adjustment is made for purposes of this section. A committee report, emergency designation, transfer, reprogramming, or executive finding does not satisfy this subsection.

**(d) Automatic execution.**—

(1) For the first fiscal year subject to subsection (a), the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall publish the initial calculation not later than 30 days after enactment if the President's budget for that fiscal year was submitted before enactment, and otherwise not later than 15 days after that budget is submitted. For each later fiscal year, the Director shall publish the initial calculation not later than 15 days after the President submits the budget. Not later than 10 days after enactment of an Act providing covered defense budget authority, the Director shall update the cumulative amount for the fiscal year, the statutory amount under subsection (a), each adjustment under subsection (c), and the amount, if any, above the permitted total.

(2) Covered defense budget authority above the permitted total is unavailable and canceled by operation of law. The Director shall allocate the cancellation pro rata among nonexcluded accounts containing covered defense budget authority before apportionment and shall publish the ministerial calculation. Sections 254 and 256 of the Balanced Budget and Emergency Deficit Control Act of 1985 apply only to the extent necessary to execute that calculation and consistent with this section. Failure to publish does not delay or reduce the cancellation.

(3) As a rule of construction, an Act enacted after this Act is not construed to exempt or add an amount unless it satisfies subsection (c) and expressly identifies the amount, fiscal year, and source of funding, or unless the later Act is irreconcilable with this section to that extent. A general appropriation, transfer authority, emergency or contingency designation, or boilerplate statement that another law applies notwithstanding inconsistent law does not by itself establish that irreconcilable conflict. This paragraph does not bind a later Congress.

(4) Outlays from an obligation legally incurred before the applicable fiscal year remain payable according to law and shall be displayed separately from new budget authority. Such outlays do not enlarge the amount in subsection (a), are included in every calculation under sections 204, 302(c), and 302(m), and may not be described as eliminated before they cease.

(5) A budgetary resource canceled or rescinded under this section may not be canceled a second time under section 204(c). Coordination prevents duplicate cancellation but does not remove an actual outlay, cash shortfall, or independent cash-funding requirement from section 204 or 302.

**(e) Audit and remediation.**—

(1) The Department of Defense shall continue to prepare and submit annual financial statements for audit under chapter 35 of title 31, United States Code, and other applicable law. The Inspector General of the Department of Defense shall publish each component opinion, material weakness, and corrective-action status in a machine-readable format.

(2) Not later than 90 days after an auditor identifies a material weakness, the responsible component shall publish a corrective-action plan identifying the accountable official, milestones, resources, and target completion date. The Comptroller General shall report annually to Congress on whether those plans are supported by reliable evidence and are sufficient to meet the deadline of December 31, 2028, established by section 1005 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024 (Public Law 118–31; 10 U.S.C. 240a note), or a later deadline expressly enacted by Congress.

(3) An audit or review under this subsection is limited to Federal entities, funds, property, and contractors and may not require a State to administer a Federal audit program.

**(f) Effective dates.**—Subsections (b), (d), and (e) take effect on enactment. Subsection (a)(1) applies to the first fiscal year beginning after enactment, and subsection (a)(2) applies as stated therein.

### SEC. 207. PRIVATIZATION OF FEDERAL COMMERCIAL AND GOVERNMENT-SPONSORED ENTERPRISES

**(a) Definitions.**—In this section:

(1) The term "covered Federal enterprise" means an entity that engages primarily in commercial or financial activity and is owned or controlled by the United States, or that was established by Federal law as a government-sponsored enterprise with a special financing, collateral, tax, or regulatory privilege, including—

(A) any wholly owned Government corporation or mixed-ownership Government corporation (as those terms are defined in section 9101 of title 31, United States Code);\
(B) the United States Postal Service (39 U.S.C. 201);\
(C) the National Railroad Passenger Corporation (Amtrak) (49 U.S.C. 24301);\
(D) the Federal National Mortgage Association and the Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation (12 U.S.C. 1716 et seq. and 1451 et seq., respectively), including while in conservatorship or receivership; and\
(E) each Federal Home Loan Bank and the Office of Finance of the Federal Home Loan Bank System (12 U.S.C. 1421 et seq.).

(2) The term does not include an entity whose principal functions relate exclusively to national defense, intelligence, or foreign affairs.

(3) The term does not include a Federal trust fund, a Federal Reserve entity governed by section 401, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation or National Credit Union Administration acting as insurer or receiver, or an entity solely to the extent it administers a debt, guarantee, insurance claim, pension, or protected legacy benefit that remains an obligation of the United States.

**(b) In general.**—Not later than 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary of the Treasury shall commence, and not later than 5 years after that date shall complete, the privatization of each covered Federal enterprise through competitive sale, conversion to an entity organized solely under generally applicable State law, transfer, orderly liquidation, or another disposition that—

(1) maximizes net proceeds to the Treasury through a transparent and competitive process, subject to paragraphs (2) through (5);\
(2) does not confer any exclusive Federal franchise, monopoly, or regulatory privilege on the purchaser or any successor entity;\
(3) identifies and provides for each valid debt, bond, pension, collective-bargaining agreement, executory contract, pending claim, and other obligation before residual proceeds are transferred to the general fund;\
(4) does not transfer classified material, a sovereign power, or property required for national defense; and\
(5) does not create or materially increase market power in a relevant national or regional market; the Secretary shall divide assets, stagger dispositions, or disqualify a bidder when reasonably necessary to satisfy this paragraph and shall publish the beneficial owners and controlling interests of each qualified bidder.

**(c) Termination of postal monopoly.**—Sections 1693 through 1697 of title 18, United States Code, and sections 601 through 606 of title 39, United States Code (commonly referred to as the "Private Express Statutes"), are repealed, effective on the date of enactment of this Act.

**(d) Enterprise Transition Fund.**—

(1) The Secretary shall establish in the Treasury an Enterprise Transition Fund. Gross disposition proceeds and cash transferred from a covered Federal enterprise shall be credited to the Fund and are permanently appropriated, without fiscal year limitation, solely for transaction costs and the satisfaction or lawful assumption of obligations identified under subsection (b)(3). The Secretary shall maintain a separate ledger for each enterprise and may not conceal an entity-specific shortfall through netting.

(2) Before a disposition closes, the Secretary shall publish an independently reviewed entity-by-entity estimate of gross proceeds, costs, liabilities by lawful priority, contingent claims, and any shortfall, and shall identify the budget authority or binding non-Federal assumption that covers each amount. A transaction may not close by transferring an unfunded Federal obligation to a purchaser or by treating the obligation as extinguished.

(3) If enterprise assets and proceeds are insufficient, there are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury such sums as are necessary to satisfy a valid obligation for which the United States, the United States Postal Service, a wholly owned Government corporation, or another covered enterprise owned or controlled by the United States is legally liable, but not a debt of a government-sponsored or mixed-ownership enterprise that was not guaranteed by law before enactment. The Secretary shall assign each unresolved funded obligation to a named custodial office with no authority to undertake new commercial activity and shall report each use of this paragraph as a Federal outlay and debt effect.

(4) After all covered dispositions are complete and every obligation is satisfied or fully reserved, the Secretary shall consider a final independent audit, determine and certify the residual balance, publish that determination with reasons, transfer the certified residual balance to the general fund of the Treasury, and close the Fund.

**(e) Identification, valuation, and plan.**—Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary shall publish in the Federal Register a list of covered entities and a plan for each entity that includes independently reviewed valuations, material obligations, the shortfall estimate required by subsection (d)(2), proposed transaction method, competition safeguards, milestones, treatment of essential services, and bidder beneficial-ownership disclosure. The Comptroller General shall submit an advisory review to Congress and the Secretary not later than 90 days after receiving a complete plan. After considering that review, the Secretary shall make and publish the operative finding that the disposition satisfies subsections (b) and (d). A final sale may not close before the later of 60 days after submission of the advisory review or 150 days after the Comptroller General received the complete plan; if no review is submitted by the latter date, the Secretary may proceed after considering an independent public audit.

**(f) No implied guarantee.**—After privatization, no debt or obligation of a successor is guaranteed by the United States unless an Act of Congress expressly identifies the obligation and the maximum amount guaranteed.

**(g) Termination of charter privileges.**—On completion of a disposition under subsection (b), a Federal charter and each special financing, collateral, tax, regulatory, mission, or line-of-credit privilege of the covered Federal enterprise terminate. A provision of Federal law enacted before this Act has no effect after that date to the extent it establishes the enterprise or gives the enterprise or its successor Federal status, a mission mandate, a monopoly, or a special privilege. The disposition shall preserve valid contracts and property interests, including member capital in a Federal Home Loan Bank and privately held equity or debt, according to their lawful priority, but no holder has a right to continuation of a Federal charter or future governmental support. The plan under subsection (e) shall include proposed legislation listing technical repeals and amendments, but an omission from that legislation does not continue a status or privilege terminated by this subsection.

**(h) Housing-finance transition.**—The Federal Housing Finance Agency shall retain only the authority necessary to conserve, value, dispose of, or liquidate the entities described in subsection (a)(1)(D) and (E), protect records, and resolve pre-disposition claims. It may not expand a portfolio, affordable-housing mandate, advance program, guarantee, or Federal credit commitment. After considering an advisory report by the Comptroller General, the Secretary shall determine whether the dispositions and preserved receiverships are complete or whether adequate reserves and a lawful successor custodian exist for each unresolved claim. If the Comptroller General does not report within 90 days after receiving a complete proposed determination, the Secretary may act on an independent public audit. On publication of the Secretary's determination, the Agency is abolished and its remaining records, reserves, and custodial duties transfer to the Treasury.

## TITLE III—TAX SYSTEM REFORM

### SEC. 301. ABOLITION OF ALL EXISTING FEDERAL TAXES

**(a) Enumerated Internal Revenue Code repeals.**—Effective on the tax conversion date, subtitles A, B, C, D, and E of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 are repealed. Subtitle F of that Code and other procedural provisions remain in force to the extent provided in section 303 or necessary to resolve a pre-conversion matter under subsection (c).

**(b) Termination of other Federal taxes.**—Notwithstanding a provision of Federal law enacted before the tax conversion date, no officer may assess or collect a tax, duty, impost, or excise with respect to an event, transaction, payment, use, or period occurring on or after that date, and a preexisting taxing provision has no effect to that extent, except for—

(1) the VAT imposed by section 302;

(2) a user fee that is limited to the reasonable cost of a specific service furnished to the payer and is not imposed to raise general revenue;

(3) a monetary penalty, forfeiture, restitution obligation, or amount of interest imposed for a violation of law; and

(4) a customs processing provision necessary to administer the VAT on imports, but not a customs duty abolished by section 503.

**(c) Closing and prior taxable periods.**—

(1) A repeal or termination under subsection (a) or (b) does not extinguish a liability, refund claim, credit, carryback, audit, collection action, lien, or judicial proceeding attributable to a taxable period or transaction before the tax conversion date. A net overpayment for such a period shall be refunded with applicable interest under the law in effect for that period.

(2) A taxable year for a tax imposed under subtitle A of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 that begins before, and otherwise would end on or after, the tax conversion date closes at the end of the day before that date. Income, gain, deduction, loss, and credit shall be allocated by a closing of the books, except that an item properly accruing ratably over time shall be allocated ratably. Section 443 and the procedural provisions of subtitle F of that Code apply to the resulting short taxable year as if a change of annual accounting period were required by law, without a consent requirement. The Secretary may prescribe only those allocation, consolidated-return, partnership, S-corporation, withholding, estimated-tax, and information-return rules necessary to prevent omission or double counting.

(3) A final return for a short taxable year under paragraph (2) is due, and the resulting tax shall be paid, at the time prescribed for a short-period return under pre-conversion law. A payment, withholding amount, credit, carryback, or overpayment attributable to a pre-conversion period remains subject to that law. An unused carryforward or other tax attribute does not become refundable merely because of repeal and may be used after the tax conversion date only to resolve a liability or refund expressly preserved by this subsection.

(4) Paragraphs (1) through (3) apply in the same manner to a Federal organic, mirror, or conformity tax whose operation ends under subsection (g). They do not close a taxable period of an independently enacted territorial or local tax that remains in force after the tax conversion date.

**(d) Coordinated operation.**—The repeals and terminations under subsections (a) and (b) and the imposition under section 302 take effect simultaneously on the tax conversion date determined under sections 302(m) and 605(7). Before that date, pre-conversion tax law remains in force subject to the other repeals in this Act. If a candidate tax conversion date fails the objective fiscal-eligibility or operational-test requirement in section 302(m), all of the coordinated provisions roll together to the next candidate date. No officer may accelerate, waive, or separately delay one side of the conversion, and no later Act or discretionary executive approval is required.

**(e) Public inventory and conforming legislation.**—Not later than 18 months before the earliest candidate tax conversion date, the Secretary of the Treasury, in consultation with the Office of the Law Revision Counsel of the House of Representatives, shall publish a machine-readable inventory of each provision treated as inoperative under subsection (b) and shall submit to Congress proposed technical conforming legislation. The inventory shall be updated for a material legal change not later than 1 year before each later candidate date. It is informational, and an omission does not authorize collection of a tax barred by subsection (b).

**(f) Payroll- and excise-funded program coordination.**—

(1) **Scope.**—This subsection governs each program, trust fund, or Federal-State financing arrangement materially financed by a tax repealed under subtitle C or D of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, including the Railroad Retirement Act of 1974 (45 U.S.C. 231 et seq.), the Railroad Unemployment Insurance Act (45 U.S.C. 351 et seq.), the Federal Unemployment Tax Act, and each trust fund governed by chapter 98 of that Code, except to the extent another provision of this Act supplies a more specific rule.

(2) **Protected pre-conversion claims.**—A benefit, reimbursement, credit, withdrawal right, contractual payment, or other claim legally attributable under pre-conversion law to service, wages, a contribution, tax payment, exposure, injury, benefit year, insured event, obligation, or other operative fact occurring before the tax conversion date remains enforceable according to that law even if determined or payable later. A claim may not be counted twice under this paragraph and another transition provision. The applicable trust fund or account is the first source of payment, and, to the extent that source is insufficient, amounts necessary to pay the protected claim are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury.

(3) **No post-conversion accrual by implication.**—Service, wages, a contribution, exposure, injury, benefit year, insured event, or other operative fact occurring on or after the tax conversion date creates no new Federal benefit, credit, insurance coverage, grant, matching payment, reimbursement, or intergovernmental transfer under an affected program unless another provision of this Act or an Act enacted after this Act expressly creates and finances that post-conversion claim without a tax repealed by this section. An amount credited before the tax conversion date to a State's account in the Unemployment Trust Fund remains available for withdrawal under the State-account and unemployment-compensation rules otherwise applicable, but no new Federal unemployment tax, Federal administrative grant, or Federal contribution is created after that date.

(4) **Administration and closing.**—On the tax conversion date, the Secretary of the Treasury succeeds to the Federal assets, liabilities, payment authority, records, and pending proceedings of each affected trust fund or financing arrangement solely to administer paragraphs (2) and (3), settle pre-conversion intergovernmental accounts, collect an amount already due, and close the arrangement. The Secretary may assign ministerial claims administration to a surviving agency but may not continue a terminated program, create a new eligibility category, or impose a tax, contribution, or State administrative duty. The Secretary shall maintain separate accounts and publish an annual independent audit until the last protected claim is discharged, then transfer any unobligated residual balance to the general fund and transfer the records to a lawful custodian.

(5) **Receipt-based formulas.**—A permanent appropriation, transfer, or intergovernmental payment calculated by reference to receipts from a tax repealed by this section applies after the tax conversion date only to the amount attributable to pre-conversion receipts or to a protected claim under paragraph (2). It does not attach to VAT receipts. A negative closing adjustment or Federal payment legally attributable to a pre-conversion period remains payable under paragraph (2).

(6) **No conversion condition.**—Not later than 18 months before the earliest candidate tax conversion date, the Secretary shall publish a machine-readable inventory applying this subsection to each affected program, fund, and financing arrangement and shall update it for a material change not later than 1 year before each later candidate date. The inventory is informational; an omission, error, delayed rule, incomplete transfer, or failure to publish does not alter eligibility, preserve a repealed tax, or create a post-conversion claim.

**(g) Territorial mirror taxes and cover-over rules.**—

(1) **Federal mirror systems.**—On the tax conversion date, sections 1397, 1406h, 1421h, 1421i, 1469a-1, 1642, and 1842 of title 48, United States Code, sections 7651 through 7654 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, sections 601 and 703(b) of the Covenant to Establish a Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands in Political Union with the United States (Public Law 94-241; 90 Stat. 263), and any other Federal organic, mirror-tax, administration, coordination, or cover-over provision have no effect to the extent they incorporate, impose, administer, or direct payment of a tax repealed or terminated by this Act. Sections 1406h, 1421h, 1469a-1, 1642, and 1842 of title 48, United States Code, section 703(b) of that Covenant, and another preexisting cover-over law do not apply to Federal VAT receipts, which shall be deposited under section 302(k).

(2) **Independent territorial law.**—Nothing in this subsection invalidates a tax independently enacted under territorial law, including a tax that incorporates a fixed body of Federal statutory text. Repeal of Federal law does not itself supply an otherwise missing local tax, rate, base, amendment, or administrative power. Each territory or possession may retain, amend, replace, or repeal its own tax under otherwise applicable organic authority. A territorial or local tax is not creditable against the Federal VAT.

(3) **Legacy administration.**—The incumbent territorial tax administrator remains authorized, but is not required by this Act, solely to assess, collect, refund, compromise, litigate, and preserve a lien or record for a pre-conversion liability administered by that office. The Secretary of the Treasury and the Value-Added Tax Administration succeed to the corresponding Federal duties formerly assigned to the Secretary or the Internal Revenue Service. Not later than 1 year before the earliest candidate tax conversion date, the Secretary, after consultation with each affected territorial government that chooses to participate, shall publish a closing protocol identifying the administrator, return, payment, deposit, withholding, estimated-tax, refund, lien, records custodian, pending case, limitation period, and intergovernmental transfer applicable to each class of preserved matter. The protocol shall be updated before each later candidate date for a material change. A territorial government may enter a voluntary agreement to perform or assist with a legacy function, subject to reimbursement expressly provided by that agreement and an appropriation, but no territorial officer is required to administer Federal law.

(4) **Closing reconciliation.**—Not later than 2 years after the last timely final return is due, the Secretary shall publish a proposed closing reconciliation prepared with each affected territorial government that voluntarily participates or, if a government declines, from available Federal records. After considering an advisory independent audit and any territorial submission, the Secretary shall make and publish the final Federal reconciliation and each Federal payment or refund determination through an officer authorized under section 601(d), with written reasons and otherwise applicable judicial review. The reconciliation shall identify each unresolved intergovernmental item and remains subject to a later adjustment for a preserved audit, refund claim, or judicial proceeding. A refund or net collection shall be charged to or credited to the government that retained, or under pre-conversion law was entitled to retain, the corresponding receipt. There are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury amounts necessary for a Federal closing payment or refund required by this paragraph or pre-conversion law, net of amounts lawfully payable by the territorial government. A transfer of taxpayer records is governed by the confidentiality, notice, and use restrictions that applied before the transfer and by section 603.

(5) **No duplicate territorial refund.**—A refund or payment under this subsection shall be credited against, and to the extent paid shall satisfy, the same claim under subsection (c). No claimant or government may receive more than one payment for the same overpayment, and each prior credit or refund shall be deducted.

### SEC. 302. ESTABLISHMENT OF TIERED VALUE-ADDED TAX

**(a) Imposition and structure.**—

(1) **In general.**—Effective on the tax conversion date, a value-added tax (in this Act referred to as the "VAT") is imposed on each taxable supply for consideration by a taxable person in the course of a trade or business and on each taxable import. The VAT shall be collected through the credit-invoice method, under which tax is charged on the full consideration for a taxable supply and the supplier may claim allowable input credits under subsection (f).

(2) **Destination basis.**—The VAT is destination-based: a taxable import is taxed, an export is zero-rated, and the place-of-supply rules under subsection (e) determine where services, intangibles, and remote sales are consumed.

(3) **Taxable person.**—The term "taxable person" means an individual or entity that independently carries on a trade or business and is required or elects to register under subsection (g). An employee acting solely as an employee, and a unit of government acting solely in a sovereign capacity, is not a taxable person.

(4) **Taxable supply.**—The term "taxable supply" means a sale, lease, license, exchange, transfer, or other provision of goods, services, real-property rights, or intangible property for consideration, other than an activity expressly excluded by this section. A transfer between related persons for less than fair market value is treated as made for fair market value to the extent prescribed by regulation.

(5) **Taxable amount.**—The taxable amount is the money and fair market value of nonmoney consideration for a supply, excluding the VAT itself. If a stated price includes VAT and only one rate applies, the tax-exclusive amount shall be determined by dividing the price by 1 plus the applicable rate expressed as a decimal. A threshold in subsection (b)(3) is measured in tax-exclusive consideration. If a tax-inclusive price (P) spans a tax-exclusive threshold (T), the tax-exclusive amount is (P div (1 + r_S)) when (P) does not exceed ((1 + r_S)T), and otherwise is (T + [P - (1 + r_S)T] div (1 + r_P)), where (r_S) and (r_P) are the operative standard and premium rates expressed as decimals. The fair market value of nonmoney consideration is determined when the supply occurs under subsection (h), using the arm's-length exchange rate actually applied by the parties or, if none is reliably established, contemporaneous market data from a consistently used independent source; if no active market exists, the parties' good-faith valuation controls unless clearly unreasonable. Payment, transfer, or redemption of a covered monetary instrument described in section 402(a)(6) as consideration is outside the VAT base under subsection (n)(1) and does not itself create a second taxable supply by the payer, but the goods, services, or other property acquired remain subject to VAT as otherwise provided. A third-party payment is consideration only to the extent directly linked to the price or quantity of an identified supply. A voluntary gratuity not required as a condition of service is not consideration.

(6) **Uniformity.**—The VAT is imposed as a uniform excise under article I, section 8, clause 1 and article IV, section 3, clause 2 of the Constitution. Each statutory rate and classification shall apply on the same terms throughout the VAT territory, subject only to destination and place-of-supply rules.

(7) **Related persons.**—Persons are related if one directly or indirectly owns or controls more than 50 percent of the other, the same persons directly or indirectly own or control more than 50 percent of each, or the persons are an individual and the individual's spouse, ancestor, lineal descendant, or sibling. The Secretary may prescribe attribution rules that prevent avoidance without treating ordinary arm's-length counterparties as related.

(8) **VAT territory.**—The term "VAT territory" means each State as defined in section 605, including every territory and possession of the United States.

(9) **Taxable import.**—The term "taxable import" means the entry for consumption into the VAT territory of goods from outside that territory, whether or not the entry follows a supply, excluding a temporary admission or returned domestic good under documented procedures that prevent avoidance. A remotely supplied service or intangible is taxed as a taxable supply under subsection (e), not again as an imported good.

(10) **Output VAT.**—The term "output VAT" means VAT that a taxable person charges or is required to charge on taxable supplies made during a taxable period, before reduction by input credits, whether or not the person timely registered.

**(b) Tiered rates and classifications.**—The following provisional rates apply to the taxable amount of a supply and to the taxable amount of a taxable import determined under subsection (e)(4), subject to the automatic annual rates calculated under subsection (c). An imported good has the same rate and classification as an equivalent domestic supply, subject to the input credits in subsection (f):

(1) **Zero rate limited to exports.**—Only an export qualifying under subsection (d)(1), and no domestic supply or domestic consumption merely because it is considered necessary or socially beneficial, is zero-rated. This paragraph does not place an activity outside the VAT base under subsection (n).

(2) **Twenty-five-percent provisional standard rate.**—A taxable supply or taxable import not zero-rated under paragraph (1) and not described in paragraph (3) is provisionally taxed at 25 percent. The standard rate expressly includes unprepared food and nonalcoholic beverages for human consumption; prescription drugs, prescribed durable medical equipment, medically necessary health-care services and insurance; residential water, sewer, electricity, and primary heating service; primary, secondary, and postsecondary education and required instructional materials; a lease of a dwelling; the first sale of newly constructed residential property; prepared food; clothing; household goods; electronics; professional services; and the standalone financial services identified in subsection (n)(1). The classification does not imply that government must provide, subsidize, license, or regulate any listed supply.

(3) **Thirty-seven-and-one-half-percent provisional premium rate.**—The following supplies are provisionally taxed at 37.5 percent:

(A) jewelry or fur apparel for which the consideration for a single item exceeds $10,000;\
(B) a recreational vessel for which the consideration exceeds $250,000;\
(C) a passenger motor vehicle for which the consideration exceeds $150,000, or a noncommercial private aircraft;\
(D) tobacco products, alcoholic beverages, wagers, and gambling services; and\
(E) a nonstructural residential improvement for which the consideration exceeds $500,000.

For a supply described in subparagraph (A), (B), or (E), and for a passenger motor vehicle described in subparagraph (C), the standard rate applies up to the applicable threshold and the premium rate applies only to the excess. The premium rate applies to the full taxable amount of a private aircraft or a supply described in subparagraph (D). The premium rate for each rate year is 150 percent of the standard rate for that rate year under subsection (c).

(4) **Indexing and classification.**—For a calendar year after the first calendar year beginning on or after the tax conversion date, each dollar amount in paragraph (3) shall be multiplied by the ratio of the average Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers for the 12 months ending with August of the preceding calendar year to the average index for the 12 months ending with August immediately before the first calendar year beginning on or after the tax conversion date, shall not be reduced below its statutory amount, and shall be rounded to the nearest $1,000. The Secretary of the Treasury shall publish binding classification rules not later than 1 year before the earliest candidate tax conversion date and update them for a material statutory or factual change not later than 270 days before each later candidate date. A rule may define a statutory category and address a bundled supply but may not move a supply to a different rate based solely on brand, purchaser identity, or a subjective determination of quality or prestige.

(5) **Objective classification standards.**—The Secretary shall define the premium categories in paragraph (3) by reference to objective product, construction, and billing characteristics. A "nonstructural residential improvement" means work not reasonably required for structural integrity or to remedy a material health or safety defect; related contracts and change orders for the same residence during a 12-month period are aggregated. A rule may not create a domestic zero rate, premium category, exemption, deduction, rebate, or exclusion not enacted by Congress.

**(c) Automatic fiscal-continuity calculation.**—Congress enacts the rates, formula, input hierarchy, initial-eligibility ceiling, continuing-rate ceilings, reserve, and fallback in this subsection. The Secretary of the Treasury and the Director of the Office of Management and Budget perform only the required estimation, classification, and arithmetic and may not choose a policy adjustment, waive the formula, or postpone its operation.

(1) **Rate year and reports.**—A "rate year" is a calendar year beginning on or after the tax conversion date during which the VAT is operative. Not later than March 31 preceding each candidate tax conversion date, and thereafter not later than 270 days before each rate year, the Secretary and the Director shall publish a joint machine-readable report estimating the partial fiscal year containing the start of the rate year and each of the next 10 fiscal years. For a candidate date, the report shall also state the actual values and eligibility calculation required by subsection (m). The report shall state separately—

(A) the gross tax-exclusive standard and premium VAT bases, taxable imports, output tax, input credits, export and other refunds, net legal VAT yield, unit-rate net-yield coefficient, actual collections, and administrative costs;

(B) recurring non-VAT receipts and total cash outlays as defined in section 204(a), including each protected legacy benefit and voucher, transition-bond redemption and interest payment, maximum statutory transition allotment, defense and veterans' outlay, refund and interest, judgment and compensation, insurance backstop, court and territorial-court cost, agency wind-down cost, and administrative outlay;

(C) the amount that would have been collected under each tax repealed by section 301, displayed for comparison but not treated as post-conversion receipts;

(D) the cash-funding target, realization factor, unit-rate net-yield coefficient, quotient, rounded rates, ceiling shortfall, Residual Cash Shortfall Ledger, reserve balance, and automatic sequestration required by this subsection and section 204; and

(E) sensitivities for compliance, substitution, growth, consumption, recession, interest rates, benefit uptake, bond elections and redemption, refunds, and compensation.

The report shall display each available estimate of the Congressional Budget Office and the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation and explain each material difference. An estimate is not a condition of conversion or of a rate.

(2) **Cash-funding target.**—For rate year (t), (C_t) is 110 percent of the positive difference between projected total cash outlays other than VAT refunds and projected recurring total receipts other than VAT during that calendar year, plus the unpaid balance in the Residual Cash Shortfall Ledger that is not already included in projected outlays, and less the balance then available in the fiscal-continuity reserve above projected obligations exempt under section 204(d) that are due during the first 30 days of the rate year. Total cash outlays and recurring total receipts have the meanings in section 204(a); a projected non-VAT receipt is included only if the law producing it will remain in force during the rate year. VAT refunds are excluded from (C_t) because they are deducted in the unit-rate net-yield coefficient under paragraph (3); VAT-refund interest is included in (C_t) as a projected cash outlay and is not deducted in that coefficient. Both remain cash outlays for section 204 and may not be omitted from complete fiscal reporting. The projections used in paragraphs (2) through (5) shall reflect budgetary resources and legally payable obligations before any sequestration attributable to the calculation for that rate year; a reduction already in force from an earlier year or independent order remains reflected. The 10-percent addition is a contingency enacted by Congress and is not attributed to an estimate of the Congressional Budget Office or the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation. No amount may be credited for borrowing, issuance of a transition bond or another obligation, an intragovernmental transfer, an asset sale, liquidation proceeds, or another one-time receipt. Scheduled redemption, acceleration, principal, and interest on a Treasury transition bond or another cash obligation created by this Act are included; ordinary refinancing principal excluded by section 204(a)(2) is not.

(3) **Unit-rate net-yield coefficient.**—(Y_t) is the projected net legal VAT liability for rate year (t) if the standard rate were 100 percent and the premium rate were 150 percent, before nonpayment, nonfiling, evasion, and collection loss. It includes output tax on domestic supplies and taxable imports and deducts every allowable input credit, export refund, and other VAT refund, so that multiplying (Y_t) by a standard-rate decimal produces net legal yield without double counting a credit or refund. It does not deduct VAT-refund interest, which is time-dependent rather than proportional to the statutory rate and is included in the cash-funding target under paragraph (2). The report shall also display the corresponding tax-exclusive standard and premium final-consumption bases.

The coefficient shall reflect the most recent 4 complete calendar quarters of audited national accounts, customs entries, business receipts, and tax data, adjusted by the latest official consumption and price projection under paragraph (4). If no current projection is available, the latest coefficient is multiplied by the lesser of 1 or the ratio of the most recent 4-quarter nominal personal-consumption-expenditure level to the preceding 4-quarter level. A classification, credit, refund, or estimation method shall be consistent across years and may not be changed to lower the rate by moving a domestic supply outside the base, increasing a credit, or omitting a taxable import.

(4) **Binding source hierarchy and realization factor.**—An actual receipt or outlay shall be taken from the final Monthly Treasury Statement and reconciled Governmentwide Treasury Account Symbol Adjusted Trial Balance System data, subject to a later published correction and the annual audit of the financial statements of the United States. A tax-base or net-yield estimate shall use, in order of availability, an estimate prepared by the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation for this statutory base, an estimate of the Congressional Budget Office, and an estimate of the Office of Tax Analysis. If reasonably comparable estimates at the same priority are available, the lowest net-yield coefficient and the highest cash-funding target apply. An officer shall publish the source, vintage, adjustment, and reconciliation and may not substitute a lower-priority or more favorable input.

For the first rate year and each later rate year for which 4 complete post-conversion VAT quarters are not available by the report deadline, (k_t) is 0.75. That value is a conservative assumption enacted by Congress and is not a factual finding attributed to another entity. Thereafter, (k_t) is the quotient of actual gross VAT collections minus VAT refunds for the most recent 4 complete VAT quarters divided by the net legal VAT liability produced by the rates and audited unit-rate coefficients for those quarters before nonpayment, nonfiling, evasion, and collection loss. VAT-refund interest is excluded from both amounts and remains a cash outlay under paragraph (2). If that denominator is zero, negative, or unavailable, (k_t) is 0.50. Otherwise, if the quotient is less than 0.50, (k_t) is 0.50; if it is greater than 1, (k_t) is 1. A refund lawfully due is deducted whether or not payment was timely.

(5) **Formula and operative rates.**—The standard-rate quotient for rate year (t), with the quotient and rates expressed as decimals, is—

> (q_t = C_t div [k_tY_t]).

If (k_tY_t) is zero or negative, the maximum rates in this paragraph apply. Otherwise, the operative standard rate is (q_t), rounded upward to the next one-tenth of 1 percentage point, but not less than zero or more than 40 percent. The operative premium rate is 150 percent of the operative standard rate, rounded upward to the next one-tenth of 1 percentage point, but not more than 60 percent. The zero rate on exports remains zero. The provisional 25- and 37.5-percent rates in subsection (b) are used for implementation estimates and are superseded for each rate year by the ministerial notice under paragraph (6). The "ceiling shortfall" is the positive amount, if any, by which (C_t) exceeds (0.40k_tY_t). The ceiling binds only when that amount is positive, without regard to use of a fallback rate. The ceiling does not permit an officer to omit that amount; section 204(c) automatically applies to it. The operative rates and ceiling shortfall are fixed for that rate year from the pre-sequestration calculation and are not recomputed after the resulting order under section 204(c); the order reduces the residual cash shortfall rather than changing the formula that produced it.

(6) **Ministerial notice and fallback.**—Not later than 250 days before a rate year, the Secretary shall publish the values and arithmetic under paragraphs (2) through (5) and the resulting operative rates. The notice may correct a demonstrable clerical or arithmetic error not later than 210 days before the rate year but may not revise a policy assumption, classification, ceiling, or statutory input hierarchy. If no timely notice is published for the first rate year, the operative rates are the provisional rates of 25 percent standard and 37.5 percent premium; the fiscal-eligibility test in subsection (m) still must be satisfied. If no timely notice is published for a later rate year, the operative rates remain the rates in force for the immediately preceding rate year. Failure to report may not raise a rate, preserve a tax repealed by section 301, authorize a different tax, or alter the objective determination of the tax conversion date. Section 204(c) applies to a projected shortfall during a fallback year. A taxpayer may rely on the published or fallback rates for a transaction in that rate year.

(7) **Fiscal-continuity reserve.**—A Fiscal Continuity Reserve is established as a separate account in the Treasury. Recurring total receipts as defined in section 204(a)(3) in excess of total cash outlays shall be credited first to the reserve until its balance equals 10 percent of projected total cash outlays for the next rate year and thereafter to reduction of debt held by the public. An amount used from the reserve to pay a Residual Cash Shortfall Ledger entry reduces that entry by the same amount. Amounts in the reserve are permanently appropriated only to pay a due obligation exempt from sequestration under section 204(d), a VAT refund and interest lawfully payable on that refund, or a temporary within-year cash shortfall, and not to create or expand a program. Each use, replenishment, and balance shall be reported monthly and included without netting in the next calculation.

(8) **Review.**—A registered taxable person directly affected by a final notice, or a person whose pre-conversion Federal tax liability would terminate on a disputed candidate date, may obtain review in the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit not later than 30 days after publication. Review is limited to whether the officers complied with the binding source hierarchy, classifications, input rules, operational-test rules, and arithmetic required by this subsection and subsection (m). Before conversion, a correction may determine prospectively that a candidate date satisfies or fails the objective test; after a date has passed, a correction may not create retroactive VAT or retroactively revive a tax terminated by section 301. A correction of a later annual rate operates prospectively and does not invalidate tax collected at a rate on which a person was entitled to rely.

(9) **No unsupported fiscal claim.**—A report, rule, or official statement may not describe a fixed or calculated rate as revenue-neutral, sufficient, or scored by the Congressional Budget Office or the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation unless the description identifies the statutory base, realization assumption, time period, included obligations, behavioral response, and supporting official estimate. The provisional rates in subsection (b) are not an official score.

(10) **Initial-rate limitation.**—The uncapped standard-rate quotient calculated under paragraph (5) for the first rate year may not exceed 25 percent. If it does, the candidate tax conversion date is ineligible under subsection (m), the VAT and coordinated repeals do not take effect on that date, and the calculation shall be repeated for the next candidate date. This paragraph limits the rate at initial conversion and does not reduce the 40-percent standard-rate and 60-percent premium-rate ceilings for a later rate year.

**(d) Zero rating and nonregistered suppliers.**—

(1) **Exports.**—A supply is zero-rated as an export only under the following objective rules:

(A) A good qualifies if the supplier delivers it to a destination outside the VAT territory and retains a customs export record, carrier record, and commercial delivery evidence showing physical exit not later than 90 days after the time of supply. The Secretary may allow a longer period for a custom-built good or documented carrier delay and may allow a provisional credit secured by a bond, but a refund is not payable before reliable exit evidence exists.

(B) A service or intangible qualifies if the recipient's usual residence or business establishment receiving the supply is outside the VAT territory, the service or intangible is used or enjoyed outside that territory, and it is not an in-person service performed in the territory or a service relating to property there. The supplier shall retain at least 2 noncontradictory items of ordinary-course evidence, such as payment location, billing address, contracting establishment, network location, delivery record, or place of property use. Citizenship, incorporation, or a foreign billing address alone is insufficient.

(C) If exported property is diverted to domestic consumption, returned other than for repair or replacement, or supplied on facts that the supplier knew or reasonably should have known did not satisfy subparagraph (A) or (B), the person causing or knowingly participating in the diversion shall account for output VAT at the rate that would have applied domestically, with interest from the original due date. A good reimported after a completed foreign use is treated under the ordinary import rules rather than this recapture rule.

No VAT shall be imposed on a qualifying export, and input VAT attributable to it is creditable or refundable under subsection (f).

(2) **Inputs for taxable and zero-rated supplies.**—A registered taxable person may claim input credits for purchases used to make taxable or zero-rated supplies as provided in subsection (f). No credit is allowed to the extent an input is used for a personal purpose or to make a supply outside the VAT base.

(3) **Nonregistered small suppliers.**—A person below the registration threshold that does not elect registration shall not charge VAT and may not claim an input credit. A purchase from that person does not entitle the purchaser to an input credit.

**(e) Territorial and place-of-supply rules.**—

(1) The VAT applies throughout the VAT territory. The Secretary of the Treasury shall prescribe rules coordinating entry procedures, bonded zones, filing, and timing with territorial or local consumption taxes, but may not credit a territorial or local tax against Federal VAT, alter the Federal base, rate, liability, or refund, permit a territory or possession to opt out, or permit a non-Federal government to retain Federal receipts. A State, territory, possession, or locality remains free to alter or repeal its own tax.

(2) Goods are supplied where delivery to the customer occurs. Services relating to real property are supplied where the property is located. In-person services are supplied where performed. Digital, telecommunications, intangible, and other remotely supplied services are supplied at the customer's usual residence or business establishment, determined from objective payment, billing, network, and contracting evidence.

(3) A nonresident supplier that exceeds the registration threshold through supplies to customers in the VAT territory shall register and collect VAT. The Secretary may require a registered business customer to account for tax through a reverse-charge mechanism when the nonresident supplier does not collect the tax.

(4) The importer of record is liable for VAT on a taxable import, and U.S. Customs and Border Protection shall collect the VAT at entry, subject to input credit for a registered importer of record. Import valuation uses the customs value plus duties and incidental costs, but does not include the VAT itself. Movement of goods between two places within the VAT territory is not a taxable import, subject to reporting and bonded-movement rules that prevent a foreign import from avoiding tax by transshipment through a territory or possession.

(5) An electronic marketplace is treated as the supplier of a remotely ordered supply made through the marketplace by a nonresident if the marketplace controls payment, essential transaction terms, or delivery and the nonresident does not establish that it collected the VAT under a valid registration. The marketplace may rely on a registration-verification system and shall receive the corresponding input treatment under rules that prevent double taxation.

**(f) Credit-invoice mechanism.**—

(1) **Credits.**—A registered taxable person is entitled to a credit against output VAT for VAT properly charged on a purchase or import to the extent the input is used to make taxable or zero-rated supplies in the course of the person's trade or business.

(2) **Documentation.**—No credit shall be allowed under paragraph (1) unless the taxable person possesses a valid VAT invoice that meets subsection (i), an import entry, reverse-charge return, or other reliable third-party evidence expressly authorized by regulation for a circumstance in which the supplier cannot issue an invoice. A regulation may not allow an unsupported estimate to substitute for evidence of tax actually paid or accounted for.

(3) **Refunds.**—If allowable credits exceed output VAT for a taxable period, the excess shall be refunded within 60 days after a complete return is filed. The Secretary may suspend the portion reasonably in dispute by issuing a written notice that states particular grounds. Interest at the Federal short-term rate plus 3 percentage points accrues after the 60th day on an amount ultimately allowed. Section 1274(d) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 remains in force solely to determine the Federal short-term rate for this paragraph and section 203(c)(2).

(4) **Apportionment and restrictions.**—An input used partly for taxable business supplies and partly for personal activity or supplies outside the VAT base shall be apportioned by a reasonable method consistently applied. The Secretary may deny or limit credits for entertainment and passenger vehicles not used predominantly in a trade or business, but may not deny a credit solely because an input is a capital asset.

(5) **Bad debts and adjustments.**—A registered supplier may reduce output VAT for consideration that becomes wholly or partly uncollectible after reasonable collection efforts, and shall increase output VAT if the consideration is later recovered. A price adjustment, return, cancellation, or invoice correction shall be reflected in the period in which it occurs.

(6) **No credit trading.**—An input credit may be used only by the person to whom the qualifying supply was made, except in a merger, division, or transfer of a going concern governed by regulations that preserve the audit trail.

(7) **Self-supply and change of use.**—A taxable person that claimed an input credit and later permanently withdraws the input for personal use, makes a gift, or changes the input to an activity outside the VAT base shall account for output VAT on the fair market value at the time of withdrawal or make a proportionate capital-goods adjustment. The Secretary shall prescribe adjustment periods not exceeding 10 years for real property and 5 years for other capital assets and shall prevent duplicate tax when the asset is later sold.

**(g) Registration.**—

(1) **Threshold.**—Any person whose taxable turnover in the VAT territory exceeds $250,000 in any 12-month period shall be required to register for VAT with the Value-Added Tax Administration. "Taxable turnover" means the tax-exclusive value of taxable and zero-rated supplies placed in the VAT territory under subsection (e) and made in the course of the person's trade or business, excluding a sale of a capital asset not held primarily for sale to customers.

For a calendar year after the first calendar year beginning on or after the tax conversion date, the threshold shall be multiplied by the ratio of the average Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers for the 12 months ending with August of the preceding calendar year to the average index for the 12 months ending with August immediately before the first calendar year beginning on or after the tax conversion date, shall not be reduced below $250,000, and shall be rounded to the nearest $5,000. Turnover of related persons shall be aggregated if separate organization has a principal purpose of avoiding registration.

(2) **Mandatory registration.**—A person that exceeded the threshold during the preceding 12 months, or reasonably expects to exceed it during the next 30 days, shall register not later than 30 days after that condition first occurs. Liability begins when registration was required, whether or not the person timely registered.

(3) **Voluntary registration.**—A person whose taxable turnover does not exceed the threshold in paragraph (1) may elect to register voluntarily. A person who registers voluntarily shall be treated as a taxable person for all purposes of this title.

(4) **Cancellation of registration.**—The Secretary shall provide for cancellation if taxable turnover remains below the threshold for 12 months and is reasonably expected to remain below it. A person that voluntarily registered shall remain registered for at least 2 years unless the business ceases.

**(h) Returns and payment.**—

(1) **Filing requirement.**—Each registered taxable person shall file a return not later than 30 days after each taxable period. The taxable period is a calendar quarter, except that the Secretary may require monthly filing by a person with annual taxable turnover exceeding $50,000,000 or permit annual filing by a person with turnover below $1,000,000.

(2) **Contents.**—The return shall state—

(A) the total value of taxable supplies made by the taxable person during the period;\
(B) the total VAT charged on such supplies;\
(C) the total VAT paid on taxable purchases for which credit is claimed; and\
(D) such other information as the Secretary of the Treasury may require.

(3) **Payment.**—Any VAT shown as due on the return, net of allowable credits, shall be paid at the time the return is filed.

(4) **Electronic filing.**—The Secretary of the Treasury may require electronic filing and payment by classes of taxable persons, taking into account size, sophistication, and administrative capacity.

(5) **Time of supply.**—Except as otherwise prescribed for continuous supplies, deposits, installment sales, or self-billing, a supply occurs at the earliest of invoice issuance, payment, or delivery or performance. A registered person shall use the accrual method unless authorized to use a cash method because annual taxable turnover does not exceed $1,000,000.

(6) **Related entities.**—Commonly controlled entities may elect to file a consolidated VAT return under rules that impose joint and several liability and eliminate tax on transactions within the group without breaking the invoice trail for transactions with persons outside the group.

**(i) Invoices and records.**—

(1) **VAT invoices.**—A taxable person making a taxable supply shall issue to the recipient a VAT invoice showing—

(A) the name, address, and registration number of the supplier;\
(B) the name and address of the recipient, if registered;\
(C) a unique sequential invoice number and the date of the supply;\
(D) a description of the goods or services supplied;\
(E) the value of the supply, exclusive of VAT; and\
(F) the applicable rate and amount of VAT charged.

(2) **Recordkeeping.**—Taxable persons shall keep and preserve such records, including copies of VAT invoices issued and received, as the Secretary of the Treasury may require by regulation, for a period of not less than 6 years after the end of the taxable period to which they relate.

(3) **Simplified invoices.**—The Secretary may prescribe a simplified receipt for low-value retail transactions with unregistered consumers, but the receipt shall state the tax-inclusive price and applicable VAT rate and shall not support an input credit unless it identifies the registered purchaser.

(4) **Electronic invoices.**—The Secretary may establish interoperable electronic-invoice standards and may require their use by persons above a turnover threshold, but shall provide a non-electronic alternative for a person that demonstrates lack of reasonable access.

**(j) Anti-avoidance.**—The Secretary may disregard or recharacterize a transaction only if, considering all steps together, it lacks substantial economic effect apart from obtaining a VAT benefit or uses an entity, invoice, or consideration that does not reflect the parties' actual conduct. A determination shall be stated in a notice of deficiency and is subject to ordinary administrative and judicial review. A regulation under this subsection may not apply to a completed transaction before the regulation was proposed.

**(k) Coordination with other law.**—The VAT is in lieu of the taxes repealed by section 301. Receipts shall be deposited in the general fund of the Treasury and are available only as provided by an appropriation or other express budget authority, including the permanent appropriations in sections 201 and 205. This title does not preempt a State or local sales, use, gross-receipts, or value-added tax. The Secretary shall seek voluntary agreements for common registration, definitions, electronic filing, and audits without giving a State authority to administer or retain Federal VAT receipts.

**(l) Judicial review and Tax Court jurisdiction.**—

(1) The United States Tax Court has jurisdiction to redetermine a VAT deficiency and to determine the rate, place of supply, registration status, input credit, refund offset, and penalty properly at issue in that proceeding. Deficiency procedures in subtitle F of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 apply except as otherwise provided in this Act.

(2) A registered person that will be directly affected by a final classification rule and faces a present compliance obligation may petition the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit for review not later than 90 days after publication. Review is based on the rulemaking record and does not prevent later review as applied in a deficiency or refund proceeding.

(3) A decision of the Tax Court is appealable as provided in section 7482 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986.

**(m) Objective initial fiscal eligibility and operational testing.**—

(1) **Ministerial calculation.**—Not later than March 31 preceding each candidate tax conversion date, the Secretary of the Treasury and the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall publish a joint machine-readable calculation. The Comptroller General and the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration shall each publish an independent audit or separate view not later than 15 days afterward. For the eligibility measurement period applicable to that candidate date, the calculation shall state operative cash outlays (O_m), operative recurring non-VAT receipts (N_m), and the unit-rate net-yield coefficient (Y_m) determined under subsection (c)(3) and (4) for the statutory VAT base and classifications in force. Actual cash amounts shall come from the final Monthly Treasury Statement reconciled by Treasury Account Symbol to certified Governmentwide Treasury Account Symbol Adjusted Trial Balance System data, subject to a later public correction and the annual audit of the financial statements of the United States. The calculation shall identify every included and excluded account and may not use a forecast, asset value, borrowing, or a receipt from a law scheduled to terminate at conversion as actual cash funding.

(2) **Eligibility arithmetic.**—If (Y_m) is zero, negative, or unavailable, the candidate date is not fiscally eligible and (r_m) shall not be calculated. Otherwise, the initial standard-rate requirement (r_m), expressed as a decimal, is—

> (r_m = 1.10 times max(0, O_m - N_m) div (0.75Y_m)).

The result shall be rounded upward to the next one-tenth of 1 percentage point. A candidate date is fiscally eligible only if (Y_m) is positive, (r_m) does not exceed 25 percent, the uncapped first-rate-year quotient under subsection (c)(5) does not exceed 25 percent, and each repeal, abolition, spending termination, subsidy prohibition, outlay cap, and transition reduction that this Act schedules to occur before that candidate date was legally in force throughout the eligibility measurement period or for the entire portion of that period beginning on its otherwise applicable effective date. The 0.75 realization factor and 10-percent contingency are enacted assumptions, not findings attributed to the Congressional Budget Office or the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation. A protected or nonsequesterable obligation remains in (O_m).

(3) **Objective operational test.**—A candidate date is operationally eligible only if, not later than 300 days before that date, registration, return processing, payment, import collection, invoice validation, refund payment, cybersecurity failover, taxpayer assistance, and independent appeals have each completed a publicly documented end-to-end test using production-scale systems. The test shall cover every State, territory, and possession; first entry and customs-zone treatment; bonded movement within the VAT territory; registration and filing access; taxpayer-record transition; and the interfaces among U.S. Customs and Border Protection, the Internal Revenue Service acting through the dedicated VAT implementation office under subsection (o)(1), and participating territorial tax or customs administrators. For testing and transition, that office is the legal and operational predecessor of the Value-Added Tax Administration established under section 303(a). A known defect that prevents an enumerated function throughout any part of the VAT territory must be corrected and retested before the condition is satisfied. A test failure does not authorize a geographic exemption, opt-out, or different Federal rate.

(4) **Automatic date; no discretion.**—The first candidate date that is both fiscally and operationally eligible is the tax conversion date by force of this Act. A failed candidate rolls automatically to the next candidate date, and the calculation and tests shall be repeated. No officer may accelerate, waive, or postpone that result, select a different rate or tax, treat pre-conversion tax receipts as post-conversion funding, or make eligibility depend on a policy finding or later Act. The reports are ministerial evidence of the statutory facts and do not create the result. If the Secretary and the Director omit the joint calculation, the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration shall publish the calculation from the raw sources within 15 days; omission by every officer does not alter the statutory test, and a person directly affected may obtain expedited mandamus or review in the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit. If an indispensable source value or operational test record does not exist, the candidate date is not eligible; an officer has a continuing nondiscretionary duty to produce the missing value or complete the test for the next candidate.

**(n) Special sectors.**—

(1) **Financial services.**—Principal, interest, dividends, the issuance or transfer of a security, and core financial intermediation are outside the VAT base. "Core financial intermediation" means accepting a deposit, extending credit, operating a payment account, transferring funds, exchanging currency, or dealing in a financial instrument for the supplier's own account, whether the consideration is an explicit fee, an implicit margin, or both. Standalone investment advice, brokerage or underwriting for a customer, data service, safe-deposit service, debt collection, and professional service are taxed at the standard rate. An input used to make a supply outside the VAT base does not give rise to an input credit to that extent. The Secretary shall prescribe consistent apportionment and anti-relabeling rules so an economically equivalent intermediation service receives the same treatment without regard to whether its price is stated as a fee or spread. Not later than 2 years after the tax conversion date, the Secretary shall submit to Congress proposed legislation for a cash-flow or account-based method that would include intermediation margins without taxing saving or investment principal; no such method takes effect without an Act of Congress.

(2) **Insurance.**—Property, casualty, health, and other non-life risk insurance is taxed at the standard rate under the following margin method:

(A) For each calendar year, an insurer's taxable margin is premiums and other policy consideration received for risk located in the VAT territory, plus recoveries and reinsurance proceeds attributable to claims for that risk, minus claims actually paid, policyholder refunds, and premiums paid for ceded reinsurance attributable to that risk. A change in an accounting reserve, without a receipt or payment described in the preceding sentence, is not included.

(B) A reinsurer applies the same formula to assumed risk. A ceding insurer shall include a reinsurance recovery and deduct the corresponding ceded premium, so the same amount is neither taxed nor deducted twice. That deduction is the exclusive credit-equivalent treatment of ceded reinsurance, and the ceding insurer may not also claim a separate input credit for the reinsurer's margin.

(C) A negative margin is carried forward against later positive margins and is not refunded except on permanent cessation after audit. The insurer shall make provisional quarterly payments under a reasonable consistently applied estimate and reconcile them on the return for the final quarter of the calendar year.

(D) Premiums and payments attributable to coverage completed before the tax conversion date are excluded. A policy spanning that date shall be allocated by time and insured risk under objective actuarial records. Risk is located where insured real property is situated, where an insured individual usually resides, or, for other business risk, at the establishment to which the policy relates.

(E) A policy combining health coverage and other coverage shall use a supportable actuarial allocation when different margin, location, or credit rules apply. After the annual reconciliation, the insurer shall issue an adjustment invoice allocating a positive taxable margin among registered business policyholders in proportion to their net taxable premiums; that invoice supports the policyholder's input credit. An interim invoice may state creditable VAT only under a published method that requires a year-end correction.

(F) For life insurance, an annuity, or another contract combining insurance with saving or investment, an actuarially supportable mortality, disability, or other non-life risk component is treated under this paragraph. A separately maintained policyholder account, deposit, investment principal, credited investment return, death or maturity benefit attributable to that account, surrender payment, or annuity principal is outside the VAT base under paragraph (1). A standalone administrative service is taxed under paragraph (1), subject to its anti-relabeling rule.

(G) If a nonresident reinsurer does not register and collect VAT, a registered ceding insurer shall apply a year-end reverse charge to the positive amount of ceded premiums over recoveries attributable to risk in the VAT territory, with a negative amount carried forward under subparagraph (C). The reverse charge does not produce a separate input credit because the ceded-premium deduction under subparagraph (B) supplies the corresponding credit-equivalent treatment.

(3) **Real property.**—The first sale of newly constructed real property by a developer in business and a lease of real property, including a dwelling used as a primary residence, are taxed at the standard rate unless the premium rate expressly applies. A sale of previously occupied residential property by a person not acting as a developer is outside the VAT base, except that the Secretary shall require a proportionate adjustment during a 10-year capital-goods period for input credits claimed by a lessor or developer on construction or substantial improvement. Commercial rent, brokerage, construction, and improvement services are taxed at the standard rate unless the premium rate expressly applies.

(4) **Governmental and nonprofit activity.**—A government or nonprofit entity is treated as a taxable person for a class of supplies only when it makes those supplies in substantial competition with private businesses. "Substantial competition" exists only if, during the preceding 12 months, at least 2 unrelated private taxable persons made the same or reasonably substitutable supplies to customers in the same relevant geographic market, the government or nonprofit entity received tax-exclusive consideration for that class exceeding the registration threshold in subsection (g)(1), and the entity accounted for at least 5 percent of the total tax-exclusive consideration for that class in that market. Supplies are reasonably substitutable when customers ordinarily can use one in place of the other for substantially the same purpose; the relevant geographic market is the smallest area in which customers reasonably can obtain those substitutes. A sovereign, adjudicative, legislative, police, military, or generally available public service for which no specific consideration is charged is not in substantial competition. The Secretary may prescribe record and allocation rules but may not deem competition to exist merely because a private person could theoretically offer a service. A grant or donation for which the payer receives no specific good, service, or right is not consideration.

(5) **Wagers and gambling.**—For wagers and gambling services, the taxable amount for a taxable period is consideration received for wagers and participation less cash prizes and winnings paid or unconditionally credited to customers for that period. A stake or prize does not create an input credit, and a negative amount shall be carried forward rather than refunded except on permanent cessation under anti-abuse rules.

(6) **Cessation refunds.**—A person permanently ceases an activity under paragraph (2) or (5) only when the person and each related person have stopped making that class of supply in the VAT territory, have transferred no material part of the activity to a successor other than in a transaction in which the unused negative margin carries over to that successor, and have satisfied or adequately reserved each VAT liability for that activity. The person may claim, not later than 3 years after filing the final return for the activity, a refund of the unused negative margin multiplied by the statutory rate that would have applied to a positive margin from that activity when the negative margin arose. The Value-Added Tax Administration shall audit the claim, supporting premiums, wagers, claims, prizes, recoveries, prior carryforwards, credits, and refunds. A refund is denied to the extent the negative margin was purchased, duplicated, previously used, produced by a transaction lacking economic substance, or attributable to an activity continued by a related person. Section 303(h) supplies the appropriation and payment limit for an allowed refund under this paragraph.

**(o) Transition rules.**—

(1) The Internal Revenue Service, acting through a dedicated VAT implementation office, shall open voluntary registration and invoice-system testing at least 1 year before the earliest candidate tax conversion date and shall maintain testing and registration until conversion. Test invoices have no tax effect and shall be clearly marked.

(2) No input credit is allowed for inventory or a capital asset acquired before the tax conversion date unless VAT was lawfully imposed on that acquisition. The Secretary may prescribe a basis-allocation rule for a single supply performed partly before and partly after that date.

(3) VAT applies to a payment or performance after the tax conversion date under a preexisting contract unless the supply occurred before that date under subsection (h)(5), as modified by paragraph (5). Whether the stated contract price includes VAT is determined under the contract and applicable State law; the United States does not alter the bargain between private parties.

(4) A registration, election, classification ruling, or accounting-method approval issued before the tax conversion date takes effect on that date and is subject to the same review as a post-conversion determination.

(5) An invoice or payment issued or made before the tax conversion date for a supply actually delivered or performed on or after that date does not move the time of supply before the conversion date. VAT applies on the conversion date to the portion not actually delivered or performed before that date, except for a customary refundable deposit under rules preventing avoidance. A supply completed before the conversion date is not subject to VAT merely because payment is received later.

**(p) Enacted conversion and continuing fiscal disclosure.**—

(1) **Advisory estimates.**—Not later than March 31 preceding each candidate tax conversion date until conversion occurs, the Director of the Congressional Budget Office, in consultation with the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation, shall publish or update an advisory estimate of the effect of this Act on receipts, outlays, deficits, and debt for the period from the date of enactment through the day before that candidate date and for the 10-, 30-, and 75-fiscal-year periods beginning with the fiscal year that includes that date. The estimate shall include transition bonds, transition allotments and grants, protected legacy benefits, vouchers, refunds, administrative and liquidation costs, asset-sale proceeds displayed separately from recurring receipts, preserved contractual and insurance obligations, reasonably expected contingent compensation under section 604, and macroeconomic feedback customarily included in an estimate. It shall also display distributional effects by household income and age, estimated State fiscal incidence with a separate display for each territory and possession, the revenue and refund effects of terminating each mirror or cover-over system, and a recession stress scenario for receipts, benefit obligations, and debt service. For this subsection, the comparison baseline is the law in effect immediately before enactment of this Act, adjusted for a later Act other than the provisions of this Act being estimated.

(2) **Conversion enacted by this Act.**—The VAT and the coordinated repeals and terminations under section 301 become operative on the tax conversion date by force of this Act. That date and the 25-percent initial-rate limitation are determined solely from the objective candidate-date, measurement-period, cash, yield, and operational-test rules in subsection (m) and section 605. No later Act, congressional finding, discretionary certification, appropriation, debt-limit measure, or executive approval is a condition of conversion. A report or notice supplies the required arithmetic and public notice but may not accelerate, waive, or postpone the statutory result once the underlying data and tests satisfy or fail the enacted rule.

(3) **Statutory fiscal dispositions.**—The debt-limit treatment for Treasury transition bonds is enacted in section 201(h)(5); the disposition of payroll- and excise-funded programs and trust funds is enacted in section 301(f); territorial closing and reconciliation rules are enacted in section 301(g); fiscal rate review and budget enforcement are enacted in subsection (c) and section 204; and judicial restart and resumption rules are enacted in section 602(b). An officer shall implement those provisions without awaiting further legislation and has no authority to substitute a different tax, spending program, territorial rule, or transition condition.

(4) **Advisory and operative status.**—An estimate of the Congressional Budget Office, the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation, the Office of Management and Budget, or an executive officer is advisory except for a source value, classification, or ministerial calculation that subsection (c) or (m) expressly makes operative. An advisory conclusion does not alter a statutory rate, create spending authority, or excuse a duty imposed by this Act. An operative input or calculation controls only through the objective hierarchy and arithmetic enacted in those subsections and remains subject to review.

(5) **Updated implementation material.**—For each candidate date after an ineligible candidate, each pre-conversion report, notice, testing result, classification, and taxpayer instruction shall be updated for material legal, fiscal, economic, or operational changes not later than the corresponding deadline for the new candidate. Voluntary registration and system testing shall remain open without interruption. Failure to update does not make an otherwise ineligible candidate eligible or authorize an officer to alter the next candidate date.

(6) **No framework-only status.**—This title enacts, rather than merely proposes, the VAT base, rates, administration, transition, and coordinated repeals. Implementation begins on the date of enactment under subsection (o), section 301, section 303, and section 601, and conversion does not depend on enactment of additional legislation.

### SEC. 303. ADMINISTRATION AND ENFORCEMENT

**(a) Administration and succession.**—On the tax conversion date, the Internal Revenue Service is redesignated as the Value-Added Tax Administration (in this section referred to as the "Administration"), the dedicated VAT implementation office under section 302(o)(1) becomes the operational core of the Administration without a break in personnel, records, systems, contracts, or test status, and the office of Commissioner of Internal Revenue is redesignated as the Commissioner of the Value-Added Tax Administration. The Commissioner is the head of the Administration. The incumbent Commissioner shall continue for the remainder of the term for which appointed, and the appointment, removal, supervision, and other provisions of section 7803 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 apply to the redesignated office. A reference in Federal law to the Internal Revenue Service or the Commissioner of Internal Revenue is deemed to refer to the Administration or its Commissioner, respectively, solely with respect to the VAT or a preserved pre-conversion function. The Administration shall retain the personnel, records, and authority necessary to resolve liabilities and refunds for pre-conversion taxable periods.

**(b) Assessment, collection, interest, and penalties.**—The Administration may examine returns, assess and collect VAT, pay refunds, impose interest and civil penalties, and investigate and refer a suspected criminal offense for prosecution only under the provisions expressly incorporated by subsection (c). A court may impose a criminal punishment only after conviction under an incorporated criminal provision. A penalty shall require the level of knowledge, negligence, willfulness, or other culpability specified in the incorporated provision. No penalty may be imposed twice for the same conduct under this Act and an incorporated provision.

**(c) Application of internal revenue laws.**—

(1) **Administrative and civil provisions.**—Except as otherwise provided in this Act, subtitle F of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 (26 U.S.C. 6001 et seq.), other than chapter 75, applies to the VAT and to a person liable for VAT as if the VAT were an excise tax imposed by chapter 41 of that Code. A term may be adapted only to the extent necessary to apply an existing administrative procedure, limitation period, interest rule, civil penalty, collection remedy, refund remedy, or judicial proceeding to the corresponding VAT matter.

(2) **Criminal provisions.**—Sections 7201 through 7207, 7212 through 7214, and 7216 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, and no other provision of chapter 75 incorporated by this Act, apply to the VAT. Solely for those sections, the VAT is treated as a tax imposed by title 26, a required VAT return or refund claim is treated as a return under that title, and a required VAT invoice is treated as a document required under that title. No regulation or administrative adaptation may create an offense, change an element or level of culpability, or increase a punishment.

(3) **Adaptation table.**—The Secretary shall publish a table of the applications authorized by paragraphs (1) and (2) not later than 18 months before the earliest candidate tax conversion date and update it for a material legal change not later than 1 year before each later candidate date. The table may clarify terminology but has no force to create tax liability, a penalty, an offense, an enforcement power, or a waiver of sovereign immunity.

**(d) Confidentiality and disclosure.**—Section 6103 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 applies to VAT returns and return information. Invoice-verification systems shall disclose to a purchaser only whether a registration number and invoice are valid and may not disclose unrelated return information.

**(e) Taxpayer protections.**—Before assessment, a person is entitled to notice of the factual and legal basis, access to nonprivileged material relied upon, an opportunity for administrative review, and the deficiency procedures incorporated under subsection (c). The Administration shall maintain independent appeals, taxpayer-assistance, and whistleblower functions. The Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration retains independent oversight of the Administration and may not be placed under the supervision of its head.

**(f) Regulations.**—The Secretary may prescribe regulations necessary to carry out this title and may delegate administrative authority to the Commissioner of the Value-Added Tax Administration. A regulation may not change a statutory rate or formula, expand a premium-rate category, create a domestic zero-rated category, or impose tax on an activity outside the statutory base.

**(g) Implementation appropriation and limits.**—Amounts made available to the Internal Revenue Service for tax administration before the date of enactment remain available, without fiscal year limitation, to the extent necessary for the implementation and preserved closing functions in this title. In addition, there are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury such sums as are necessary, before the tax conversion date and during the first 5 fiscal years beginning on or after that date, solely for startup systems, personnel training, taxpayer assistance, import coordination, invoice and payment testing, cybersecurity, fraud prevention, refund processing, and independent appeals under this title. An amount under this subsection may not be used to administer a tax terminated by section 301 except for a preserved pre-conversion matter, to enforce a function terminated elsewhere in this Act, or to expand the VAT base or rate. The Commissioner shall publish monthly obligations and outlays by function; the Inspector General shall audit them annually; and an unobligated balance expires at the end of the fifth such fiscal year. Every amount is a Federal outlay included in sections 204, 302(c), and 302(m).

**(h) Refund appropriation.**—There are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury amounts necessary to pay each refund expressly authorized by this title or section 602(b)(3), and interest lawfully payable on that refund, including an allowable input-VAT refund under section 302(f) and an audited cessation refund under section 302(n). A cessation refund may not exceed net VAT previously paid on the same special-sector activity and not previously credited or refunded. Each refund and associated interest are Federal outlays for purposes of every estimate and report under this Act. This subsection does not create a refund or interest entitlement not otherwise expressly authorized.

## TITLE IV—MONETARY POLICY REFORM

### SEC. 401. ABOLITION OF THE FEDERAL RESERVE SYSTEM

**(a) Repeal date.**—The Federal Reserve Act (12 U.S.C. 221 et seq.) is repealed effective on the date that is 3 years after the date of enactment of this Act (in this title referred to as the "monetary transition date"), subject to subsections (d) and (h) and except to the extent temporarily preserved under subsection (f) solely to complete an identified transition.

**(b) Monetary Transition Office.**—Not later than 90 days after the date of enactment, the Secretary of the Treasury shall establish a Monetary Transition Office. The Office shall prepare and execute the transfers under this section, protect records and information systems, coordinate with domestic and foreign payment operators, and publish monthly progress reports. The Board of Governors and the Federal Reserve Banks shall provide enforceable access to records, systems, personnel, and contracts; the Secretary may detail Federal personnel and enter contracts necessary for independent testing and secure transfer. Of unobligated Board assessment funds, not more than $2,000,000,000 is transferred to the Treasury and permanently appropriated, without fiscal year limitation, for the Office, subject to annual audit. The Office terminates 2 years after the final accounting under subsection (g), and no later than 10 years after the monetary transition date unless an Act of Congress extends it for identified unresolved claims.

**(c) Inventory and transition plan.**—Not later than 1 year before the monetary transition date, the Secretary, the Board of Governors, and the Federal Reserve Banks shall publish an independently audited inventory and a transaction-level transition plan covering—

(1) currency in circulation; reserve balances; term, foreign-official, international-organization, government-sponsored-enterprise, designated-financial-market-utility, joint, and other deposit liabilities; accrued interest through cutover; pending, daylight, and negative settlement positions; Treasury accounts; member-bank stock and setoffs; repurchase agreements; swap lines; capital; surplus; deferred assets; personnel; collective-bargaining agreements; pension and postretirement plans; and workers' compensation;

(2) Treasury securities, agency securities, mortgage-backed securities, loans, collateral, property pledged for Federal Reserve notes, customer and custodial property, securities beneficially owned by foreign or domestic account holders, real property, intellectual property, contracts, litigation, and contingent claims, with beneficial ownership and liens identified before any disposition;

(3) Fedwire Funds, Fedwire Securities, FedACH, FedNow, the National Settlement Service, check processing, settlement, fiscal-agent, securities issuance and redemption, cash distribution, and emergency-liquidity functions;

(4) supervision, examination, consumer-compliance, statistical, and international responsibilities arising under the Federal Reserve Act or another law, Board assessment funds, property or funds held for the Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection, and each authority that must be repealed, transferred, or terminated; and

(5) the receiving entity, legal authority, operational test, valuation method, counterparty treatment, and fallback procedure for each item.

**(d) Termination, claims, and disposition.**—On the monetary transition date, the Board of Governors and each Federal Reserve Bank are abolished. The Secretary shall administer the following order and may not sell or transfer an asset until ownership and priority are resolved:

(1) return customer, custodial, trust, and other property beneficially owned by a person other than a Federal Reserve entity, subject to a valid lien, and segregate property pledged for Federal Reserve notes or another secured claim;

(2) establish a public claims process with notice, a filing period of not less than 1 year, written priority determinations, and administrative review by an officer appointed in accordance with section 601(d) who was not involved in the initial determination; an appropriate United States district court has exclusive jurisdiction to review a final ownership, lien, priority, specific-property-return, or account-balance determination without regard to the amount of a payment incident to that determination and may award declaratory, injunctive, specific-restitution, and legally due monetary relief; the United States Court of Federal Claims has jurisdiction only over a standalone claim for money that does not require review of such a determination, and the United States waives sovereign immunity solely for the relief authorized by this paragraph;

(3) settle accepted payment orders and pending net positions as of a published cutover time and credit each holder of a Federal Reserve deposit liability, including a reserve, term, foreign-official, international-organization, government-sponsored-enterprise, designated-financial-market-utility, or joint-account balance and accrued interest through cutover, with the same dollar amount in a transferable account on the books of the Treasury;

(4) maintain the first and paramount lien of Federal Reserve notes under section 16 of the Federal Reserve Act (12 U.S.C. 414) and all specifically pledged collateral until the Treasury has lawfully assumed the notes and provided assets or the full faith and credit of the United States in substitution, at which time the lien may be released under a published accounting;

(5) satisfy perfected secured claims from their collateral and then satisfy deposit and payment-system liabilities, accrued employee compensation, funded and unfunded pension and postretirement obligations, and other valid contractual claims in the priority established by otherwise applicable law;

(6) redeem paid-in Federal Reserve Bank stock at par, plus an accrued statutory dividend through the monetary transition date, only after the claims in paragraphs (1) through (5) and net of a debt owed by the member to a Federal Reserve Bank;

(7) extinguish a Federal Reserve deferred asset without payment, because it represents only potential future Reserve Bank earnings, and cancel or reconcile a gold certificate or Special Drawing Right certificate held within the System before any underlying Treasury asset is used under section 403(b);

(8) honor, terminate, novate, or settle each remaining valid contract according to its terms and applicable law and sell remaining marketable assets gradually through transparent auctions or through the liquidating trust under paragraph (9); and

(9) establish any liquidating trust as a Treasury-controlled fiduciary with no authority to borrow, lend, create leverage, or acquire a new asset except in settlement; require conflict-of-interest rules, independently reviewed valuations, competitive sales, annual audits, and termination not later than 7 years after the monetary transition date unless an Act of Congress extends it.

An account created under paragraph (3) may not bear interest or be overdrawn, and its balance may be increased only by a transfer from another funded account, a deposit of legal tender, or a payment of the United States supported by lawful receipts or borrowing. Not later than 180 days before the monetary transition date, after reserving property and collateral required by paragraphs (1), (4), and (5), the Board and the Federal Reserve Banks shall identify and place cash and marketable Treasury securities into a segregated Treasury escrow or, if pre-cutover transfer of legal title would impair a preserved ownership interest, lien, or claim, grant the Treasury perfected control and an automatic transfer of the identified asset at the monetary transition date. After considering an advisory independent audit, the Secretary shall determine and certify the aggregate converted balances, the market value of the escrowed or controlled assets, and any shortfall. The permanent appropriation in subsection (i)(2) applies to the certified shortfall, but the monetary transition date may not occur unless lawful receipts, transferred assets, and borrowing within the debt limit provide capacity to fund the full amount. A postponement caused solely by insufficient debt-limit capacity is a funding delay separate from the operational delay under subsection (h). During a funding delay, the Federal Reserve entities continue solely as custodians and ministerial operators of existing payment, settlement, currency-distribution, securities-servicing, and fiscal-agent systems; all monetary-policy, discretionary-lending, emergency-facility, asset-purchase, new-commitment, and balance-sheet-expansion authority is suspended. The Secretary, the Board, and the Federal Reserve Banks shall publish quarterly updates to the inventory and plan under subsection (c). Not more than 30 days before the funding certification, they shall publish a refreshed inventory and transaction-level plan after considering an advisory independent audit or, if the Secretary determines that no material item has changed, a detailed certification to that effect supported by the advisory audit. After a funding delay, when the Secretary certifies that the funding condition has been satisfied, the rescheduled monetary transition date is the date that is 360 days after that certification, the operational-test period under subsection (h) begins, and the one-time delay limit in that subsection applies only after the certification. The Treasury shall close each converted account not later than 5 years after the monetary transition date by transferring its balance, at the holder's election, to legal tender to the extent available without violating subsection (e)(2), a Treasury security acquired at market value, or a lawful private settlement account accompanied by currency or a Treasury security of equal market value. No holder may require physical currency issuance in violation of the statutory cap, and equivalent Treasury securities shall be available when aggregate currency demand exceeds that cap. Only the excess certified under subsection (g) after satisfaction or adequate reserve for this subsection may be deposited in the general fund of the Treasury.

A converted balance is held for the beneficial owner, may not be offset against an unrelated tax, debt, or claim of the United States without the owner's consent or a final judgment, and may be seized only through otherwise applicable due process. Sections 3401 through 3423 of title 12, United States Code, apply to a converted account and its Treasury operator as if the operator were a financial institution, in addition to the firewall in section 601(c), except that an otherwise lawful sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counterterrorism, or foreign-official-account rule is not narrowed.

**(e) Currency and legal tender.**—

(1) United States coins and currency retain their status as legal tender under section 5103 of title 31, United States Code. Each outstanding Federal Reserve note remains an obligation of the United States, and the Treasury assumes direct responsibility for its administration on the monetary transition date. The note remains legal tender until retired, redeemed, or replaced under a published procedure.

(2) Notwithstanding section 5115(b) of title 31, United States Code, the Secretary may issue non-interest-bearing United States currency notes to replace retired Federal Reserve notes. The notes are obligations of the United States, payable to bearer, legal tender under section 5103 of that title, eligible to be held as a reserve asset, and subject to anti-counterfeiting, denomination, accounting, redemption, cancellation, and public-reporting standards prescribed by law and regulation. Unless an Act of Congress expressly provides otherwise, the combined face amount of Federal Reserve notes and replacement United States currency notes outstanding may not exceed the face amount of Federal Reserve notes outstanding at the close of the day before the monetary transition date. Section 5112(k) of title 31, United States Code, is repealed on the monetary transition date, and no coin denomination, bearer instrument, certificate, or unfunded book-entry credit may be used to evade the cap or funding rules in this section. A fully allocated commodity certificate under section 403(b) is not counted against the cap because it is a bailment claim to identified metal rather than an obligation payable from general revenues.

(3) Currency conversion does not change the dollar amount of a debt unless the parties validly agreed to a different unit of account. No authority in this subsection permits a discretionary loan, asset purchase, or open-market operation prohibited by this title.

**(f) Payment and fiscal-agent continuity.**—Before the monetary transition date, the Secretary shall designate the Treasury, a Treasury-controlled operator, or a competitively selected user-owned clearing utility as the successor operator for each system necessary to provide uninterrupted wholesale and retail payment, clearing, settlement, cash-distribution, Treasury-account, securities-servicing, and fiscal-agent services. A private operator acts only under a written Treasury contract; a Federal officer retains final authority over Federal funds, policy, access to Federal systems, and compliance review.

On the monetary transition date, the successor is substituted by operation of law for the Board or a Federal Reserve Bank under each applicable account agreement, payment order, security interest, fiscal-agent designation, and operating rule. Before that date, the successor shall publish replacement rules after notice and end-to-end legal and operational testing. Regulation J, applicable Federal Reserve operating circulars, the Treasury/Reserve Automated Debt Entry System rules, netting and settlement-finality rules, and rules governing Fedwire Funds, Fedwire Securities, FedACH, FedNow, and the National Settlement Service remain in force solely as temporary system rules to the extent a tested replacement rule is not yet effective. A temporary rule expires when the replacement takes effect and, in all events, when the last converted account under subsection (d)(3) closes or 5 years after the monetary transition date, whichever occurs first, unless an Act of Congress provides a shorter or longer identified period. Temporary rules and contracts may not authorize monetary policy, open-market operations, discretionary lending, Federal credit, or an expansion of participants' substantive liability beyond the pre-transition rule.

Except for the Treasury acting solely with respect to its own accounts or a fiscal-agent function, each successor operator shall offer objective, nondiscriminatory, cost-based access to a lawful issuer that satisfies published operational, collateral, liquidity, cybersecurity, and loss-allocation requirements. A denial or suspension shall state the specific reason. An aggrieved lawful issuer may elect prompt arbitration under an applicable participation contract or, not later than 90 days after notice, bring a civil action in an appropriate United States district court for declaratory and injunctive relief. The court shall determine whether the published requirement was satisfied and whether the denial or suspension was consistent with this paragraph. The United States waives sovereign immunity solely for that equitable relief, and no damages are authorized against the United States or a private operator under this paragraph. This paragraph does not require Federal credit, access for an unlawful issuer, or relaxation of a requirement reasonably necessary to protect settlement finality or system security.

A Treasury-controlled successor may operate commercial interbank or retail payment and settlement functions only during a funding delay under subsection (d), the fallback period authorized by subsection (h), or an orderly migration. Not later than 5 years after the monetary transition date, the Secretary shall transfer those commercial functions through an open competitive process to a user-owned clearing utility or to two or more operationally independent private operators. The Treasury may retain its own accounts, public-debt issuance and redemption, disbursement, collection, and other fiscal-agent functions. A private successor receives no sovereign, regulatory, taxing, monetary-policy, or lawmaking authority, and a further period of Treasury operation requires an Act of Congress identifying the function, necessity, duration, and migration milestones.

**(g) Audit, accounting, and congressional notice.**—Notwithstanding section 714(b) of title 31, United States Code, section 3910(a)(3) of title 12, United States Code, or another audit limitation enacted before this Act, the Comptroller General may audit any transaction, communication, account, or record to the extent necessary for the transition and liquidation under this section. The Comptroller General shall protect classified information, personal information, trade secrets, and sensitive foreign-counterparty information under otherwise applicable law and may delay public disclosure without withholding the information from the congressional committees of jurisdiction.

The Secretary shall publish a proposed final accounting not later than 180 days after the claims filing period closes and each liquidating trust has disposed of its last asset, identifying every payment, reserve for an unresolved timely claim, and proposed residual transfer. The Comptroller General shall review the inventory, valuations, operational tests, asset sales, claims, contract settlements, information-security controls, and final accounting and report advisory findings to Congress and the Secretary. The Secretary shall make the operative certification that liabilities are adequately reserved, issue the final accounting, and identify the excess, if any, transferable to the general fund after considering that report; if the Comptroller General does not report within 90 days after receiving a complete proposed final accounting, the Secretary may act on an independent public audit. The Secretary may not materially depart from the published plan without giving Congress and affected counterparties at least 60 days' notice describing the reason, legal authority, and expected fiscal effect.

**(h) Limited operational delay.**—Not later than 180 days before the then-scheduled monetary transition date, after giving effect to any funding delay under subsection (d) but determined without giving effect to a delay under this subsection, the Comptroller General shall report whether the replacement systems for currency issuance, converted-account balances, Treasury accounts, Fedwire Funds, Fedwire Securities, FedACH, FedNow, the National Settlement Service, cash distribution, and securities servicing have passed end-to-end operational and cybersecurity tests. After considering the report and the underlying test evidence, the Secretary shall determine whether a material deficiency is reasonably likely to interrupt a core payment or Treasury function and may delay the monetary transition date once for not more than 12 months. If the Comptroller General does not timely report, the Secretary shall base the determination on an independent test by an operator other than the proposed successor. The Secretary shall publish each deficiency, corrective milestone, and monthly test result and may continue only those Federal Reserve functions strictly necessary to correct the deficiency and maintain service. If a material deficiency remains at the end of the delay, the Federal Reserve entities and all monetary-policy and lending authority still terminate, but the Secretary may transfer the necessary personnel, hardware, software, contracts, and operating procedures directly to a Treasury-controlled successor and operate the inherited system under subsection (f) solely for ministerial payment, settlement, securities-servicing, cash-distribution, and fiscal-agent continuity for not more than 2 additional years. Any further extension requires an Act of Congress identifying the system, deficiency, duration, and prohibition on Federal credit.

**(i) Funding.**—

(1) The Secretary shall establish in the Treasury a Federal Reserve Liquidation Fund. All assets and proceeds transferred under subsection (d), other than property returned to its beneficial owner, shall be credited to the Fund and are permanently appropriated, without fiscal year limitation, solely to administer the claims process, operate a liquidating trust, and satisfy the liabilities and transition costs authorized by subsections (d) and (f).

(2) To the extent the Fund is insufficient when a preserved obligation is due, including a converted-balance shortfall certified under subsection (d), there are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury amounts necessary to satisfy that obligation. Each use of the backstop shall be reported to Congress and recorded as a Federal outlay and debt effect and may not be excluded from a fiscal estimate under this Act.

(3) In addition to the capped appropriation in subsection (b), there are authorized to be appropriated such sums as are necessary for independent audits, operational testing, contract transition, and secure transfer of systems and records. An amount certified as excess under subsection (g) shall be transferred to the general fund and is not available for expenditure under this section.

(4) A converted balance under subsection (d)(3) shall be reported as a gross liability of the United States and may not be netted against a transferred asset in a public balance sheet or fiscal estimate. Not later than 1 year before the monetary transition date, the Secretary shall report to Congress the aggregate converted balances, the assets and lawful receipts available to fund them, the Treasury securities expected to be issued when accounts close, and any debt-limit capacity required. Nothing in this section authorizes issuance of an obligation under chapter 31 of title 31, United States Code, above the limit otherwise provided by law.

**(j) Interim balance-sheet restraint.**—From the date of enactment through the monetary transition date, the Federal Reserve System may conduct only operations necessary for ordinary payment settlement, currency distribution, performance of an existing contract, and an orderly runoff. It may not establish a new emergency facility or foreign swap line, acquire a new class of asset, increase the aggregate par value of System Open Market Account holdings above the amount held on the date of enactment, or increase aggregate loans and other credit above that amount except for a temporary intraday settlement advance fully collateralized and extinguished by the end of the operating day. Maturing Treasury securities may be replaced without increasing aggregate par value; other assets shall run off absent a written finding that a temporary replacement is strictly necessary to prevent failure of a core payment system. Each transaction and finding under this subsection shall be reported monthly.

**(k) Disposition of related Federal banking authorities.**—On the monetary transition date—

(1) the Bank Holding Company Act of 1956 (12 U.S.C. 1841 et seq.), section 10 of the Home Owners' Loan Act (12 U.S.C. 1467a), title I of the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (12 U.S.C. 5311 et seq.), title II of that Act (12 U.S.C. 5381 et seq.), and title VIII of that Act (12 U.S.C. 5461 et seq.) are repealed, except to the extent necessary to complete a receivership commenced before that date without assistance to an uninsured creditor;

(2) notwithstanding subsection (a), a corporation organized under section 25A of the Federal Reserve Act (12 U.S.C. 611 et seq.) continues automatically on that date as a Federal free-banking association under section 402(c), with the same juridical identity, property, contracts, debts, liens, offices, and pending proceedings. A Federal branch or agency licensed under the International Banking Act of 1978 (12 U.S.C. 3101 et seq.) may continue as an office of its foreign bank if, not later than 180 days before the monetary transition date, the foreign bank publicly identifies an agent for service, consents to jurisdiction in a United States district court for claims arising from the office, and makes the disclosures required by section 402(b). Neither continuation requires State permission or Federal prudential approval. Provisions of those Acts establishing or regulating the former Federal form are repealed 3 years after the monetary transition date, except as needed to resolve a pre-transition claim; and

(3) a duty assigned by another preexisting law to the Board of Governors, a Federal Reserve Bank, the Comptroller of the Currency, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, the National Credit Union Administration, or the Financial Stability Oversight Council terminates unless this Act expressly preserves the duty for payment-system operation, conversion, a protected insurance claim, liquidation, or receivership. A reference to one of those entities does not transfer a supervisory, lending, guarantee, or bailout authority by implication.

### SEC. 402. ESTABLISHMENT OF FREE BANKING SYSTEM

**(a) Affirmative right of free entry.**—

(1) On and after the monetary transition date, any natural person or legal entity, acting alone or in voluntary association, may engage in covered banking activity without obtaining a Federal, State, territorial, or local bank, depository-institution, money-transmission, stored-value, payment-stablecoin, virtual-currency, trust-company, lending, branching, or similar charter, license, franchise, permit, approval, or certificate of public convenience and necessity. The person may operate in an ordinary corporate, cooperative, partnership, trust, limited-liability, nonprofit, or unincorporated form available under otherwise applicable entity law.

(2) The affirmative right in paragraph (1) includes the right to—

(A) accept money, commodities, securities, digital assets, or other property under a contract of deposit, debt, bailment, custody, trust, or investment;\
(B) make loans, discount obligations, invest, and agree on interest, maturity, collateral, and repayment terms;\
(C) create, issue, circulate, transfer, and redeem physical or electronic notes, coins, tokens, checks, drafts, ledger balances, payment accounts, stable-value instruments, variable-value instruments, warehouse receipts, and other payment media in any lawful unit of account;\
(D) provide custody, exchange, transfer, clearing, settlement, and payment services; and\
(E) obtain private insurance, join a voluntary clearinghouse, and contract for liquidity or loss sharing without access to Federal credit.

(3) "Covered banking activity" means an activity described in paragraph (2) that uses a channel or instrumentality of interstate or foreign commerce, is offered to or for persons in more than one State, produces an instrument intended or reasonably expected to circulate or settle across a State or national boundary, accesses a successor system under section 401(f), or occurs in a territory or possession of the United States. A State may govern activity whose issuer, customers, instruments, operations, and effects are wholly confined to that State, but may not prohibit a person in the State from holding, accepting, transferring, or enforcing an instrument lawfully issued in covered banking activity.

(4) No governmental authority or clearing operator may prescribe a reserve ratio, reserve asset, capital amount, portfolio composition, maturity structure, branching limit, ownership limit, activity limit, interest rate, or unit of account for covered banking activity, except that a voluntary clearing operator may require collateral or liquidity reasonably limited to the operator's settlement exposure under subsection (e), and a legacy insurer may protect its fixed runoff exposure under subsection (d). Reserve, redemption, priority, and maturity terms otherwise arise solely from contract and property law.

(5) This section does not require a person to accept a private instrument, make a loan, extend credit, or join a clearinghouse; make a private instrument legal tender; confer a governmental guarantee; or displace a generally applicable law concerning capacity to contract, entity formation, agency, contract, fraud, theft, conversion, fiduciary duty, property, secured transactions, insolvency, taxation not discriminatory under subsection (f), sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counterterrorism, or cybersecurity trespass. Such a law may not be applied as a disguised charter, prior-approval, reserve, capital, portfolio, or activity requirement for covered banking activity.

(6) In this section, the term "covered monetary instrument" means a physical or electronic, bearer or registered note, coin, token, check, draft, ledger credit, deposit debt, bailment or warehouse claim, stable-value instrument, variable-value instrument, or other transferable claim intended or commonly used as a medium of exchange, unit of account, or settlement asset. The unit may be defined by United States or foreign currency, a commodity or basket, a cryptographic or other digital asset, a price index, a formula, or another objectively determinable measure. The "issuer" is the person that undertakes the instrument's payment, redemption, custody, or account liability; merely publishing software, validating transactions, communicating messages, or providing a noncustodial transfer mechanism does not make a person the issuer.

**(b) Contract, disclosure, and property rules.**—

(1) Before accepting value in covered banking activity, an issuer shall make available to the customer a concise human-readable and machine-readable contract stating—

(A) the issuer's legal name, form, principal place of business, agent for service of process, and selected governing law;\
(B) whether the instrument or account is a debt, demand debt, time debt, bailment, warehouse receipt, trust, equity interest, or other claim;\
(C) the unit of account and the redemption asset, rate or valuation method, place or mechanism, timing, fees, defenses, and any contractually permitted suspension;\
(D) the reserve assets, custody and audit arrangements, leverage, material maturity mismatch, security interest, subordination, and priority of claims, or a clear statement that no asset reserve is promised;\
(E) whether governmental or private insurance applies and, if private insurance applies, its limit, exclusions, and responsible insurer;\
(F) the law governing the instrument, the forum or arbitration procedure for a dispute, and the insolvency or receivership regime the issuer expects to apply; and\
(G) the most recent independent attestation required by paragraph (4).

(2) A physical or electronic payment instrument need not reproduce the entire contract if it clearly identifies the private issuer, states that the instrument is not United States legal tender or government guaranteed, identifies the redemption unit, and provides a durable means of obtaining the governing contract. The identified terms travel with the instrument and are enforceable by and against each transferee according to ordinary holder, assignment, negotiability, and property law; an issuer may not reserve a secret term against a secondary holder. An issuer may not use a name, seal, portrait, legend, size, design, or marketing statement reasonably calculated to make the instrument appear to be issued, legal tender, insured, or guaranteed by the United States or another government.

(3) Property described as a bailment, custody asset, warehouse deposit, or fully allocated or fully reserved claim shall be identified individually or as a disclosed fungible pro rata share, segregated from the issuer's own property, and free of an undisclosed lien or use right. The issuer may not lend, pledge, invest, rehypothecate, or otherwise use that property without the owner's express contract authorization. Compliant property remains the customer's property and is not property of the issuer or its insolvency estate. A debt claim, including a fractionally reserved demand claim, may use or invest proceeds and need not maintain a governmental reserve ratio, but the contract shall state conspicuously that the holder is a creditor, that redemption depends on the issuer's performance and solvency, and that the claim is not a bailment or government-insured deposit.

(4) An issuer that represents that assets support an instrument or account shall obtain and publish at least quarterly an independent attestation of the existence, custody, encumbrance, valuation method, and aggregate value of those assets and the aggregate amount and priority of corresponding claims. The attestor may not be controlled by, share profits with, or hold a material financial interest in the issuer and shall use observable market prices or disclose the method and uncertainty of another valuation. An issuer shall promptly publish a material impairment, redemption suspension, breach of the promised reserve terms, change in claim priority, or change in governing insolvency law. An issuer that makes no representation of asset support shall instead publish that fact and, at least annually, the aggregate amount and priority of outstanding monetary claims; the annual statement need not be independently attested.

(5) A customer injured by a knowing or reckless material misstatement, misclassification, commingling, unauthorized use of customer property, or omission required by this subsection has an express civil action for rescission, return of specific property, actual damages, and appropriate equitable relief in a court of competent jurisdiction. The action shall be filed not later than 4 years after discovery and 6 years after the violation. A substantially prevailing customer may recover reasonable attorney's fees and costs. A contractual waiver of liability for intentional or reckless conduct is void, and this remedy does not displace a more protective generally applicable remedy.

**(c) Federal charter and deposit-insurance transition.**—

(1) **Optional Federal free-banking association.**—

(A) One or more persons may form a Federal free-banking association by filing articles with the Secretary of the Treasury. The articles shall state the association's name; principal office; agent and address for service; governance method; ordinary State, territorial, Tribal, or foreign entity law selected to govern internal affairs; owner-liability rule under that law; classes of ownership or membership interest; and whether the association elects the uniform liquidation option in subsection (j). The articles may state the claim types and banking activities initially contemplated, but a change in activity does not require prior approval.

(B) Filing is ministerial and effective when a complete signed record and a cost-based filing fee are received. The Secretary shall time-stamp and publish the record not later than 2 business days after receipt and has no authority to decide whether the association is needed, safe, sound, adequately capitalized, convenient, competitive, or in the public interest. The Secretary may reject only an incomplete record, a duplicate or deceptively governmental name, or a designation of internal-affairs law that is not available to an ordinary entity with a substantial relationship to the designated jurisdiction, and shall identify the curable defect in writing not later than 5 business days after receipt. If the Secretary does not act by that deadline, filing is deemed complete at the original time of receipt. An applicant may obtain mandamus, declaratory, or injunctive relief and reasonable attorney's fees for a violation of this subparagraph; the United States waives sovereign immunity solely for that relief.

(C) An association formed under this paragraph is a body corporate with perpetual succession and power to sue and be sued, contract, own and transfer property, establish offices, exercise fiduciary powers it validly assumes, and engage nationwide in every activity protected by subsection (a). Its internal affairs and owner liability are governed by the ordinary entity law designated in its articles, as that law changes from time to time, except that Federal law governs formation, the monetary rights in this section, and dissolution under subsection (j). A designation may not alter the property rights of a nonconsenting third party or the law governing perfection and priority of a security interest.

(D) A Federal free-banking association is not an agency or instrumentality of the United States, receives no sovereign immunity, tax preference, monopoly, Federal guarantee, deposit insurance, or access to Federal credit, and is not subject to Federal supervision merely because of its Federal form. It shall state conspicuously in each public offering and account contract that it is privately owned and not insured, guaranteed, or approved by the United States. The ministerial registry shall have no examination, subpoena, rulemaking, enforcement, or receivership authority.

(E) The Federal form is optional. A natural person, unincorporated association, or entity formed under other generally applicable law may exercise the same substantive rights under subsection (a) without filing under this paragraph, and a State may not condition those rights on use of the Federal form.

(F) The articles shall state a good-faith intent to begin covered banking activity not later than 1 year after filing. Federal formation automatically lapses if the association neither begins such activity within that year nor obtains from a United States district court a time-limited extension for a concrete operational delay. Lapse does not extinguish property, a contract, or a claim; the organization continues, to the extent possible, as an ordinary entity or unincorporated association under the internal-affairs law selected in its articles. An association may re-form prospectively by a new ministerial filing. This subparagraph does not condition the substantive right of a natural person or non-Federal entity under subsection (a).

(2) **Automatic continuation of existing institutions.**—A national bank or Federal savings association existing on the monetary transition date continues automatically, without State approval or a break in legal existence, as a Federal free-banking association under paragraph (1). Its charter and bylaws are treated as its filed articles until amended, and the law of the jurisdiction containing its principal office governs internal affairs to the same extent as for a comparable ordinary entity. The successor is the same juridical person and retains by operation of law all property, contracts, deposits, debts, liens, branches, fiduciary appointments, ownership interests, priorities, and pending proceedings. It may instead convert voluntarily to a State or other lawful form with the authorization required by that form, or liquidate under subsection (i) or (j). No failure to convert causes forced liquidation. During the 3 years after the monetary transition date an institution may continue an existing corporate name if every note, account agreement, branch, and website conspicuously disclaims current Federal insurance or guarantee; after that period its name may not use "national", "United States", or another term implying governmental status except the authorized designation "Federal free-banking association".

At the end of that 3-year period, the Act of June 3, 1864 (chapter 106; 13 Stat. 99), commonly known as the "National Bank Act," provisions derived from that Act (principally 12 U.S.C. 21 et seq.), and section 5 of the Home Owners' Loan Act (12 U.S.C. 1464) are repealed to the extent they establish, charter, privilege, or prudentially regulate a national banking association or Federal savings association, except as needed to resolve a pre-transition claim or receivership. The repeal does not terminate or impair the juridical existence preserved by this paragraph. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency is abolished after the articles and records of each active institution have transferred to the ministerial registry and remaining receivership records and duties have transferred to a separate Treasury liquidation office. Until then, the Comptroller retains only authority necessary to record a continuation or voluntary conversion, commence a receivership based on pre-transition law, and resolve a pre-transition matter. The liquidation office may not operate a bank, extend credit, charter or supervise an issuer, or commence a new receivership after the runoff period and terminates after the last transferred receivership closes.

(3) No new Federal deposit-insurance commitment may be issued on or after the monetary transition date. The Federal Deposit Insurance Act (12 U.S.C. 1811 et seq.) is repealed on the date that is 5 years after the monetary transition date, except that provisions strictly necessary to resolve a failure occurring before that date and honor a protected insured amount remain in force for that limited purpose. The Corporation shall determine each depositor's protected insured amount at each insured depository institution as of the close of the day before the monetary transition date under the ownership-category aggregation, accrued-interest, and limit rules in section 11 of that Act (12 U.S.C. 1821). The amount may not increase and declines to 80 percent on the first anniversary of the monetary transition date, 60 percent on the second, 40 percent on the third, 20 percent on the fourth, and zero on the fifth. For a failed institution, the percentage applicable when a receiver is appointed or the institution is closed is fixed for that claim and does not decline during adjudication or payment. Under tracing rules that prevent duplication, a depositor may transfer the remaining protected amount with a deposit to an institution that enters a binding runoff agreement subjecting that amount to the preserved assessment, examination, corrective-action, and receivership authorities. An institution not insured immediately before the monetary transition date shall also place eligible collateral having a market value at least equal to the transferred protected amount under the Corporation's perfected first-priority control and replenish the collateral after a material decline. The transfer creates no new or increased protected amount.

(4) The Secretary of the Treasury shall abolish the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation after determining, on the basis of a public independent audit and after considering any report of its Chairperson, that all preserved receiverships and insurance claims have been resolved or are fully reserved and assigned to a lawful successor custodian. If that determination has not been made by the date that is 10 years after the monetary transition date, remaining claims, reserves, records, and custodial duties shall transfer to a separate Treasury liquidation office, and the Corporation shall then be abolished. The liquidation office has no authority to issue new insurance or assistance and terminates when the last preserved claim is resolved.

(5) For purposes of this section, "eligible collateral" means United States coins or currency, a direct obligation of the United States, or another readily marketable debt or security that has a transparent independently verifiable price, is not issued by the pledging institution or an affiliate, and is held by an independent custodian subject to the applicable insurer's perfected first-priority control. Market value shall be determined after a published conservative haircut reflecting duration, volatility, liquidity, credit, and concentration risk. The insurer may reject or require replacement of an asset that no longer satisfies this paragraph but may not use collateral eligibility to impose a general portfolio mandate unrelated to preserved insurance exposure.

**(d) No Federal guarantee or bailout.**—On and after the monetary transition date, except for a protected insured amount under subsection (c)(3) or (h)(3), a private note, deposit, account, clearinghouse obligation, or reserve asset is not guaranteed by the United States. During runoff, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation and the National Credit Union Administration may collect existing assessments, examine solely to protect preserved insurance exposure, act as receiver, pay a protected claim, and use the least-cost sale or transfer of assets necessary to resolve a failed institution. The systemic-risk exception, support for an uninsured creditor, a new guarantee, and open-institution assistance have no effect. No other Federal officer may lend to, purchase the assets of, or guarantee an issuer for the purpose of preventing its failure, except under an Act of Congress that identifies the recipient, maximum amount, consideration, duration, and source of budget authority.

Solely to protect the fixed and declining exposure under subsection (c)(3) or (h)(3), the applicable insurer may, on an institution-specific written finding of a material risk of asset dissipation, require corrective action, segregation of eligible collateral, or restriction of a dividend or affiliate transfer reasonably necessary to protect that exposure. This authority may not support an uninsured creditor, impose a generalized portfolio requirement, increase a protected amount, or continue after the protected amount reaches zero.

The Deposit Insurance Fund and the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund, respectively, shall be the first source for a protected insured amount and the reasonable cost of resolving the associated failed institution. To the extent the applicable fund is insufficient when such a fixed legacy obligation is due, there are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury amounts necessary to pay that obligation, but not an uninsured claim, open-institution assistance, or a protected amount greater than subsection (c)(3) or (h)(3) allows. A recovery, assessment, or collateral realization attributable to a payment from the general fund shall be returned to that fund. Each use and expected recovery shall be reported as a gross Federal outlay, receipt, and debt effect and included in every fiscal estimate under this Act. Nothing in this paragraph authorizes borrowing above the limit otherwise provided by chapter 31 of title 31, United States Code.

**(e) Voluntary clearing, access, and payment finality.**—

(1) The Department of the Treasury may facilitate, but may not require participation in, one or more voluntary clearinghouses for payment and interbank settlement. A clearinghouse shall publish membership, messaging, collateral, liquidity, loss-allocation, default, settlement-finality, operational-resilience, cybersecurity, and wind-down rules and shall have no access to Federal credit.

(2) For this section and section 401(f), a "lawful issuer" is a person exercising the right in subsection (a) that is not subject to a final judgment enjoining the relevant activity for fraud, theft, sanctions evasion, or another generally applicable offense. Lawful status does not depend on a governmental charter, license, insurance, reserve ratio, capital amount, domicile, technology, or unit of account.

(3) A clearinghouse or successor operator shall offer direct access or a commercially reasonable sponsored-access path on nondiscriminatory, cost-based terms to a lawful issuer that satisfies published technical, cybersecurity, collateral, liquidity, and loss-allocation requirements. A collateral or liquidity requirement shall be based on measurable settlement exposure to that operator, may apply a disclosed prudent stress margin, and may not regulate the issuer's assets or liabilities beyond property pledged to cover that exposure. Access may not be denied or burdened because of the issuer's form, domicile, lack of governmental insurance, reserve asset, reserve ratio, instrument technology, or lawful denomination. A denial or suspension shall identify the unmet rule and supporting facts.

(4) An aggrieved lawful issuer may elect prompt arbitration only under an actual participation agreement or, not later than 90 days after notice, bring a civil action in an appropriate United States district court for declaratory and injunctive relief. The court shall determine compliance with the published rules and this subsection. The United States waives sovereign immunity solely for that equitable relief. No damages are authorized against the United States or a private operator by this paragraph. A State or private person may establish or recognize another reciprocal clearing system consistent with this section.

(5) A transfer or multilateral net obligation that becomes final under published clearing rules before commencement of an insolvency proceeding remains final and enforceable notwithstanding a subsequent stay, avoidance power, or receivership, except to the extent the transfer was unauthorized, resulted from operational error, or was made with actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud creditors. This paragraph protects ordinary-course payment and settlement finality and does not restore a derivatives, repurchase-agreement, swap, commodity, securities-contract, or master-netting safe harbor eliminated by subsection (i).

(6) For purposes of sections 4401 through 4407 of title 12, United States Code, a lawful issuer is a financial institution and a clearinghouse operating under this subsection is a clearing organization without regard to a Federal Reserve, Securities and Exchange Commission, Commodity Futures Trading Commission, or other governmental designation. Those sections do not authorize Federal credit, supervision, or a priority broader than paragraph (5).

**(f) State law, choice of law, and enforcement.**—

(1) A State, territory, possession, political subdivision, or Tribal government may not prohibit, condition on prior permission, or impose a special burden on covered banking activity because a person issues, offers, sells, acquires, holds, transfers, redeems, backs, clears, or settles a private monetary instrument; accepts a debt deposit or bailment; uses the word "bank" without implying governmental status; operates a payment account; establishes an office or branch; transmits its own liability; selects a lawful reserve asset or ratio; or agrees on interest, maturity, redemption, or a unit of account. A law imposing a banking, money-transmission, stored-value, payment-stablecoin, virtual-currency, trust-company, lender, branching, ownership, reserve, capital, portfolio, activity, rate, or public-convenience requirement is preempted as applied to covered banking activity.

(2) Paragraph (1) does not preempt ordinary entity formation and foreign-entity registration; an agent-for-service requirement; contract, agency, fraud, theft, conversion, tort, fiduciary-duty, property, secured-transactions, escheat, or insolvency law of general application; neutral zoning or building rules for a physical office; or a tax that applies at the same rate and on the same base to economically comparable property, income, consumption, or business activity without regard to issuer status, instrument denomination, backing, or technology. A law is not generally applicable merely because it applies to every bank, depository institution, money transmitter, currency issuer, payment instrument, or virtual-currency business. A saved law may not be administered as a discretionary entry review or disguised reserve, capital, portfolio, or activity rule.

(3) A disclosed choice of governing law in a contract for covered banking activity is enforceable if the chosen jurisdiction has a substantial relationship to a party or transaction. Absent an effective choice, the law of the issuer's disclosed principal place of business governs the contract and the issuer's internal affairs. This paragraph does not displace this Act, a nonwaivable saved fraud or property rule, or the law governing perfection and priority against a nonconsenting third party. A change of governing law, redemption unit, claim type, or priority does not bind an existing holder without that holder's affirmative assent.

(4) A holder may bring the civil action created by subsection (b)(5), including an action for promised redemption, in a United States district court without regard to amount in controversy or citizenship, or in a State court of competent jurisdiction. Federal and State courts have concurrent jurisdiction, except that a State-court action may be removed under section 1441 of title 28, United States Code. An agreement to arbitrate is enforceable only against a holder who actually assented to it and does not bar provisional relief to preserve customer property.

(5) A person subject to actual or imminent governmental enforcement inconsistent with this section may bring an action against the responsible officer in the officer's official capacity for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief in the United States district court for the district in which the plaintiff resides or does business. The United States waives sovereign immunity solely for that relief; this paragraph recognizes prospective relief against a State officer to the extent permitted by the Constitution and does not authorize damages against a government. A substantially prevailing plaintiff may recover reasonable attorney's fees and costs unless the governmental position was substantially justified or special circumstances make an award unjust.

**(g) Inconsistent Federal requirements and conforming law.**—

(1) On and after the monetary transition date, a Federal law enacted before this Act has no effect to the extent it imposes a charter, license, deposit-insurance, reserve, capital, portfolio, branching, ownership, activity, interest-rate, denomination, or prior-approval requirement solely because a private person engages in covered banking activity. This paragraph does not terminate a receivership or protected insurance claim, impair a pre-transition contract, or displace a generally applicable law preserved by subsection (a)(5).

(2) Section 21 of the Banking Act of 1933 (12 U.S.C. 378) is repealed on the monetary transition date.

(3) On the monetary transition date, Public Law 119-27, known as the Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act or the GENIUS Act, sections 5901 through 5916 of title 12, United States Code, and each statutory note, effective-date rule, regulation, order, and guidance issued under that public law are repealed. Each amendment made by that public law to section 13104(a)(3) of title 5; section 1a(9) of title 7; sections 101, 362, 507, 541, and 1109 of title 11; section 1(b)(3) of title 12; and sections 77b, 78c, 78lll, 80a-2, 80a-3, and 80b-2 of title 15 is reversed, and each provision shall read as it would have read if Public Law 119-27 had not been enacted, subject to an amendment enacted independently of that public law. A right, liability, penalty, forfeiture, action, proceeding, final order, or private contract arising before the monetary transition date remains governed by the law applicable when it arose, but no permission, reserve, capital, liquidity, activity, interest, issuer-status, priority, or estate exclusion created by that public law applies to an instrument issued or materially expanded on or after that date.

(4) Section 336 of title 18, United States Code, is repealed. Making, issuing, circulating, uttering, passing, transferring, and attempting to make, issue, circulate, utter, pass, or transfer a covered monetary instrument under this section are "authorized by law" for purposes of sections 486 and 491 of title 18. This paragraph does not authorize a counterfeit, deceptive resemblance to governmental money, vending-machine fraud, or a false claim of legal-tender or governmental status.

(5) Section 1960 of title 18, United States Code, is amended—

(A) in the section heading, subsection (a), and each other place the term appears, by striking "unlicensed money transmitting business" and inserting "prohibited money transmitting business";

(B) by striking subsection (b)(1) and inserting:

> "(1) the term 'prohibited money transmitting business' means a money transmitting business that affects interstate or foreign commerce and knowingly—
>
> (A) fails to comply with the Federal registration requirement in section 5330 of title 31 or a regulation prescribed under that section; or
>
> (B) transports or transmits funds known by the defendant to be derived from a criminal offense or intended to promote or support unlawful activity;"; and

(C) in the table of sections for chapter 95, by striking the item relating to section 1960 and inserting "1960. Prohibition of prohibited money transmitting businesses.".

Absence of a State license is not an element or predicate under section 1960. Section 5330(a)(3) of title 31, United States Code, is repealed. For purposes of sections 5312 and 5330 of title 31 and section 1960 of title 18, issuing, redeeming, or recording transfer on the issuer's own books of the issuer's own liability for its own account is not money transmission solely by reason of that conduct; accepting or transmitting a third party's property as custodian or intermediary remains subject to otherwise applicable registration, reporting, sanctions, and criminal-proceeds law.

(6) A covered monetary instrument issued by a private person is not an obligation or security of the United States for purposes of section 8 of title 18, United States Code, or legal tender under section 5103 of title 31, United States Code. For purposes of sections 513 and 514 of title 18, a "security" includes a physical or electronic covered monetary instrument issued by an organization or other person in or intended for interstate or foreign commerce. Publication of noncustodial software, validation of a public ledger, or transfer through a self-hosted wallet does not by itself make a person the issuer or create money-transmitter status. Sections 471 through 485 of title 18 continue to protect obligations and coin of the United States, and section 709 of that title continues to prohibit a deceptive claim of governmental status.

(7) For purposes of section 335 of title 18, United States Code, an institution automatically continued under subsection (c)(2) or (h)(1) is not an expired corporation, and a private obligation lawfully reissued by that successor is not an obligation of an expired corporation. This paragraph does not validate circulation of an obligation after the issuer and every lawful successor have dissolved.

**(h) Federal credit unions and share insurance.**—

(1) A Federal credit union existing on the monetary transition date continues automatically, without State approval or a break in legal existence, as a cooperative Federal free-banking association under subsection (c)(1). Its charter, bylaws, membership rules, and field-of-membership provisions are treated as its filed articles until amended, except that no former field-of-membership restriction limits entry or activity after that date. The successor is the same juridical person and retains by operation of law all property, contracts, shares, deposits, debts, liens, branches, ownership and voting rights, priorities, and pending proceedings. Its cooperative governance and owner liability remain governed by its bylaws and the ordinary cooperative law of the jurisdiction containing its principal office, subject to this section. It may voluntarily convert to a State cooperative or another lawful form with the authorization required by that form, or liquidate under subsection (i) or (j); no failure to obtain State authorization requires liquidation.

(2) At the end of the 3-year period beginning on the monetary transition date, title I of the Federal Credit Union Act (12 U.S.C. 1752 et seq.) is repealed to the extent it establishes, charters, privileges, imposes a field of membership on, or prudentially regulates a Federal credit union, except as needed to resolve a pre-transition claim or liquidation. The repeal does not terminate or impair the juridical existence preserved by paragraph (1). On the monetary transition date, the National Credit Union Administration and its Board retain no chartering, field-of-membership, capital, reserve, portfolio, branching, activity, or general examination authority over a continuing cooperative; their authority is limited to the fixed legacy share-insurance exposure, Central Liquidity Facility runoff, and a liquidation commenced before that date or elected voluntarily by a cooperative.

(3) No new Federal share-insurance commitment may be issued on or after the monetary transition date. Title II of the Federal Credit Union Act (12 U.S.C. 1781 et seq.) is repealed on the date that is 5 years after the monetary transition date, except that provisions strictly necessary to resolve a failure occurring before that date and honor a protected insured amount remain in force for that limited purpose. The Board shall determine each member's protected insured amount at each insured credit union as of the close of the day before the monetary transition date under the ownership-category aggregation, accrued-dividend, and limit rules in section 207 of that Act (12 U.S.C. 1787). The amount may not increase and declines under the percentages and anniversaries in subsection (c)(3). For a failed credit union, the percentage applicable when a liquidating agent is appointed or the credit union is closed is fixed for that claim and does not decline during adjudication or payment. Under tracing rules that prevent duplication, a member may transfer the remaining protected amount with an account to a credit union that enters a binding runoff agreement subjecting that amount to the preserved assessment, examination, corrective-action, and receivership authorities. A credit union not insured immediately before the monetary transition date shall also place eligible collateral having a market value at least equal to the transferred protected amount under the Board's perfected first-priority control and replenish the collateral after a material decline. The transfer creates no new or increased protected amount.

(4) On the monetary transition date, the National Credit Union Central Liquidity Facility may make no new advance, loan, or credit commitment. Title III of the Federal Credit Union Act (12 U.S.C. 1795 et seq.) is repealed 3 years after that date, except as necessary to collect an outstanding asset, satisfy a valid liability, and return capital stock or deposits according to lawful priority.

(5) The Secretary of the Treasury shall abolish the National Credit Union Administration and its Board after determining, on the basis of a public independent audit and after considering any report of the Board, that all continuation records, Central Liquidity Facility assets and liabilities, liquidations, and preserved insurance claims have been resolved, transferred, or fully reserved and assigned to a lawful successor custodian. If that determination has not been made by the date that is 10 years after the monetary transition date, remaining claims, reserves, records, and custodial duties shall transfer to a separate Treasury liquidation office, and the Administration and Board shall then be abolished. Remaining assets, including the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund, remain subject to preserved claims and the property rights of insured credit unions. The liquidation plan shall return each insured credit union's capitalization deposit under section 202(c) of the Federal Credit Union Act (12 U.S.C. 1782(c)), net of the credit union's lawful share of resolved insurance losses and expenses. The liquidation office has no authority to issue new insurance or credit and terminates when the last preserved claim is resolved.

**(i) Insolvency and financial-contract priority.**—An issuer is subject to the generally applicable entity, insolvency, and receivership law selected or supplied under subsection (f), except when subsection (j), a preserved legacy receivership, or an otherwise valid cross-border insolvency rule applies. Sections 362(b)(6), 362(b)(7), 362(b)(17), 362(b)(27), 546(e), 546(f), 546(g), 546(j), 548(d)(2)(B) through (E), 555 through 561, and 753 of title 11, United States Code, and the special financial-contract exceptions in sections 553(a)(2)(B)(ii), 553(a)(3)(C), and 553(b)(1) of that title, do not apply to a securities contract, commodity contract, forward contract, repurchase agreement, swap agreement, or master netting agreement entered into or materially expanded on or after the monetary transition date. Rights under a pre-transition contract remain subject to the law applicable when the contract was made. This subsection does not impair the narrowly defined payment and settlement finality in subsection (e)(5), make an entity eligible to be a debtor except under subsection (j), or give a monetary claim priority merely because it circulates as money.

**(j) Uniform liquidation option for covered issuers.**—

(1) This subsection applies to an issuer that engages in covered banking activity or is formed or continued as a Federal free-banking association, except while the issuer is in a receivership expressly preserved under subsection (c), (d), or (h), has a nonzero protected insured amount outstanding, or is subject to a runoff agreement under subsection (c)(3) or (h)(3). Chapter 7 is unavailable during that exclusion, and the preserved insurance and receivership authorities govern until the protected exposure reaches zero.

(2) **Bankruptcy amendments and rules.**—

(A) Section 109 of title 11, United States Code, is amended by adding at the end:

> "(i)(1) Notwithstanding subsections (a), (b)(2), (b)(3), and (d), an issuer covered by section 402(j) of the Economic Liberty and Federal Renewal Act of 2026 may be a debtor under chapter 7 solely for an orderly liquidation under that section if the issuer has a place of business or property in the United States and has publicly designated an agent for service of process in the United States before the petition.
>
> (2) An issuer eligible under paragraph (1) may not be a debtor under chapter 11. This subsection does not make an insurance company, railroad, stockbroker, or commodity broker eligible by reason of an activity unrelated to covered banking activity.".

(B) Section 1408 of title 28, United States Code, is amended by adding at the end:

> "(3) For a case concerning an issuer eligible under section 109(i) of title 11, in the district in which the issuer's principal place of business or principal assets in the United States have been located for the longer portion of the 180 days preceding commencement or, if the issuer has neither, in the district containing the issuer's publicly designated agent for service of process.".

(C) A voluntary or involuntary case, trustee appointment, automatic stay, avoidance action, claims process, and judicial review proceed under title 11 as modified by subsection (i). An involuntary petition requires the number and amount of noncontingent claims otherwise required by section 303 of title 11 and may not be filed by a regulator merely because the issuer lacks a charter, reserve ratio, or governmental insurance.

(3) Property held in bailment, trust, custody, or a fully allocated reserve under subsection (b)(3), including property traceable into a commingled pool, is not property of the estate and shall be returned subject to a valid disclosed lien. If a fungible customer pool is deficient, similarly situated beneficial owners share the traceable pool pro rata and may assert ordinary unsecured claims for an unpaid deficiency caused by breach. Other property and claims follow generally applicable secured-creditor priority, section 507 of title 11, consensual subordination disclosed under subsection (b), and pro rata treatment within the same priority. A debt note, deposit debt, or payment claim receives no special priority merely because it functions as money.

(4) In place of a chapter 7 case, a court in the jurisdiction containing the issuer's disclosed principal place of business may conduct a receivership that a United States district court shall recognize and enforce nationwide only if, before insolvency, the issuer publicly elected that regime in the governing contracts and the proceeding provides public notice, a single claims process, a temporary stay subject to prompt judicial review, recognition of customer and security interests, avoidance of actual fraudulent transfers, neutral same-priority treatment, independent administration, and no Federal credit. An election made after a missed redemption, balance-sheet insolvency, or filing or threat of an insolvency proceeding is ineffective against a nonconsenting holder.

(5) This subsection supplies a uniform liquidation forum under the Bankruptcy Clause and does not authorize pre-insolvency capital, reserve, portfolio, or activity regulation, a bridge-bank subsidy, reorganization with Federal funds, or administrative seizure outside a preserved legacy receivership.

**(k) Enumerated powers, no commandeering, and application severability.**—

(1) Congress enacts this section under its powers to coin money and regulate its value, regulate actual commerce with foreign nations and among the several States and the channels and instrumentalities of that commerce, establish uniform laws on bankruptcy, borrow and tax for Federal fiscal operations, govern territories and possessions, regulate commerce with Indian Tribes, punish counterfeiting, and make laws necessary and proper for carrying those powers into execution. The national issuance and circulation of monetary instruments, voluntary clearing, and continuity of Treasury payment operations are the Federal ends to which the means in this section are directed.

(2) This section regulates voluntary economic activity and instruments entering or intended for interstate or foreign circulation; it does not compel a person to bank, borrow, lend, issue or accept an instrument, or enter commerce. Purely intrastate activity that satisfies none of the jurisdictional elements in subsection (a)(3) remains subject to State law. A Federal free-banking association exists as a means of conducting covered banking activity and may invoke Federal preemption only for that activity and activity reasonably incidental to it.

(3) This section supersedes conflicting law within its constitutional scope but does not require a State, territory, possession, Tribal government, or political subdivision to enact or enforce Federal law, operate a registry, provide an officer or forum, guarantee an instrument, or expend funds. A government may choose to accept a private instrument, provide ordinary entity filings, or operate a clearinghouse, but no such action is a condition of the Federal rights created here.

(4) If a court holds an application of this section beyond Congress's enumerated powers, the holding shall be limited to that application. The interstate and foreign circulation right, Federal and territorial applications, clearing and payment provisions, bankruptcy forum, and applications supported by a separate enumerated power remain in force. If the optional Federal entity form is held invalid, the substantive rights of a natural person or non-Federal entity remain in force, and an institution automatically continued under subsection (c)(2) or (h)(1) continues as the same juridical person under the internal-affairs law designated or supplied by those provisions rather than dissolving.

### SEC. 403. SOUND MONEY PRINCIPLES

**(a) Currency competition and monetary contracts.**—

(1) Within the scope of covered banking activity under section 402(a)(3), a person may create, issue, acquire, hold, quote, price, invoice, account, transfer, exchange, circulate, redeem, or settle in a covered monetary instrument described in section 402(a)(6). The instrument may use any objectively determinable unit of account or redemption formula, may be fully, fractionally, or not asset-backed as accurately disclosed under section 402(b), and may circulate in physical, electronic, custodial, bearer, registered, centralized, decentralized, or other form. No governmental authority may prefer or disfavor an instrument solely because of its issuer, denomination, backing, reserve ratio, ledger, validation method, or technology, except as expressly provided by section 402.

(2) Acceptance is voluntary. A contract may denominate an obligation in, require delivery of, or provide redemption in a specified covered monetary instrument, commodity, foreign currency, digital asset, or objectively determinable unit. A court having jurisdiction shall enforce the agreed unit, redemption promise, valuation method, and medium of performance according to their terms, including specific performance when available under ordinary equitable standards. If agreed performance has become impossible or unlawful and the contract supplies no remedy, damages shall be measured in the agreed unit and converted, only as necessary to enter judgment, by the contract's valuation method and time or, if none is stated, by fair market value when performance was due. A neutral rule of procedure or remedy may apply but may not deny or materially burden enforcement merely because an obligation is not denominated in United States dollars.

(3) United States coins and currency retain their status as legal tender under section 5103 of title 31, United States Code. That status does not make a covered monetary instrument an obligation or security of the United States, require a person or government to accept a private instrument without consent, confer a governmental guarantee, or rewrite an unambiguous contract requiring delivery or redemption in specified property rather than payment of a dollar debt.

**(b) Treasury commodity certificates.**—The Secretary of the Treasury may issue a physical or electronic certificate representing an undivided beneficial property interest in a fixed weight and fineness of gold or silver only if—

(1) the Treasury holds, free of any other pledge, at least the full stated metal weight for every outstanding certificate;

(2) the certificate is redeemable on demand, subject only to a disclosed reasonable processing period and fee;

(3) each reserve bar or coin and its custody location, encumbrance status, assay record, outstanding certificate claim, fee, and independent physical-count and financial-audit report are published at least monthly in a manner that permits reconciliation without disclosing a holder's identity;

(4) the metal is segregated from the general property of the United States, held solely for certificate holders, not available for expenditure, pledge, lease, swap, attachment, or use as a reserve for another obligation, and transferred only to honor a certificate or pay a holder-approved custody expense;

(5) the certificate and each offering document state that the certificate is a property claim on the segregated metal, not United States legal tender, a dollar-redeemable obligation, or evidence that a United States dollar is redeemable in metal; and

(6) issuance, transfer, redemption, and custody fees are uniform, cost-based, and published in advance.

The dollar value of a commodity certificate shall be determined at the market rate when a dollar conversion occurs. Notwithstanding section 5117(b) of title 31, United States Code, a certificate issued under this subsection is governed solely by this subsection and is not a gold certificate subject to the dollar valuation or issuance cap in section 5117(b). Subsections (b) and (c) of section 5118 of title 31 do not prohibit delivery of gold or silver bullion or coin in redemption of a certificate under this subsection or a holder's action to compel delivery of the holder's beneficial property. The United States consents to an action in the United States Court of Federal Claims or a United States district court to compel an accounting, segregation, or redemption required by this subsection and waives sovereign immunity solely for specific relief, actual loss caused by unlawful disposition or withholding of the segregated property, and reasonable litigation costs. Nothing in this subsection changes the dollar amount of an existing obligation, authorizes a certificate in excess of physically allocated reserves, or authorizes payment of an ordinary dollar obligation in gold.

**(c) Neutrality of Federal monetary intervention.**—On and after the monetary transition date, except as expressly authorized by an Act of Congress enacted after the date of enactment of this Act, or as necessary to execute this Act, pay a debt or make a payment when due, or carry out sanctions, anti-money-laundering, or counterterrorism laws, no Federal agency may engage in open-market operations, targeted lending, asset purchases, guarantees, foreign-exchange intervention, or other interventions for the purpose of supporting or suppressing the market value, exchange rate, or circulation of a currency or class of currencies. Passive custody of a foreign reserve under subsection (e) does not authorize a transaction prohibited by this subsection.

**(d) Regulatory and tax neutrality.**—On and after the monetary transition date, no Federal tax, regulation, form, reporting rule, accounting rule, or administrative practice shall, solely because an obligation, asset, payment, or transaction uses a covered monetary instrument rather than United States dollars, impose a higher rate, less favorable base or deduction, more frequent or burdensome report, or more restrictive condition than applies to an economically comparable dollar-denominated obligation, asset, payment, or transaction. A rule may require consistent dollar valuation solely to calculate a generally applicable tax or financial statement and may enforce a generally applicable law preserved by section 402(a)(5), but may not treat denomination, lawful backing, self-custody, issuance of one's own liability, or use of a non-dollar ledger as evidence of tax avoidance or unlawful conduct without additional facts.

**(e) Termination of exchange-stabilization authority.**—

(1) Sections 5301, 5302, and 5304 of title 31, United States Code, are repealed on the monetary transition date. On and after that date, no officer may use the Exchange Stabilization Fund or a successor fund to make a loan, guarantee, currency swap, gold or foreign-exchange intervention, securities purchase, or other credit or market-support transaction. A preexisting reference to one of those sections or to the Fund authorizes only the liquidation and satisfaction of a valid pre-transition obligation under this subsection and does not preserve regulatory or intervention authority.

(2) The Secretary shall place the Exchange Stabilization Fund in liquidation, publish an independently audited inventory of its assets, liabilities, contracts, and contingent commitments, and settle valid pre-repeal obligations according to their terms. Foreign currency and Special Drawing Rights reasonably required to satisfy an existing obligation of the United States to the International Monetary Fund may be held in passive custody but may not be used for discretionary lending or exchange-rate intervention. Net dollar proceeds shall be deposited in the general fund of the Treasury.

(3) The Comptroller General shall audit the liquidation annually until completion. Nothing in this subsection authorizes the United States to disregard a binding international obligation, and the Secretary shall submit proposed legislation for any withdrawal, renegotiation, or appropriation needed to resolve such an obligation without continuing the repealed stabilization authority.

**(f) Interim tax neutrality for currency use.**—If the monetary transition date occurs before the tax conversion date, the following rules apply to a disposition on or after the monetary transition date and before the tax conversion date, notwithstanding section 988(e)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986:

(1) No gain or loss is recognized to an individual solely by reason of a change in dollar exchange value when the individual directly disposes of foreign currency, a covered monetary instrument, or gold or silver to acquire goods or services for the individual's or the individual's family's personal use. There is no dollar cap. This paragraph does not apply to a sale or exchange for United States dollars, investment property, an interest in a business, goods held for sale, or services performed by the individual, or to a disposition in the course of the individual's trade, business, dealing, or trading activity.

(2) Redemption of a covered monetary instrument for the property, currency, or unit that the instrument expressly promises is a nonrecognition exchange. The holder's adjusted basis and holding period carry over to property received, reduced by money and increased by gain recognized under otherwise applicable law. Stated interest, original-issue discount, a yield distribution, and consideration exceeding the promised redemption amount remain subject to otherwise applicable law.

(3) The person accepting property under paragraph (1) determines gross receipts and basis at fair market value when the transaction occurs, using the actual arm's-length exchange rate or, if none is reliably established, contemporaneous market data from a consistently used independent source. A person required to report the transaction may report that value without treating the individual's nonrecognized exchange-rate gain as income.

(4) This subsection expires on the tax conversion date because the Federal taxes to which it relates terminate under section 301; section 302(a)(5) thereafter governs valuation when a covered monetary instrument is used as consideration for a taxable supply.

**(g) Constitutional construction.**—Subsection (a) applies to activity satisfying section 402(a)(3), an obligation in or affecting interstate or foreign commerce, an instrument entering or intended for interstate or foreign circulation, and activity in a territory or possession. It does not regulate an activity wholly confined to one State that satisfies none of those jurisdictional elements. Subsections (b) through (f) implement Congress's powers over coinage and the value of money, Federal borrowing and fiscal operations, taxation, bankruptcy, actual interstate and foreign commerce, territories and possessions, and laws necessary and proper to execute those powers. This section does not require a State or territorial officer to administer Federal law or a State treasury or private person to accept a covered monetary instrument.

## TITLE V—MISCELLANEOUS REFORMS

### SEC. 501. ABOLITION OF FEDERAL SUBSIDIES AND CORPORATE WELFARE

**(a) General rule.**—

(1) On and after the date of enactment, no officer may make a new covered-subsidy commitment, increase the amount or Federal exposure of an existing commitment, or obligate funds for that new or increased commitment under a law enacted before that date. A preexisting authorization has no effect to the extent it authorizes such action.

(2) Except as expressly provided elsewhere in this Act, on and after the date that is 1 year after the date of enactment, no officer may renew, continue, approve, allow, or disburse a covered subsidy except to satisfy an enforceable obligation fixed before the applicable prohibition in paragraph (1). A preexisting preferential tax provision described in paragraph (3) has no effect for a taxable year or period beginning on or after that date. This paragraph does not advance or alter an effective date or tax treatment expressly prescribed by section 201, 203, 205, 301, 403(f), or 602.

(3) The term "covered subsidy" means a grant, below-market loan, loan guarantee, price support, production payment, or other direct or indirect transfer to a private person, foreign government, or international organization, including a transfer routed through a State, political subdivision, nonprofit entity, special-purpose entity, or other pass-through, for which the United States does not receive reasonably equivalent goods, services, or property at a documented fair and reasonable price. The term also includes a tax credit, deduction, exclusion, deferral, or preferential rate available only to a named person or firm, a specified industry or product, a specified technology or energy source, a designated geographic investment, or another expressly favored private activity rather than to materially comparable taxpayers under a generally applicable rule. It does not include a uniformly available rule for measuring receipts, cost, basis, gain, loss, or timing that is reasonably necessary to compute net income or prevent duplicate taxation and does not favor a specified private activity.

(4) This subsection terminates subsidy authority without repealing unrelated authority contained in the same provision or Act. As a rule of construction, a law enacted after this Act is not construed to authorize a covered subsidy unless it expressly identifies the recipient or class, maximum Federal exposure, duration, source of budget authority, and this section, or unless the later law is irreconcilable with this section to that extent. This paragraph does not bind a later Congress.

**(b) Included authorities.**—Covered subsidies include authorities for—

(1) farm subsidies under the Agricultural Adjustment Act and successor provisions;\
(2) energy subsidies under the Energy Policy Act of 2005;\
(3) the Export-Import Bank Act of 1945 (12 U.S.C. 635 et seq.); and\
(4) the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961 (22 U.S.C. 2151 et seq.) and foreign-aid programs administered by the United States Agency for International Development or a successor, except an amount expressly appropriated after the date of enactment for a named treaty obligation or emergency evacuation.

**(c) Exclusions.**—A covered subsidy does not include—

(1) payment under a bona fide procurement contract for goods, services, or property used by the United States at a documented fair and reasonable price, whether awarded through competition or through a properly justified exception authorized under title 10 or title 41, United States Code;

(2) just compensation, restitution, a refund, payment of a judgment, or satisfaction of a valid preexisting obligation;

(3) a protected legacy benefit, transition allotment, or voucher expressly required by this Act; or

(4) temporary disaster relief furnished directly to an individual for loss of life, health, or a primary residence.

**(d) Existing instruments.**—A valid loan, guarantee, grant, or contract executed before the applicable prohibition under subsection (a) remains enforceable according to its terms. No officer may accelerate a loan or terminate a commitment unless the instrument or otherwise applicable law authorizes that action. No discretionary renewal, increase, or new disbursement may be made after that prohibition takes effect except to the extent the United States became legally obligated beforehand.

**(e) Public inventory.**—Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall publish a machine-readable inventory of authorities affected by this section, outstanding commitments, expected recoveries, termination costs, and provisions requiring technical amendments. The inventory is informational, and an omission does not authorize a subsidy barred by subsection (a).

**(f) Domestic-content and carrier preferences.**—A provision of Federal law, regulation, grant, loan, guarantee, procurement policy, or contract condition requiring or preferring a domestic person, vessel, carrier, article, material, service, or labor solely for economic protection has no prospective effect on and after the date that is 1 year after enactment. The termination includes chapter 83 of title 41, United States Code; section 313 of title 23; section 3342 of title 29; section 55305 of title 46; and sections 40118, 5323(j), 50101, and 24405 of title 49. It does not prevent a source or security condition narrowly and demonstrably necessary for national defense, classified systems, sanctions, prevention of fraud or theft, exclusion of forced-labor goods, prevention of death or serious physical injury, or protection of Federal property. A retained condition may not be used for general industrial protection, and this subsection does not impair a valid pre-effective-date procurement contract.

### SEC. 503. PROMOTION OF FREE TRADE

**(a) Zero customs-duty rate.**—Effective on January 1 of the first calendar year beginning at least 2 years after the date of enactment (in this section referred to as the "trade liberalization date"), each rate of duty in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States and each additional duty imposed by proclamation, executive order, or agency action shall be zero. The United States International Trade Commission shall publish conforming modifications to the Schedule not later than 180 days before that date. VAT on an import under section 302 is not a customs duty.

**(b) Quotas and executive tariff authority.**—On and after the trade liberalization date, no officer of the United States may impose, administer, or enforce a tariff-rate quota, quantitative import restriction, retaliatory duty, safeguard duty, antidumping duty, countervailing duty, or other charge or limitation for revenue or economic protection under the Tariff Act of 1930, the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, the Trade Act of 1974, the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, or another law enacted before this Act. Each existing order of that kind ceases to have prospective effect on that date, and an inconsistent delegation in such a law has no effect after that date.

**(c) Limited exclusions.**—Subsection (b) does not prevent—

(1) an embargo, asset blocking, or transaction prohibition directed to a named foreign state, person, terrorist organization, instrumentality, weapon, or contraband item under otherwise applicable national-security, sanctions, or criminal law;

(2) an inspection, exclusion, or transaction prohibition reasonably necessary to administer customs processing, the VAT if then in force, or a generally applicable law against fraud, forced-labor goods, stolen property, or a demonstrated material threat of pest, disease, death, or serious physical injury, if the action is no broader or longer than reasonably necessary to address that ground; or

(3) a restriction expressly enacted by Congress after the date of enactment of this Act.

An action under paragraph (1) or (2) may not be structured as a charge on lawful trade for general revenue or protection. A restriction under paragraph (2) that materially limits otherwise lawful trade, other than routine customs inspection, entry processing, or VAT collection conducted under published generally applicable procedures, shall be imposed by the head of the responsible agency in a written determination published in the Federal Register stating the evidence, scope, and expected duration, subject to lawful protection of classified or personally identifying information, and expires after 180 days unless renewed on current evidence. A person directly affected may, not later than 90 days after publication or renewal, bring an action for expedited declaratory and injunctive review in an appropriate United States district court of whether the restriction satisfies this subsection. The United States waives sovereign immunity solely for that equitable relief.

**(d) International agreements.**—The President shall seek reciprocal elimination of tariffs and quotas and shall submit to Congress any legislation needed to implement an agreement. No agreement concluded by the Executive may change Federal law without the constitutional and statutory approval required for that agreement.

**(e) Transition report.**—Not later than 1 year before the trade liberalization date, the United States Trade Representative, the Secretary of the Treasury, and the United States International Trade Commission shall report to Congress on affected trade agreements, customs systems, bonded goods, duty refunds, trade-remedy orders, pending proceedings, expected revenue effects, and concentrated regional or industry adjustments. The report shall separately identify for each territory and possession the customs authority terminated, receipts forgone, pending refunds and proceedings, first-entry and bonded-movement changes, records custodian, and implementation responsibility.

**(f) Advisory fiscal and adjustment estimate.**—Not later than 1 year before the trade liberalization date, the Director of the Congressional Budget Office, in consultation with the staff of the Joint Committee on Taxation and the United States International Trade Commission, shall publish an advisory estimate for the 10-fiscal-year period beginning with the fiscal year that includes that date. The estimate shall address customs-receipt changes, interaction with a VAT then in force or scheduled to take effect during that period, administrative savings and transition costs, concentrated regional and industry effects, and a separate fiscal-incidence display for each territory and possession. The Office of Management and Budget shall reflect the estimate in the budget-control calculations under section 204, the annual cash-funding report under section 302(c)(1), and the advisory estimate under section 302(p)(1). The absence, timing, or conclusion of an estimate under this subsection does not delay the trade liberalization date or authorize a tariff, quota, or trade remedy otherwise terminated by this section.

**(g) Territorial customs parity.**—Notwithstanding section 301(g)(2), on the trade liberalization date a customs duty, tariff, tariff-rate quota, quantitative import restriction, retaliatory duty, safeguard duty, antidumping duty, countervailing duty, or comparable charge or limitation imposed under Federal or territorial law solely by reason of entry into, exit from, or movement between a State, territory, possession, or foreign country has no prospective effect. The termination includes the customs-duty authority in section 8(f) of the Revised Organic Act of the Virgin Islands (48 U.S.C. 1574(f)), section 603(b) of the Covenant to Establish a Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands in Political Union With the United States, and a materially equivalent territorial provision. It does not terminate the uniform Federal VAT, an independently enacted sales or consumption tax applied on the same terms to domestic and imported supplies, a cost-based processing fee, or a measure permitted by subsection (c). A territorial customs officer may continue a ministerial entry, inspection, bonded-movement, and collection function necessary to enforce subsection (c) or the Federal VAT, but no territory or possession may opt out of this subsection.

**(h) Entries, bonded merchandise, and pending matters.**—

(1) Notwithstanding section 315 of the Tariff Act of 1930 (19 U.S.C. 1315) or another rate-date rule, merchandise for which an entry for consumption or withdrawal from warehouse for consumption is accepted before the trade liberalization date remains subject to the customs-duty and trade-remedy rates lawfully applicable to that entry or withdrawal. Merchandise for which such an entry or withdrawal is accepted on or after that date is subject to the zero rate and the terminations in this section, regardless of an earlier date of importation, transportation entry, admission to a foreign-trade zone, or placement in a bonded warehouse. A valid bond, lien, custody duty, or charge for storage, handling, or a cost-based customs service is not extinguished merely because the duty rate becomes zero.

(2) A liquidation, reliquidation, reconciliation, protest, drawback claim, refund claim, court action, or collection concerning an entry or withdrawal before the trade liberalization date may continue to final disposition under the law applicable to that entry or withdrawal. A duty lawfully assessed or paid under that law is not refundable merely because this section later establishes a zero rate, and an importer retains every refund, drawback, interest, protest, and review right otherwise applicable to the pre-effective-date matter. An amount previously deposited shall be credited against the same liability and may not be collected or refunded twice.

(3) An antidumping, countervailing-duty, safeguard, quota, or comparable proceeding pending on the trade liberalization date may continue only to determine or review a liability, deposit, bond, refund, or other claim attributable to merchandise entered or withdrawn for consumption before that date. Prospective relief and an investigation concerning only later entries terminate on that date. A cash deposit or security for a preserved entry shall be liquidated, released, collected, or refunded with applicable interest under the law governing that entry; termination of the prospective order does not by itself predetermine the amount.

(4) U.S. Customs and Border Protection, the Department of Commerce, the United States International Trade Commission, and another incumbent Federal custodian retain only the authority, personnel, records, systems, and funds reasonably necessary to administer paragraphs (1) through (3), subsection (c), and the VAT on imports. Amounts otherwise lawfully available for a customs refund or drawback remain available for a preserved claim. The custodian shall maintain a segregated docket and annual public accounting and, after the last preserved matter is resolved, transfer records to a lawful custodian and return or rescind an unobligated balance as otherwise required by law. This paragraph does not continue a prospective tariff, quota, trade remedy, or economic-protection authority terminated by this section.

(5) Paragraphs (1) through (4) apply to a Federal or territorial customs system terminated by subsection (g), but do not require a territorial officer to administer Federal law. An incumbent territorial administrator may complete a pre-effective-date matter under territorial law, and a voluntary closing agreement with the Secretary may allocate records, services, and the responsibility for a refund or net collection according to the government that retained or was entitled to retain the corresponding receipt. The agreement may not continue a terminated duty or shift a territorial liability to the United States without an express appropriation.

### SEC. 504. ENCOURAGEMENT OF INTERSTATE COMPACTS FOR MULTI-STATE DISASTERS

**(a) Finding.**—Congress finds that voluntary interstate compacts, including the Emergency Management Assistance Compact, permit States to coordinate mutual aid while preserving State control and political accountability.

**(b) Advance consent.**—Subject to subsection (c), Congress gives consent under article I, section 10, clause 3 of the Constitution and, for a territory or possession, article IV, section 3, clause 2, to an agreement among two or more States that is limited to mutual aid, licensing reciprocity during an emergency, reimbursement, resource sharing, training, communications, or infrastructure for disaster preparedness, response, and recovery.

**(c) Limits.**—Consent under subsection (b) does not authorize a compact to—

(1) enlarge Federal power, bind a State that has not enacted the compact, regulate a person outside participating States, impair a private contract, or waive an individual's constitutional right;

(2) exercise military, customs, foreign-affairs, or criminal jurisdiction not otherwise possessed by a participating State; or

(3) conflict with a compact to which Congress has given specific consent by another Act.

**(d) Temporary matching grants.**—For each of the first 5 fiscal years beginning after the date of enactment, the Secretary of the Treasury may make a grant matching not more than 50 percent of a participating State's investment in interoperable communications, shared equipment, training, or infrastructure under a compact described in subsection (b), but only to the extent the funded activity directly protects a channel or instrumentality of interstate or foreign commerce, Federal property, or a function of the common defense. A grant may not fund a State's general police, fire, health, land-use, or emergency-management operations. Grants are subject only to appropriation, financial audit, anti-fraud rules, and the express limits in this section. There is authorized to be appropriated $500,000,000 for each such fiscal year.

**(e) Federal relief as last resort.**—Except for a Federal function relating to national defense or Federal property, disaster assistance under Federal law shall supplement, and not displace, reasonably available State, local, private, and compact resources. An agency denying assistance under this subsection shall issue a written determination identifying the resources reasonably available and the legal standard applied.

## TITLE VI—GENERAL PROVISIONS

### SEC. 601. EFFECTIVE DATES AND TRANSITION ADMINISTRATION

**(a) General rule.**—Except as otherwise provided in this Act, this Act and the amendments made by this Act take effect on the date of enactment.

**(b) Publication of dates.**—Not later than 30 days after the date of enactment, and not later than 15 days after any authorized adjustment, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall publish or republish a table of each effective date, transition date, reporting deadline, and election period in this Act. The table is informational and does not alter a statutory date.

**(c) Administrative firewalls.**—Each office, fund, custodial function, liquidation function, payment-system function, and tax-administration function established in or transferred to the Department of the Treasury by this Act shall maintain separate accounts, role-based access controls, audit logs, and records limited to its statutory purpose. A record obtained for one such function may not be used or disclosed for another merely because both are housed in the Department, except as expressly authorized by an Act of Congress, required by a court order, consented to by the affected person, or necessary to investigate fraud against the same function. This subsection does not narrow section 6103 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 or an otherwise applicable criminal, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, or counterterrorism disclosure rule. The Inspector General of the Department of the Treasury shall audit compliance annually and report material violations to Congress.

**(d) Appointment and supervision of adjudicating officers.**—A final administrative determination under this Act that fixes a private person's right, payment, ownership, priority, penalty, or eligibility for Federal funds shall be made either by the head of the responsible executive department, by an inferior officer appointed by that head under article II, section 2, clause 2 of the Constitution, or by the Merit Systems Protection Board with respect to a preserved governmental-employment function assigned to it under section 108(c). Each inferior officer appointed under this subsection is subject to the direction and supervision of the appointing head. A party may petition the appointing head for merits review not later than 60 days after notice of the officer's determination, and the head may initiate review on the head's own motion within the same period. The head may affirm, modify, or vacate the determination and shall issue a reasoned decision not later than 180 days after the original notice; if no decision issues by then, the officer's determination is deemed affirmed. A determination by such an inferior officer becomes final agency action when the 60-day period expires without review, when the head issues a decision, or when affirmance is deemed under the preceding sentence. Nothing in this subsection permits an inferior officer to issue an unreviewable final decision on behalf of the United States. An employee who is not such an officer may investigate, calculate, or recommend a disposition but may not issue the final determination.

### SEC. 602. SEVERABILITY

**(a) General rule.**—Except as provided in subsections (b) and (d), if a provision of this Act or its application to a person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of this Act and its application to other persons and circumstances are not affected.

**(b) Paired tax provisions.**—

(1) Subsections (a) and (b) and the Federal termination in subsection (g)(1) of section 301 are not severable from the general imposition and collection of the VAT under sections 302 and 303. The legacy administration and reconciliation in section 301(g)(3) and (4) remain in force to the extent necessary to administer whichever tax system applies under this subsection.

(2) If an effective judicial order that is not stayed, whether preliminary or final, generally prevents collection of the VAT, the Secretary of the Treasury shall publish notice of the order within 5 days; if the Secretary does not do so, notice is deemed published on the fifth day after the order takes effect. If the order remains in effect, the repeals and terminations under section 301(a), (b), and (g)(1) are suspended prospectively beginning on the date that is 90 days after actual or deemed publication, and pre-conversion tax, mirror, coordination, and cover-over law (in this subsection collectively referred to as "pre-conversion tax law") applies only to an event, transaction, payment, use, or period occurring on or after that restart date. No tax liability may be imposed retroactively for the interval during which section 301 barred the tax. If the order ceases to have effect before the restart date, the suspension does not occur.

(3) If an effective judicial order generally prevents the repeals or terminations under section 301(a), (b), or (g)(1) while otherwise leaving the VAT in force, the Secretary shall publish notice within 5 days, with deemed publication on the fifth day if the Secretary does not act. Pre-conversion tax, mirror, coordination, and cover-over law applies prospectively beginning 90 days after actual or deemed publication and only to an event, transaction, payment, use, or period on or after that date; no liability under that law may be imposed retroactively. Collection of VAT is suspended generally on the same date, and VAT collected for an event or period on or after that date shall be credited or refunded. A credit or refund under this paragraph is reduced by every amount previously credited or refunded for the same VAT and may not be claimed again under section 302(f), section 303(h), or another provision.

(4) If an order described in paragraph (2) or (3) ceases to have effect while pre-conversion tax law is in force, the Secretary shall publish notice of the cessation within 5 days, with deemed publication on the fifth day if the Secretary does not act. Sections 301 through 303 resume on January 1 of the first calendar year beginning at least 180 days after actual or deemed publication, unless an Act of Congress provides a different prospective transition. If pre-conversion tax law has been in force continuously for at least 1 year under this subsection, the Secretary, the Comptroller General, and the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration shall update the cash-funding, operational, and advisory reports under sections 302(c), 302(m), and 302(p)(1) not later than 120 days before resumption and the Secretary shall publish the rate notice not later than 90 days before resumption. Solely for those reports and tests, the resumption date is treated as a candidate tax conversion date; no report or test under section 302(m) may alter the resumption date or reopen the historical conversion determination. A missing or adverse report does not postpone the statutory resumption date or require an additional Act of Congress; paragraph (10) supplies the rate fallback. Pre-conversion tax law continues until the applicable resumption date, and no transaction is subject to both systems.

(5) If an order affects only a category of transactions or persons, prior tax law may apply only as expressly directed by the court or by a subsequent Act of Congress; no executive officer may select a replacement tax or expand the affected category.

(6) During a period in which pre-conversion tax law restarts under this subsection, the Value-Added Tax Administration and its Commissioner are deemed to be the Internal Revenue Service and Commissioner of Internal Revenue for administration of that law, and the procedural, staffing, records, refund, judicial-review, territorial coordination, and cover-over authorities necessary to administer it also restart. For a tax imposed under subtitle A of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, the first taxable year begins on the restart date and ends on the earlier of the taxpayer's otherwise applicable year-end or the day before the resumption date under paragraph (4). Section 443 of that Code and the closing-of-books rules in section 301(c) apply without a consent requirement. At resumption, section 301(c)(2) through (4) applies with the resumption date substituted for the tax conversion date. The Secretary shall maintain a contingency plan and tested archival access sufficient for that administration and may prescribe only prospective split-period and intergovernmental reconciliation rules necessary to prevent omission or double counting, after consultation with each affected territory that chooses to participate. A rule under this paragraph may not impose retroactive liability, require a territorial officer to administer Federal law, or alter a restart or resumption date.

(7) If a payroll or excise tax under subtitle C or D of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 restarts under this subsection, each benefit-accrual, coverage, trust-fund, Federal-State financing, and program provision directly financed by that tax under the law in effect immediately before the tax conversion date also restarts prospectively on the same date to the extent necessary to prevent collection without the corresponding statutory treatment. Sections 201(a)(2) and 205(b)(3), and an inconsistent disposition under section 301(f), are suspended to that extent for the restart period. This paragraph creates no retroactive accrual for the 90-day transition and no benefit type or amount greater than the pre-conversion law would have provided.

(8) During a general restart of pre-conversion tax law—

(A) the repeals in sections 201(f)(1), 203(a)(5), 203(a)(6), and 203(a)(11) are suspended prospectively for the restart period;

(B) the interim currency-use rule in section 403(f) resumes;

(C) a tax function formerly performed by the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau is performed by the Value-Added Tax Administration under paragraph (6), without reviving that Bureau or a prospective regulatory requirement terminated by section 105(j); and

(D) notwithstanding the restarted pre-conversion tax law, a transfer of a Treasury transition bond or Federal voucher to an account under section 201 or 205, earnings retained in such an account, and a distribution expressly permitted for retirement or a qualified health-care expense receive the same nonrecognition or exclusion treatment those sections provide before the tax conversion date. Bond principal, a protected legacy benefit, or a voucher may not be taxed twice by reason of the restart. The Secretary may prescribe prospective split-period, basis, and information-reporting rules necessary to carry out this subparagraph but may not enlarge taxable income or delay resumption.

(9) **Propagation of restart and resumption.**—A restart or resumption under this subsection does not change the historical tax conversion date under section 605(7). It does not reopen an election, create a second transition-bond valuation, redetermine a protected legacy cohort, repeat a program closing, or reverse a completed disposition, except to the precise extent paragraph (6), (7), or (8) expressly restarts a tax-administration or corresponding benefit-accrual rule. Notwithstanding the pre-conversion cutoff in section 301(f)(2), a benefit or trust-fund claim lawfully attributable to wages, service, a contribution, exposure, injury, benefit year, or insured event during the restart period is a separately preserved restart claim governed by that paragraph, shall be valued without duplicating value fixed at the tax conversion date, and remains payable after resumption. A calendar year in which the VAT is generally suspended is not a rate year for purposes of the trailing realization factor, reserve target, or rate-year count. The calendar year of resumption is treated as a first rate year solely for notice, collection, and the 0.75 realization factor in section 302(c), without changing any historical cutoff. Except for the express temporary rules in paragraphs (6) through (8), the legal status reached by sections 201, 203, 205, 207, 301(f), 401, 402, and 403 before restart continues.

(10) **Rate fallback after judicial interruption.**—If an effective judicial order invalidates or prevents operation of the annual formula, an input rule, or a rate notice while otherwise leaving the general VAT and coordinated repeals operative, the most recent valid operative rates remain in force prospectively until the first valid ministerial notice takes effect. If no valid prior rate exists, including for a resumption year after a general interruption, the prospective rates are 25 percent standard and 37.5 percent premium. The zero rate for exports remains zero. Section 204(c) applies to a resulting cash shortfall. This paragraph does not override a judicial order that directly prohibits those rates or general VAT collection and does not authorize retroactive liability.

**(c) Protected obligations.**—A repeal of title II or title XVIII of the Social Security Act is subject to the protected legacy obligations in sections 201 and 205. A court shall not construe invalidation of an account or voucher option to extinguish an underlying protected legacy benefit.

**(d) Paired territorial-court provisions.**—For each covered territory with a predecessor court identified in section 705(a)(1)(C), abolition of that predecessor court, repeal or amendment of the law governing that predecessor, and termination of a predecessor judgeship are not severable from the valid establishment and opening of the corresponding successor court with jurisdiction under article III of the Constitution. If an effective judicial order that is not stayed, whether preliminary or final, prevents a successor court from being established or from exercising jurisdiction otherwise conferred on the territorial-court conversion date, the predecessor court and the laws, judgeship, magistrate-judge authority, officers, and venue rules necessary for its operation continue for that territory only until the order ceases to have effect or an Act of Congress cures the defect. If the order concerns the successor court for American Samoa, during that interval the United States District Court for the District of Hawaii has jurisdiction and venue over each Federal cause arising in American Samoa that would otherwise fall within the successor court's jurisdiction, and the United States District Court for the District of Columbia retains the jurisdiction and venue continued by section 605(9) for a claim under this Act. The preceding sentence does not confer jurisdiction over a cause arising solely under American Samoan law. A vacancy, temporary assignment, incomplete permanent facility, or other operational condition for which section 705 provides a continuity mechanism is not a judicial invalidation and does not trigger this subsection. An order concerning one territory does not delay or impair conversion in another.

### SEC. 603. SAVINGS PROVISIONS

**(a) Existing liabilities and proceedings.**—Except for conduct expressly addressed by section 103, a repeal under this Act is subject to section 109 of title 1, United States Code. A right, liability, penalty, forfeiture, tax, refund, contract, or proceeding arising before the effective date remains enforceable under the law applicable when it arose unless this Act expressly provides otherwise.

**(b) Pending agency matters.**—A pending administrative matter based on authority that this Act terminates may continue only to resolve a pre-effective-date liability or preserved obligation. No successor may issue or renew prospective regulatory permission under repealed authority.

**(c) State authority and no commandeering.**—The repeal of Federal law does not itself enact State law. Authority over a matter not vested in the Federal Government remains with the States and the people, but no State is required to legislate, regulate, spend funds, accept property, or administer a Federal program.

**(d) Federalism construction.**—Nothing in this Act ratifies an interpretation of the Commerce Clause or Spending Clause as a general Federal police power. If fairly possible, a provision shall be construed as an exercise of another enumerated power or as limited to interstate or foreign commerce, naturalization, bankruptcy, coinage, Federal property, territories, payment of debts, national defense, or another power expressly vested in the Federal Government.

**(e) Treaties, Tribes, and territories.**—A repeal under this Act does not abrogate a vested right of a federally recognized Indian Tribe under a ratified treaty unless the provision expressly identifies that treaty and clearly states the abrogation. An officer shall identify, rather than conceal, any conflict between this Act and an international obligation and shall follow the notification or withdrawal procedures expressly required by this Act. This Act applies in every territory and possession of the United States unless a provision expressly provides a different rule.

### SEC. 604. CONTINGENT CONSTITUTIONAL SAFEGUARDS

**(a) Jurisdiction and trigger.**—The United States Court of Federal Claims has exclusive original jurisdiction over a claim that section 103 takes a patent, trademark, copyright, plant-variety protection certificate, or other exclusive right repealed or terminated by that section in violation of the Fifth Amendment. A claimant shall file not later than 6 years after the date of enactment of this Act. Compensation is payable only under a final judgment, or a settlement approved by the Attorney General, that identifies the property interest taken and the constitutionally required amount.

**(b) Payment.**—There are permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury such sums as are necessary to pay a final judgment or Attorney-General-approved settlement under subsection (a), including interest, costs, or fees only to the extent constitutionally or statutorily required. Proceeds deposited under sections 101(f) and 207(d) are not earmarked and may not be counted twice as both deficit reduction and compensation funding.

**(c) Valuation.**—The claimant bears the burden of proving title, validity, causation, and value. Valuation shall exclude speculative future rights, duplicative recovery, value attributable to conduct independently unlawful without regard to the exclusivity of the asserted right, and loss avoidable through reasonable mitigation, but only to the extent each exclusion is consistent with the Fifth Amendment and binding precedent governing just compensation.

**(d) No inference.**—This section does not constitute a congressional finding that section 103 effects a taking or that any asserted interest has the value alleged by a claimant.

### SEC. 605. DEFINITIONS

In this Act:

(1) The term "actuarial present value" means the expected amount of a future stream of payments discounted to a stated valuation date under published mortality, morbidity, disability, inflation, and interest-rate assumptions applicable to the program.

(2) The term "date of enactment" means the date on which this Act becomes law.

(3) The term "final judgment" means a judgment for which the time for direct appellate review has expired without an appeal or for which direct appellate review, including review by the Supreme Court of the United States, has been completed.

(4) The term "protected legacy benefit" means an obligation expressly protected under section 201 or 205 and does not include a benefit based on wages, service, disability, death, medical entitlement, or another event occurring after the applicable cutoff in that section, except for coverage, payment, a voucher, or another benefit expressly protected under section 205 for a protected enrollee whose entitlement arose on or before the applicable cutoff, and for contingent survivor or disability value fixed under section 201(b)(4) as of the tax conversion date and payable solely under section 201(c)(7) or 201(e)(3).

(5) The term "qualified custodian" means a person approved by the Secretary of the Treasury to hold retirement or health assets in a fiduciary capacity that is bonded, independently audited, operationally resilient, and legally required to segregate customer assets.

(6) The term "State" means each of the several States, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, the United States Virgin Islands, American Samoa, the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands, and any other territory or possession of the United States.

(7) The term "tax conversion date" means the first candidate tax conversion date that satisfies the fiscal-eligibility and operational-test requirements in section 302(m). If no candidate satisfies those requirements, no tax conversion date occurs, and a provision that depends on that date does not take effect until one occurs. It is an historical cutoff once it occurs and is not changed by a later judicial restart or resumption under section 602(b).

(8) The term "Federal Reserve entity" means the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, the Federal Open Market Committee, a Federal Reserve Bank, and a trust or other entity wholly controlled by one of them solely for holding or liquidating System assets.

(9) Before the territorial-court conversion date defined in section 705(a), for purposes of jurisdiction, review, and venue expressly conferred by this Act, a reference to a "district court," "United States district court," or "appropriate United States district court" includes the District Court of Guam, the District Court for the Northern Mariana Islands, and the District Court of the Virgin Islands. Before that date, the United States District Court for the District of Columbia also has jurisdiction and venue over such a claim arising in American Samoa, without excluding another court that has jurisdiction and venue under otherwise applicable law. On and after that date, each such reference includes the Article III district court established for the territory under section 705. This paragraph does not create jurisdiction beyond the causes of action and review provisions expressly stated in this Act.

(10) The term "candidate tax conversion date" means January 1 of the first calendar year that begins at least 36 months after the date of enactment and each succeeding January 1.

(11) The term "eligibility measurement period" means, for a candidate tax conversion date, the 4 consecutive calendar quarters in the second calendar year preceding the calendar year in which that candidate date occurs. The period is fixed sufficiently before the candidate date to permit reconciliation of actual cash data, review, taxpayer notice, registration, and system testing.

(12) The term "operative cash outlays" means every actual budgetary cash outlay of the United States during an eligibility measurement period and each actual cash payment required by this Act that otherwise would be classified as a financing transaction, determined from the final Monthly Treasury Statement and reconciled certified Governmentwide Treasury Account Symbol Adjusted Trial Balance System data. The term includes interest on obligations of the United States; protected Social Security and Medicare legacy benefits; vouchers; redemption, acceleration, principal, and interest on Treasury transition bonds; transition allotments; defense and veterans' payments; contracts, grants, and legally preserved commitments; agency wind-down and liquidation costs; judgments and compensation; tax and VAT refunds and refund interest; insurance, receivership, government-sponsored-enterprise, and Federal Reserve backstops; court operations and territorial-court conversion; and VAT implementation and administration. It excludes an intragovernmental transaction that produces no cash payment outside the United States, issuance of a noncash obligation, and issuance or redemption of an ordinary Treasury security solely to refinance outstanding public debt within the limit under chapter 31 of title 31, United States Code. Borrowing, an asset sale, liquidation proceeds, and another one-time receipt may not be netted against operative cash outlays.

(13) The term "operative recurring non-VAT receipts" means actual cash receipts during an eligibility measurement period produced by a law that will remain in force after the candidate tax conversion date and reasonably recurs without sale or depletion of a Federal asset. A refund recorded in operative cash outlays is not also netted from receipts; a refund not recorded as an outlay shall instead reduce the corresponding receipt, so that no refund is omitted or counted twice. The term excludes the VAT; a tax under subtitle A, B, C, D, or E of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986; a customs duty scheduled to terminate under section 503; a Federal Reserve remittance that terminates under title IV; borrowing or issuance of an obligation; an intragovernmental transfer; an asset sale or liquidation proceed; a temporary recovery; and another one-time receipt. A receipt may not be included merely because it was collected before conversion if the authority producing it will terminate at conversion.

### SEC. 606. ENFORCEMENT

**(a) Executive compliance.**—The President, acting through the Director of the Office of Management and Budget and the head of each remaining executive department, shall ensure that an executive officer does not exercise an authority terminated by this Act. This subsection establishes an internal executive duty and does not displace judicial review under subsection (d) or the cause of action in subsection (c).

**(b) Actions by Attorney General.**—The Attorney General may bring a civil action against a non-Federal person in an appropriate United States district court to recover Federal funds obtained by fraud or to enforce a duty for which this Act expressly provides enforcement by the Attorney General. This subsection does not authorize litigation between components or officers of the executive branch.

**(c) Private action against unauthorized Federal enforcement.**—A person directly regulated by, and subject to actual or imminent Federal enforcement of, a prospective requirement or authority expressly terminated by this Act may bring a civil action against the responsible Federal officer in the officer's official capacity in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia or the district in which the person resides or has its principal place of business. The court may declare the requirement or authority terminated and enjoin its enforcement. The United States waives sovereign immunity for that relief. A substantially prevailing plaintiff may recover reasonable attorney's fees and costs unless the court finds that the position of the United States was substantially justified or special circumstances make an award unjust. This subsection authorizes no damages and creates no cause of action against a State or private person.

**(d) Judicial review.**—Final agency action under this Act is subject to chapter 7 of title 5, United States Code, except where this Act provides a different review procedure or commits a matter to Congress rather than an agency.

**(e) Remedies and sovereign immunity.**—No provision of this Act waives sovereign immunity or authorizes damages against the United States except to the extent expressly stated. Equitable relief remains available to the extent authorized by this section or otherwise applicable law.

### SEC. 701. JUDICIAL CAPACITY AND CASE MANAGEMENT

**(a) Judicial Conference study and recommendations.**—The Judicial Conference of the United States shall submit each report under this subsection to the Committees on the Judiciary and the Committees on Appropriations of the House of Representatives and the Senate. Not later than 90 days after the date of enactment, the Judicial Conference shall submit an interim assessment of resources needed to address claims arising from the immediate repeal in section 103 and before the repeals scheduled to take effect 180 days after enactment. Not later than 1 year after enactment, the Judicial Conference shall submit a final report assessing the capacity of the courts of the United States, the predecessor territorial courts defined in section 705(a)(1)(C), and the successor Article III courts established by section 705 to adjudicate promptly and fairly civil actions arising out of property rights, contract rights, and harms to persons or property that may increase after Federal regulatory permitting schemes are repealed, including actions alleging nuisance, trespass, fraud, product injury, pollution, or other invasions of property.

**(b) Contents.**—The report shall include—

(1) an assessment of current and projected caseloads in the district courts and courts of appeals relating to such actions, including diversity cases and cases arising under Federal law created or amended by this Act;\
(2) recommendations, if warranted, for additional Article III judgeships, magistrate judge positions, or temporary judicial assignments, including proposed allocations among districts and circuits and the conversion requirements under section 705;\
(3) best-practice recommendations for case management, including specialized tracks for complex property and environmental-harm cases, consistent with the Rules Enabling Act and the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure; and\
(4) an estimate of necessary appropriations for judges, magistrate judges, clerks, court security, experts, interpreters, and information systems.

**(c) Interim judicial-capacity funding.**—

(1) Not later than 15 days after submitting the interim assessment under subsection (a), the Director of the Administrative Office of the United States Courts shall submit to Congress and the Director of the Office of Management and Budget an interim spending plan for clerks, magistrate-judge support, court security, technical and scientific expertise, interpreters, records, case-management systems, cybersecurity, and other non-judgeship resources necessary to absorb the projected caseload. At the same time, the Director shall transmit to Congress a supplemental appropriations request for any identified need not met by paragraph (2).

(2) Of unobligated administrative balances made available before enactment to an entity abolished under section 101, 105, or 207, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget shall identify $2,000,000,000 in the aggregate or, if qualifying balances are less than that amount, all qualifying balances. A qualifying balance may not be a trust-fund balance, fee held for refund, customer or custodial property, or an amount needed for a preserved contract, claim, benefit, transition, or liquidation. The identified amount is transferred to the Judiciary, including the predecessor courts defined in section 705(a)(1)(C) and the successor courts established by section 705, and permanently appropriated, without fiscal year limitation, solely for the spending plan under paragraph (1) or the territorial-court implementation authorized by section 705(i). If the qualifying balances are less than $2,000,000,000, an amount equal to the difference is permanently appropriated from the general fund of the Treasury for the same purposes, so that the aggregate startup appropriation under this paragraph is $2,000,000,000. The Director of the Administrative Office shall determine expenditures under the plan; the Office of Management and Budget may verify availability and statutory purpose but may not approve a case-management, staffing, or adjudicative decision.

(3) The Director of the Administrative Office shall report quarterly to the committees identified in subsection (a) on obligations, outlays, positions funded, procurement, caseload, disposition time, and remaining balances. An unobligated balance remaining 5 years after enactment reverts proportionately to the source account or, if that account has closed after lawful satisfaction of its obligations, to the general fund of the Treasury.

**(d) Rulemaking authority preserved.**—Nothing in this section limits authority to prescribe general rules of practice and procedure under chapter 131 of title 28, United States Code, or authorizes a court to create substantive rights or obligations by rule.

**(e) Additional appropriations.**—There are authorized to be appropriated such sums as are necessary for non-judgeship resources identified under subsection (b)(4) that may lawfully be provided under existing authority. The $2,000,000,000 startup appropriation in subsection (c)(2) operates without an additional Act of Congress, is available for section 705(i), and is included in the cash-funding calculations under sections 204, 302(c), and 302(m). An additional Article III judgeship beyond those created by section 705(c) exists only if created by an Act of Congress. Neither an additional appropriation nor an additional judgeship is a condition of a repeal, court conversion, or other transition enacted by this Act.

### SEC. 702. NO FEDERAL PERMIT AS DEFENSE TO PROPERTY CLAIMS

**(a) General rule.**—With respect to conduct occurring, or a physical invasion first occurring or continuing, on or after the date of enactment, and except as expressly provided by an Act of Congress that clearly authorizes a taking of private property for public use and provides for just compensation, compliance with, or the existence of, a Federal permit, license, approval, or regulation shall not—

(1) authorize a trespass, nuisance, or other invasion of private property; or\
(2) bar, preempt, or otherwise limit a claim under applicable State or Federal law for damages, injunctive relief, or another remedy for that invasion.

Evidence of compliance may be admitted to the extent relevant under otherwise applicable law, but compliance does not create an irrebuttable presumption or complete defense.

**(b) Savings.**—Nothing in this section shall be construed to—

(1) waive the sovereign immunity of the United States or any State, except to the extent otherwise provided by law;\
(2) affect a claim for just compensation under the Fifth Amendment or a similar provision of State law; or\
(3) reopen a final judgment entered before the date of enactment or remove a defense that accrued under the law governing conduct completed before that date.

### SEC. 703. PRESERVATION OF STATE TORT AND PROPERTY LAW

**(a) No implied preemption.**—Except as expressly provided in this Act, this Act does not preempt or displace a State-law cause of action in tort, contract, or property, including nuisance, trespass, negligence, strict liability, fraud, breach of warranty, or another claim for harm to a person or property.

**(b) Choice of law.**—In an action based on State law under section 1332 of title 28, United States Code, or section 704, the district court shall apply the substantive law, including choice-of-law rules, that a court of the State in which the district court sits would apply, subject to otherwise applicable Federal rules governing the effect of a transfer of venue.

**(c) No Federal common law created.**—This Act does not create a general Federal common law of tort or property and does not expand a Federal common-law cause of action recognized by binding Supreme Court precedent.

### SEC. 704. LIMITED FEDERAL JURISDICTION FOR INTERSTATE NUISANCE AND TRESPASS

**(a) Jurisdiction.**—In addition to jurisdiction otherwise conferred by law, the district courts have original jurisdiction, without regard to complete diversity, over a civil action in which—

(1) at least one plaintiff is a citizen of a State and at least one defendant is a citizen of a different State, or a State as plaintiff is adverse to a citizen of another State or a citizen or subject of a foreign state;\
(2) the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000, exclusive of interest and costs, or the plaintiff seeks prospective relief from a continuing physical invasion; and\
(3) the complaint alleges nuisance, trespass, or a similar physical invasion of real property in one State caused substantially by conduct occurring in another State.

**(b) No new cause of action.**—This section confers jurisdiction only and does not create a cause of action or alter substantive elements, burdens, remedies, or defenses.

**(c) Removal and procedure.**—An action within subsection (a) may be removed under section 1441 of title 28, United States Code, or an otherwise applicable territorial removal provision. Section 703(b) of this Act and section 1367 of title 28, United States Code, apply. Ordinary rules governing personal jurisdiction, venue, forum non conveniens, and abstention remain in force.

**(d) State sovereign immunity.**—This section does not abrogate or waive the sovereign immunity of a State, alter a State's waiver of immunity, or limit an action for prospective relief against a State officer that is otherwise permitted under the Constitution and laws of the United States.

**(e) Federal jurisdiction and preservation of local courts.**—

(1) Each successor court has the same subject-matter jurisdiction, powers, procedures, and protections as every other district court of the United States, including jurisdiction under titles 11, 18, and 28, United States Code. Except for supplemental jurisdiction, diversity jurisdiction, or another basis enacted by Congress for all district courts of the United States, a successor court has no general original jurisdiction over a cause arising solely under territorial law.

(2) This section neither abolishes a local court nor transfers control of a local judiciary to the Federal Judiciary. Territorial law continues to govern the creation, jurisdiction, selection, tenure, compensation, administration, and procedure of local courts, subject to the Constitution and valid Federal law. No territorial officer is required to administer the successor court, and no action by a territorial legislature or executive is a condition of the establishment, jurisdiction, or operation of that court.

(3) Nothing in this section alters the Treaties of Cession, the Constitution of American Samoa, or substantive law governing communal land, matai titles, Samoan custom, or the internal jurisdiction of the High Court of American Samoa over a cause arising solely under that law. When a case within Federal jurisdiction turns on an unsettled and controlling question of territorial law, the successor court may certify the question to the highest local court under a procedure authorized by local law. In American Samoa, a successor court shall ordinarily certify and stay decision of an unsettled question concerning communal land, a matai title, or Samoan custom, but certification may not divest the Federal court of jurisdiction over a Federal claim or prevent timely provisional relief needed to protect a Federal right.

(4) Federal law, including every repeal and protection in this Act, applies in each covered territory on the same terms provided elsewhere in this Act. Paragraphs (2) and (3) preserve local judicial institutions and substantive territorial law; they do not create a territorial exemption from this Act.

**(h) Cases, personnel, and incumbent judges.**—

(1) On the territorial-court conversion date, every Federal civil, criminal, bankruptcy, and miscellaneous matter pending in a predecessor court, together with its docket, record, bond, order, judgment, and property in custodia legis, transfers by operation of law to the corresponding successor court. In American Samoa, every Federal civil, criminal, bankruptcy, or miscellaneous matter pending immediately before that date in a local trial court under a Federal statute or Federal jurisdictional grant amended by subsection (d)(1)(C) transfers by operation of law to the successor court; a cause arising solely under territorial law remains in the local court. An appeal already pending in a local appellate court from such a Federal matter remains in that local appellate court through final disposition under the law governing the appeal before the conversion date and is subject to any further review available under section 1257 of title 28, United States Code, as amended by subsection (d)(4). A transfer under this paragraph does not abate a prosecution, reopen a final judgment, require refiling, alter a limitations period, or affect the validity of an act completed before transfer. A pending appeal remains governed by subsection (c)(3).

(2) A matter pending in a predecessor court solely under residual territorial jurisdiction transfers to the local trial court that territorial law designates or, absent a designation, to the principal local trial court. A pending appeal from a local court transfers instead to the local appellate court that would receive a newly filed appeal of the same judgment. In a mixed matter, each Federal claim, Federal criminal count, bankruptcy matter, and claim within supplemental jurisdiction transfers to the successor court; a local-only claim or count outside that jurisdiction shall be severed and transferred to the appropriate local court without prejudice. The Federal and local courts may coordinate records and stays to prevent inconsistent adjudication without enlarging either court's jurisdiction. Each transferee court shall accept the record, treat the original filing date as controlling, and permit reasonable amendment or further transfer within its judiciary. Federal funds shall pay the mechanical cost of transferring the record, but this paragraph does not require a territorial government to fund a Federal function.

(3) An employee of a predecessor court, other than a judge, transfers to the corresponding successor court without a break in service and without reduction in base pay, accrued leave, retirement credit, or other vested employment right, subject thereafter to laws governing employees of the courts of the United States. Appropriations, records, equipment, leases, contracts, and administrative property of a predecessor court transfer to the Administrative Office solely for the successor court's operation.

(4) A magistrate judge appointed to a predecessor court and serving immediately before the territorial-court conversion date continues, without reappointment and for the unexpired portion of the magistrate judge's term, as a magistrate judge of the corresponding successor court. The position, compensation, retirement credit, jurisdiction, removal, and eligibility for reappointment are thereafter governed by chapter 43 of title 28, United States Code.

(5) The office of each predecessor-court judge terminates on the territorial-court conversion date. An incumbent does not become an Article III judge without a new appointment under article II, section 2, clause 2 of the Constitution and subsection (c)(1). The United States shall preserve every retirement, survivor-annuity, and other annuity right accrued or accruing to each incumbent or former judge of a predecessor court under sections 373 and 376 of title 28, United States Code, as if abolition of the predecessor court did not forfeit the financial right. A reference in those sections to retention of office, senior status, or recall does not authorize the former judge to exercise judicial power in a successor court after the conversion date. For the unexpired balance of the incumbent's fixed term, the incumbent shall receive transition compensation equal to the judicial salary that otherwise would have been payable, reduced by salary received from another Federal judicial office. Nothing prevents the separately authorized appointment of an incumbent as an Article III judge, bankruptcy judge, magistrate judge, or local judge, and judicial service in the successor court is limited to the authority of that new office.

(6) A United States attorney or United States marshal appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate for a predecessor district and serving immediately before the conversion continues for the remainder of the officer's term as the officer for the successor district, subject to otherwise applicable removal and vacancy law. A marshal serving immediately before the territorial-court conversion date under an appointment by the Attorney General under former section 1614(c) of title 48, United States Code, may continue to perform only the duties of the predecessor court's marshal until that date. On and after that date, the individual may perform the duties of United States marshal for the successor district only after a separate appointment or designation authorized by a statute consistent with article II, section 2, clause 2 of the Constitution, and only for the period permitted by that statute. Continuation under former section 1614(c) alone does not constitute an appointment to the office of United States marshal. The Attorney General and the Director of the United States Marshals Service shall use existing acting and interim appointment authority to staff each vacancy, including in the District of American Samoa, until officers appointed under sections 541 and 561 of title 28 qualify.

**(i) Implementation and funding.**—

(1) Not later than 180 days after the date of enactment, the Director of the Administrative Office of the United States Courts, the Judicial Conference, the Attorney General, the Administrator of General Services, and the judicial councils of the First, Third, and Ninth Circuits shall publish a coordinated implementation plan. The plan shall address nominations and assignments, court and chambers space, clerk and bankruptcy operations, detention and transport, juror source lists, grand juries, interpreters, cybersecurity, records, continuity of operations, Federal defenders and Criminal Justice Act panels, United States attorney and marshal staffing, probation and pretrial services, the transfer of predecessor matters, and Federal matters pending in a local court of American Samoa under subsection (d)(1)(C). The officials shall consult each covered territory's government, local judiciary, bar, and public, but consultation does not create an approval or opt-out power.

(2) The Administrator of General Services shall provide secure permanent or interim space in each covered territory. A permanent courthouse need not be completed before the territorial-court conversion date; leased, shared, or modular Federal space may be used if it provides public access, judicial independence, records security, disability access, and safe criminal proceedings. The Attorney General shall arrange lawful detention, transport, and witness services without making operation of a court contingent on construction of a new detention facility.

(3) The $2,000,000,000 permanently appropriated under section 701(c)(2) is available for the startup and conversion costs in this section, including facilities, information systems, security, interpreters, staffing, record transfer, juror administration, and defense services. There are authorized to be appropriated such additional sums as are necessary for those purposes and for continuing operation of the successor courts. After startup, each successor court shall be included in the Judiciary's ordinary annual budget on the same basis as another district court of the United States. The Director of the Administrative Office shall separately account for expenditures by district and report them quarterly under section 701(c)(3). An additional appropriation is not a condition of establishment, jurisdiction, or conversion, and the minimum constitutional operations protected by section 204(d) remain part of total cash outlays.

(4) Failure to complete a plan, procurement, construction project, nomination, appointment, local consultation, or intergovernmental agreement does not postpone the territorial-court conversion date or diminish a successor court's jurisdiction. The officials named in paragraph (1) shall use temporary assignments, interim appointments, existing Federal space, remote administrative functions, and lawful emergency procurement as necessary to open every successor court on that date, but a party may not be required to conduct a trial or evidentiary hearing by remote means without consent except as otherwise authorized by law.

(5) Not later than 1 year after each decennial census identifies a permanent civilian population in a United States territory or possession not listed in subsection (a)(1)(A), the Judicial Conference shall report to Congress whether an additional Article III district is required to maintain the uniform access established by this section and shall submit proposed statutory text, judgeships, circuit assignment, and cost estimates. This paragraph does not place an uninhabited island within a newly created judicial district or delay the courts expressly established by this section.
